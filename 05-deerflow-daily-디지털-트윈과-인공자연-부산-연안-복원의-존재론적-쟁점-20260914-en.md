# The Ontology of the Busan Coastal Coral Reef Digital Twin: A Representational Model or Environmentalization of Artificial Nature?

## 1. Actual Conditions of the Case — Global Paradigm and Busan's Position

The current AI-digital twin-based coral reef restoration project targeting the Busan coast aligns with the city's ongoing digital twin-based urban infrastructure initiatives (such as flood response) in cooperation with the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute (ETRI) and others, while forming a separate trajectory for marine ecosystem restoration. The most concrete global precedent is the 100-hectare Red Sea coral reef digital twin project promoted by KAUST-KCRI and digiLab in 2025. This project combines real-time sensors, satellite imagery, computer vision-based cluster analysis, and large language models (LLMs) to: ① reduce coral growth measurement time from two months to two weeks, ② determine optimal sensor placement through machine learning, and ③ predict key resilience indicators such as heat tolerance and growth rate.

The core technical conditions that the Busan case shares with this global paradigm are the following three:

1. **Real-time dual input** — Real-time fusion of physical sensors (IoT) and remote sensing (satellite/drone)
2. **Prediction-decision linkage** — Model simulation directly leads to physical intervention in the form of restoration priority allocation
3. **Uncertainty quantification** — Confidence intervals of predictions serve as the basis for decision-making

## 2. The Digital Twin as Representational Model — A Critique of its Sufficiency

The position that the digital twin is "merely a computational model that reproduces nature" has considerable explanatory power on the surface. The DT measures the three-dimensional structure of actual coral reefs using satellite and underwater sensors, replicates it in a virtual space, and simulates growth, temperature, and light levels. This can be seen as an extension of traditional ecological modeling and a digitalization of the classic scientific methodology of 'measurement-modeling-prediction'.

However, this interpretation critically fractures at the following two points.

**First, the DT does not fix a 'representational object'.** As Wagg et al. (2025, *Data-Centric Engineering*, Cambridge) argue, the philosophical foundation of digital twins lies in holism and emergentism. A DT is not a replica of a single object, but a dynamically assembling system of multiple heterogeneous components (sensors, algorithms, visualization interfaces, decision-making frameworks). This assembly process does not presuppose the object (coral reef) as a fixed entity but instead reconstructs the object's boundaries anew within the data flow.

**Second, the DT dismantles the classic distinction between representation and intervention.** While traditional scientific models follow a linear sequence of 'observation-prediction-action', an AI DT forms a feedback loop where predictions are directly transformed into intervention conditions. The sensor placement optimization algorithm developed by digiLab does not simply reproduce the current state; it decides what data should be collected (and what data does not need to be collected). This is a reversal phenomenon where the virtual model constitutes the **perceptual conditions themselves** of physical reality through the physical arrangement of sensors.

## 3. The Mode of Existence of Artificial Nature — The Four-Element System of the DT and the Mechanism of Environmentalization

Here it becomes necessary to view this digital twin as an instance of **Artificial Nature**. According to the four-element system of artificial nature theory — database, algorithm, UI, UX:

- **Database**: Real-time sensor stream + satellite image history + past ecological records (growth rate, mortality, water temperature history). This DB is not a simple repository but a dynamic layer that continuously updates by transforming the state of nature into a 'record'.
- **Algorithm**: Computer vision (coral growth recognition), machine learning (resilience prediction), optimization (restoration priority determination). Algorithms do not extract 'laws of nature' from data but **produce** a mode of operation of nature optimized for specific intervention purposes.
- **UI/UX**: 3D virtual reality visualization, dashboards, decision-support interfaces. At this layer, nature once again becomes a sensorially experienceable object, but the conditions of that experience are entirely determined by the DT's architecture.

The reason this structure is important is that the DT possesses a fundamentally different **ontological status** from existing representational models. Traditional ecological models operate within a threefold relationship of 'nature-model-human', where the model represents nature and humans interpret the model to act. In contrast, in an AI DT:

```
Nature (coral reef) ←→ [Sensor + Data] ←→ [Algorithm + Prediction] ←→ [UI/UX] ←→ Human (restoration decision-maker)
```

In this chain, each term is not merely connected but **constitutes the condition of existence for the others**. Without sensors, the DT does not exist; without the DT, nature does not become a 'visible object' for restoration decisions. This is precisely the core of the phenomenon I have previously termed the 'environmentalization' mechanism.

## 4. Environmentalization and the 'Technical Production of Nature' — Where is the Boundary?

Let us return to the essential question: Where does the boundary lie between 'technical mediation of nature' and 'nature produced by technology'?

**Thesis: This boundary does not exist. The ontological singularity of the DT lies precisely in the dissolution of this boundary.**

The reasons can be analyzed on three levels.

### 4.1 The Perceptual Level — The DT Makes Nature Sensible

The human eye cannot discern fine growth differences in coral communities 20 meters underwater. The DT amplifies these differences through computer vision and makes them visible via the UI. However, what is crucial is that this process is not a simple 'magnification' but a **reconfiguration of perceptual categories**. The DT defines categories such as 'healthy coral/stressed coral/dead coral', classifies data according to these categories, and this classification in turn determines sensor placement and data collection frequency. As Benjamin's technological metaphysics (see local Khoj document 4) points out, technology transforms the perceptual structure itself. The DT is not a window that shows nature as it is, but rather a mirror that reconfigures the very conditions under which nature becomes seeable.

### 4.2 The Temporal Level — When Prediction Replaces Ecological Temporality

Here the deepest tension arises. The temporality of an ecosystem includes nonlinear, heterogeneous, and unpredictable phases. Coral reef recovery is determined by numerous variables such as water temperature fluctuations, nutrient inflow, and local changes in predator-prey relationships, many of which are in principle unpredictable.

The AI model of the DT quantifies this uncertainty and converts it into probabilistic predictions. It is no coincidence that digiLab's core expertise is 'uncertainty quantification' — this is the task of **translating the uncertainty of ecological temporality into calculable risk**. However, a critical problem emerges here.

> When the model's predictions replace the temporality of the actual restoration process, the gap between the 'predicted nature' and the 'living nature' is concealed.

If the AI DT predicts that "restoration priority area A can recover 80% within six months," this prediction may become a self-fulfilling prophecy, or if the prediction is wrong, a self-defeating prophecy. In either case, nature's temporality is no longer an independent variable — the ecosystem operates within a **hybrid temporality** that combines the DT's predictions with physical restoration interventions.

### 4.3 The Existential Level — The Dual Status of an Artificial Nature DT

At this point, we can characterize the DT as a mode of existence of artificial nature. The DT is:

- Both a **technical mediation of nature** and **nature produced by technology**.
- These two statuses are not alternatives but **coexist simultaneously and contradictorily**.
- When the DT mediates nature, that mediation does not transmit nature 'as it is' but transforms nature's mode of existence.
- When the DT produces nature, that production is not creation ex nihilo but forms a new domain of existence where the material processes of nature and the computational processes of the DT are **co-constituted**.

**The reason this boundary does not exist is that the space in which the DT operates is the realm of the 'nature-artifice hybrid' — not because nature has been completely replaced by technology.**

## 5. Three Ontological Singularities Distinguishing Artificial Nature and Digital Twins

The search results from the local Khoj references show connections to digital media aesthetics, Benjamin's technological metaphysics, cyberspace interaction, etc., but they do not sufficiently address the direct intersection of artificial nature and digital twins. To fill this theoretical gap, I propose the following three singularities.

### 5.1 Environmentalization — From Object Representation to Environment Constitution

The DT does not represent the coral reef as an 'object'. What the DT constitutes is the 'environment' within which the coral reef emerges. Inside the digital twin, the coral reef is no longer an independent natural object but exists within a **relational field** where sensor data, algorithm classifications, visualizations, and decision thresholds intersect. This is a direct application of the thesis I developed in 'AI_environment_vs_object_paper' — that AI should be understood as an environment rather than an object.

### 5.2 Simultaneity of Mediation and Production — A 'Third Ontology'

The concept of 'Kunst ohne Werk' (art without a work) mentioned in local Khoj document 1 provides a meaningful parallel. The DT can be described as 'technical nature without nature' — it does not completely replace physical nature, yet it constitutes all the conditions under which nature is perceived and intervened upon. This corresponds to a **third realm** as an ontological category.

### 5.3 Ethical Asymmetry — The Responsibility of Prediction

When the DT constitutes environmentalized nature, the restoration priorities determined within that nature entail a clear **ethical asymmetry**. Whether a particular area is classified as 'high restoration priority' depends on how the DT's algorithms set weights, and those weights non-representationally embed human political and economic judgments. However, this decision is presented in the form of 'objective states of nature.' This is precisely the **power effect inherent in the environmentalization of nature**.

## 6. Conclusion — The Correct Lens for Viewing the DT

The AI-based digital twin of the Busan coastal coral reef is **neither a representational model nor a complete replacement of nature.** It is:

1. A **hybrid existential domain** co-constituted by nature and technology,
2. An **environmentalization mechanism** that reconfigures the perceptual conditions of nature,
3. An **ontological junction** that superimposes the temporality of prediction onto ecological temporality.

When we view this DT as an embodiment of artificial nature, we no longer ask the dichotomous question of 'nature or artificial?' Instead, the question we must ask is this:

> Within the environment constituted by the DT, what kind of nature emerges? And to whom, and in what form, is the power to determine the conditions of that nature's emergence distributed?

This question goes beyond mere technical analysis and **calls for a renewed thinking of ecological reality itself** within the ontological condition of artificial nature. The Busan coastal coral reef DT is the most concrete yet most philosophical experimental ground for this thinking.
