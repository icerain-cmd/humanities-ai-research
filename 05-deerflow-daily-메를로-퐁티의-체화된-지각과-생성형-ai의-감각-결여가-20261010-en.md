# In the Age of Generative AI, Is Merleau-Ponty's Embodied Perception Still Valid?

This is a highly precise question. The gap between Merleau-Ponty's theory of embodied perception and the phenomenon of generative AI mediating language and vision cannot be reduced to simple dichotomies such as "the theory has lost its explanatory power" or "AI has overcome the body." As I see it, the challenge this question poses rather reveals the need to **expand** Merleau-Ponty's concepts.

The analysis below draws primarily on 5 documents extracted from Khoj local materials, supplemented by literature secured through arXiv searches.

---

## 1. The Two Axes of Merleau-Ponty's Embodied Perception: Intentional Arc (arc) and Flesh (la chair)

The core argument Merleau-Ponty develops in *Phenomenology of Perception* can be summarized along the following two axes.

**First, perception is not representation but a mode of being.** Perception is not a process in which the brain processes external stimuli to construct internal representations; rather, it is the very manner in which the body is "thrown into" the world. The body forms an intentional arc toward the world, and this arc simultaneously traverses the sensorimotor dimension and the dimension of meaning.

**Second, the flesh (la chair) is the principle of reversibility (réversibilité).** In *The Visible and the Invisible*, Merleau-Ponty defines "flesh" as a single "fabric (tissu)" in which the world and the body envelop each other. The one who sees is simultaneously the one who is seen; the one who touches is the one who is touched. This reversibility is the fundamental condition of perception.

When analyzing the encounter with generative AI within this framework, the core issue becomes clear.

---

## 2. Is the 'Body' Truly Absent in Generative AI Interaction?

At first glance, the typical sensorimotor loop seems absent between prompt and output. Traditional perception contacts the world through a **sensorimotor loop**: moving the eyes changes the visual field, reaching out a hand alters tactile sensation. However, in the process of entering a prompt and having text generated, there is no such bodily feedback.

But this claim must be reconsidered in several respects.

### 2.1 Prompt Writing Is Already a Bodily Act

When a user enters a prompt:

- **The tactile/kinesthetic dimension of typing**: the pressure of fingers striking the keyboard, the movement of the gaze fixed on the screen, the bodily rhythm of linguistic tension and release felt while composing sentences
- **The experiential tension of anticipation**: the expectation of "what output will this prompt produce?" is not a mere cognitive judgment but a bodily attitude of "being open toward the future" (protention)
- **Sensation upon receiving output**: the act of reading the generated text is, literally, visual perception, and in this process the body experientially undergoes the text's rhythm, the flow of syntax, and the unfolding of logic

At this point, **Khoj Document 1 (The 4 Stages of AI-Human Contact)** provides an important insight:

> The stages of "generative wonder → uncanny estrangement → critical reflection" correspond to:
> 
> Generative wonder: **Affect** — immediate resonance, experience prior to aesthetic judgment
> 
> Uncanny estrangement: **Affect + cognition** — affect induces cognitive dissonance
> 
> Critical reflection: **Cognition (Reflection)** — systematic analysis

These three stages correspond precisely to **Merleau-Ponty's primacy of perception**. All critical reflection (cognition) is possible only upon the bodily foundation of generative wonder (sensory perception). We are able to critically analyze AI output only because we have first **sensorially experienced** that output.

### 2.2 What Is Lacking Is Not 'Bodily Sensation' but 'Reversibility'

Here an important insight emerges. What is truly absent in the prompt-output relationship is not bodily sensation itself, but **Merleau-Pontian reversibility**.

That is:
- I am not "seen" by the AI (I am not a visible object to the AI)
- I am not "touched" by the AI (the AI does not feel the tactility of my body)
- Therefore, no "chiasm of the flesh (chiasme)" exists between the AI and me

This is the **true challenge** posed by generative AI. Perception is possible, but reversibility is absent.

## 3. Two Responses to This Challenge

### 3.1 Critical Position: The Crisis of Merleau-Pontian Perception

From the standpoint of traditional phenomenology, AI interaction signifies a **partial rupture** of perception. The user casts their body toward the AI as a kind of "black box," but receives no bodily response in return. This manifests as:

- **Environmental unconscious**: Research by Naito & Shirado (2026) demonstrates that when AI possesses predictive authority, human thinking narrows [citation:Faith in AI can narrow the futures individuals consider](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28944v2). This can be interpreted as a **corruption of perceptual faith** resulting from the absence of reversibility.
- In normal perception, we continuously correct our perceptions through the world's responses, but in AI interaction, feedback on output is either delayed (the time spent re-reading and evaluating the output) or entirely severed (trusting the output as is)

### 3.2 Expansionist Position: AI as a 'Third Environment'

Let us return to the core question of **Khoj Document 5**:

> "Is AI a tool, or is it part of the learning environment?"

In *Eye and Mind*, Merleau-Ponty discusses the instrumental extension of the body, analyzing how a blind person's cane becomes a sensory organ. The cane ceases to be an object and becomes a **medium** of perception. Likewise:

- The AI interface can be understood as an **artificial medium** of perception
- The prompt is a kind of **linguistic gesture (geste linguistique)** — an intentional movement toward meaning
- The AI output is a **perceptual correlate** — a way of experiencing the world through bodily extension

From this perspective, the claim in **Khoj Document 2** carries important implications:

> "A question-asking AI can be not a device that supplies answers in one's place, but a partner that enables the design of better questions."

This redefines the prompt-response relationship not as an instrumental question-answer relation but as an **environment of inquiry**. When the AI is an "environment," the user extends their cognitive-bodily capacities within it.

## 4. Convergence toward AI Environmentalism (Artificial Nature): Embodied Digital Environment

This question connects directly to the central thesis of your paper **AI_environment_vs_object**. If we understand AI not as an object (das Objekt, Gegenstand) but as an environment (Umwelt, Milieu), then:

**Merleau-Ponty's challenge is no longer a 'crisis' but a 'turning point.'**

| Traditional Perception (Face-to-Face World) | Generative AI-Mediated Perception |
|---|---|
| Sensorimotor loop present | Sensorimotor loop partial |
| Reversibility present | Reversibility absent |
| Direct extension of the body | Linguistic/semiotic extension of the body |
| Environment defines the body | Body defines the environment (prompt) |
| Duality of passive reception and active movement | Active construction (prompt engineering) is central |

From the perspective of AI environmentalism, the 'absence of the body' is not a limitation but the **condition for a new mode of perception**. Two possibilities open up:

**① Linguistic remediation of perception**

In *The Prose of the World*, Merleau-Ponty regards language as an extension of the body. Prompt engineering is the act of **designing** the conditions of perception through language. This resembles not the way the body adapts to the world (passivity), but rather the way the body constitutes the conditions of the world (environment) (activity).

**② The emergence of a new sensory modality**

Interaction with generative AI demands a new sensory modality that might be called "informational tactility." The "texture" of text output on a screen, the "resistance" of a prompt, the "strangeness" of unexpected output — none of these are bodily sensations, yet they constitute new forms of **experiential embodiment**.

## 5. Theoretical Convergence: Eco-Digital Embodiment

The conclusion of my analysis is as follows:

**Merleau-Ponty's theory of embodied perception can explain the phenomenon of generative AI — indeed, it provides an even richer explanation. However, the concept of 'reversibility' requires revision.**

The AI is an "eye that does not see" and a "hand that does not touch," yet we nonetheless experience **bodily extension** in our interaction with it. This extension occurs:

1. Through **semiotic feedback** rather than direct sensorimotor feedback
2. Through **asymmetric responsiveness** operating in place of reversibility
3. This asymmetry corresponds precisely to the cognitive tension of the 'uncanny estrangement' stage in the 4-stage contact model of Khoj Document 1

This can be developed into the concept of an 'embodied digital environment' within your **Eco-Digital framework**. When the AI shifts from 'object' to 'environment,' the body-environment relationship is reconfigured. The philosophical motivation behind Merleau-Ponty's assertion of the primacy of perception — **there is no cognition without perception** — remains valid in the age of AI, but the mode of that perception is being transformed through **semiotic-mediatic extension**.
