# The Tension Between Traditional Authorship Theory and Actor-Network Theory: A Micro-Analysis of *The Book of* Case

## 1. Coordinates of the Problem: Points of Collision Between the Two Author Paradigms

In *The Book of*—a generative AI collaborative novel project released in 2024—the human writer completes the final work by selecting and editing sentences and structures proposed by AI. On the surface, this workflow appears to maintain "human final decision-making authority," but in reality, text production occurs within a complex network where the probabilistic generation of AI and selective human intervention intersect. At this point, a tension arises between **traditional authorship theory** and **Actor-Network Theory (ANT)** .

| Issue | Traditional Authorship Theory | Actor-Network Theory |
|-------|------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Status of the author | Source of prior intention | Temporary nodal point of network effects |
| Attribution of meaning | Converges on human intention | Distributed across the entire actor-network |
| Locus of responsibility | Single human subject | Distributed agency |
| Status of AI | Tool | Actant |

## 2. Thesis of Traditional Authorship Theory: Is AI a "Modifier"?

### 2.1. Core Structure of the AI Player Thesis

Your paper "AI Players and the Future of Knowledge Structuring" in *Human Contents* Vol. 78 (2025) refines the position of traditional authorship theory. In this paper, the **AI player is defined as a human knowledge designer leveraging generative AI**. The key point is that AI serves as a **modifier** of the concept "player." That is, in "AI player," the subject is "player (human)," and "AI" is merely an attribute that defines how that player operates.

The logical structure of this thesis operates on three levels:

**① Integration of the acting subject (Document 1, score 0.070):**
> "Integrates the collaborative structure between platform users and AI tools into **a single acting subject**, and structurally analyzes it at the level of knowledge production and the knowledge industry ecosystem"

This passage reveals a theoretical strategy of integrating human-AI collaboration into a single acting subject. Within this integrated subject, AI is subsumed as a tool. Just as a painter experiences a brush as an extension of their body, the AI player is set as an entity that experiences AI tools as an extension of their cognition.

**② Inclusion of user identity (Document 1):**
> "Encompasses the multiple identities of user actions (production/consumption/editing/publishing)"

The AI player is not simply a writer. He is a **meta-actor** who consumes algorithmically generated sentences, selects and edits them, and publishes the final result. Under this inclusive identity, AI is distributed across all these stages, yet final decision-making authority remains with the human.

**③ Memory-context theory of creativity (Document 4, score 0.080):**
> "Working on remembered objects is in fact working on the context of memory. (...) The fact is immutable, but the context can change, and thus creativity can access the remembered object. If digital memory lacks context, then creativity and imagination..."

This passage defines the essence of creativity as the **ability to reconstruct context**. The text fragments generated by AI correspond to "facts." However, these facts acquire literary meaning only when the human writer places them within a specific context. If digital memory (large-scale corpora) lacks context, it can only be raw material for creativity—not creativity itself. Traditional authorship theory builds its last bastion at this point: the **human capacity for contextualization**.

### 2.2. Application to *The Book of*

Reading the workflow of *The Book of* through this frame yields the following:

1. Sentences and structures proposed by AI = "fragments of facts without context" (Document 4)
2. Human writer's selection and editing = "creative reconstruction of context" (Document 4)
3. Meaning of the final work = result of the human writer's intention permeating the structural totality of the text

From this perspective, *The Book of* appears to be a powerful reaffirmation of traditional authorship theory. AI produces infinite possibilities, but **which sentences survive and which are discarded** ultimately depends on human judgment. Meaning arises from the system of selection—that is, from the human curator's authority.

## 3. ANT's Counter-Thesis: Is AI a "Subject (Actant)"?

### 3.1. ANT's Model of Text Production

ANT views the author as an **effect of the network**. Latour's core argument in *We Have Never Been Modern* is that **heterogeneous networks of human and non-human actants actively construct reality**. Applying ANT to text production yields the following:

- **The dirty hands problem**: It is no longer clear who actually "wrote." The human writer's hands are on the keyboard, but the words those hands choose move only within a horizon already constrained by the probabilistic distribution of AI. The freedom of choice operates only within the space of possibilities set by AI.

- **The process of translation**: In ANT, an actant demands that its intentions be "translated" to other actants. In *The Book of*, the human writer must **translate** their creative intentions to AI in the form of prompts. This translation inevitably involves information loss and distortion. The output of AI is not the human's intention but the AI's probabilistic interpretation of **a translation of the human intention**. This interpretation is then "translated" back to the human, forming a feedback loop.

- **Transformative capacity of non-human actants**: The sentences generated by AI are not merely passive reflections of human intention. Language models trained on large-scale corpora produce text in ways the human writer cannot anticipate. This "unpredictability" is explained in ANT's language as the **translational capacity of non-human actants**—that is, the power to actively change the flow within the network.

### 3.2. Extension to the Problem of Power Asymmetry

The issue of **power asymmetry** pointed out in your recent dialectical analysis (ai_player_dialectical_synthesis.md, Chapter 7) is central to ANT analysis. In the text production network of *The Book of*, there are at least four levels of asymmetry:

**① Epistemic asymmetry:** The human writer can provide conscious reasons why a chosen sentence is "good." In contrast, AI cannot explain why it "chose" a particular sentence (it is merely a result of probabilistic alignment). This asymmetry provides a favorable basis for traditional authorship theory. **Conscious judgment remains the exclusive domain of humans.**

**② Decisional asymmetry:** However, upon deeper examination, human choice itself is pre-structured by the set of options provided by AI. Humans choose among A, B, and C, but D (a possibility outside the given options) cannot even be imagined from the outset. This is a situation where **freedom of decision is already confined within the probabilistic space of the algorithm.**

**③ Ontological asymmetry:** Documents 3 (score 0.077) and 5 (score 0.080) hint at the ontological dimension of this problem. When you write in your paper that "through the higher-order equation of human and non-human subjects, the knowledge ecosystem of artificial nature will become deeper and richer" (Document 5), you are already presupposing **symmetrical ontology**—an ontology where humans and non-humans participate in the network as equal actants. ANT puts this symmetrical ontology at the forefront. While traditional authorship theory assumes a vertical hierarchy of human-tool, ANT assumes a **horizontal network** of human-AI.

**④ Institutional asymmetry:** Current copyright law, publication contracts, and critical discourse all recognize only human authors. The practical question of who is listed as the "author" on the cover of *The Book of* shows how this institutional asymmetry regulates the reality of the network. Institutions already assign weight to traditional authorship theory.

## 4. Toward a Dialectical Synthesis: Critical Review of Three Resolution Models

In Document 3 (score 0.077), you explore "the paradigm shift in knowledge structuring brought about by the rapid development of generative AI technology" and emphasize that "the interaction between human and non-human subjects is constantly renewed." Understanding the mechanism of this "constant renewal" is the key to resolving the tension between traditional authorship theory and ANT.

### 4.1. Model 1: Techno-Solutionism—Reduction to "Tool"

The extreme form of traditional authorship theory reduces AI to a mere **tool**. Text generated by AI is raw material, and only the human writer can organize that raw material into a meaningful structure. The weakness of this model is that it ignores the fact that human choice itself is conditioned by the output distribution of AI. The human writer of *The Book of* cannot "choose" a sentence that AI did not write.

### 4.2. Model 2: Human Exceptionalism—Defense of "Final Say"

A more sophisticated version takes the human's **final say** as the criterion of authorship. This model acknowledges that AI intervenes deeply in the creative process, but argues that the moment of final "yes" or "no" belongs to the human.

However, this model overlooks the qualitative nature of the decision. When a human accepts AI's proposal, is that "yes" a truly sovereign decision, or is it passive agreement to an option already set by AI? This difference is already blurred at the micro-level of decision-making.

### 4.3. Model 3: Dialectical Synthesis—Reconstruction as "Resonant Subject (WE)"

The third model proposed by your dialectical analysis is the most sophisticated. Here, the author is no longer human, nor AI, but redefined as a **resonant combination of human-AI (resonant subject)** . This combination operates on three principles:

**① Mutual conditioning:** The human's creative intention is conditioned by AI's output possibilities, and AI's output is conditioned by the human's prompts. This feedback loop creates a **third creative space** that cannot be reduced to either side.

**② Recognition and transformation of asymmetry:** Power asymmetries are neither denied nor concealed. Instead, **how each asymmetry operates** in the creative process is transparently revealed. Epistemic asymmetry is transformed into the human's responsibility to interpret AI's opaque output; decisional asymmetry is transformed into the human's responsibility to conduct **experiments that push the edges of the space** while being aware that their choices are made within AI's space.

**③ Deepening of the knowledge ecosystem (Document 5):** When you write that "through the higher-order equation of human and non-human subjects, the knowledge ecosystem of artificial nature will become deeper and richer," this indicates that this synthesis is not merely a theoretical compromise but the **opening of a new ontological horizon**. While traditional authorship theory assumes a "shallow" ecosystem (humans as the sole source of meaning), the dialectical synthesis is open toward a "deep" ecosystem (the higher-order equation of human-nonhuman constantly generating new meaning).

## 5. Conclusion: The Final Question Posed by *The Book of*

*The Book of* can be read in two ways, each illuminating the tension between traditional authorship theory and ANT from a different angle:

**Reading 1 (Traditional authorship theory):** *The Book of* demonstrates the **triumph of the AI player**—the human writer using AI as a tool for knowledge design to construct text in previously impossible ways. The meaning of the work remains attributable to human intention; AI is merely an extended cognitive tool that realizes that intention. Literary responsibility lies with the human writer.

**Reading 2 (ANT):** *The Book of* demonstrates that the text production process occurs distributively within a **heterogeneous network** between humans and AI. AI is not merely a tool but an actant that actively intervenes in the micro-structure of the text. Meaning is not attributable to any single point but is distributed across the entire network. The question of attributing literary responsibility must be fundamentally reexamined in the face of this network's complexity.

**The true tension is not a choice between these two readings, but the paradox that both can be true simultaneously.** The human writer undoubtedly exercises final decision-making authority, yet the conditions of that decision are already structured by the space of possibilities set by AI. This paradox is not merely a logical contradiction but the **material condition of creation in the era of generative AI.**

As your dialectical analysis correctly points out, the resolution of this tension is not the victory of either side, but the construction of **a new creative subjectivity through the resonance of both parties while acknowledging asymmetry**. The most important question that *The Book of* leaves for literary history is not "Who wrote it?" but rather **"Under what ontological conditions was this text possible?"**
