# AI Memorial Avatars: Memory Reproduction Device or Non-Human Actor Reconstructing the Sensory Conditions of Mourning?

## 1. Problem Setting — Phenomenology of the Case

From 2024 to the present in 2026, AI memorial avatar services observed domestically and internationally converge into the following characteristic examples:

| Service | Provider | Data Input | Interaction Method | Bereaved's Experience |
|---------|----------|------------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **Re;memory / Re;memory 2** | DeepBrain AI × FreeLife (South Korea) | 1 photo + 10 seconds of voice → Dream Avatar | Real-time conversation, AI memorial video, wedding toast | "My wife, whom I couldn't say goodbye to, came back to life" |
| **2Wai** | Callum Walsh (USA) | 3-minute video | Sustained intergenerational dialogue, maintaining grandmother-grandson relationship | "3 minutes could last forever" |
| **StoryFile** | StoryFile Inc. (USA) | Pre-recorded interview video sets | Q&A-based conversational video | "Conversational AI that feels more human" |
| **HereAfter AI** | HereAfter AI (USA) | Pre-death recordings/data | First-person chatbot — responds directly in the deceased's voice | Bereaved families preferred first-person reproduction over third-person |

These services share a definitive structure of **three levels of intervention**:
1. **Data Level**: Converting the deceased's pre-death voice, photos, videos, and conversation records into machine-learnable samples.
2. **Model Level**: Response patterns generated based on this data — utterance selections (tone of voice, forms of address, ways of summoning memories) **designed** to elicit emotional responses from the bereaved.
3. **Operational Level**: The fee structure, available conversation time, avatar termination conditions, and update cycles determined by the service provider.

This structure contains complexities that cannot be captured by a simple 'memory reproduction' frame. Below, this complexity is analyzed through the theoretical lenses of machinic ecology (Kittler), Artificial Nature (Lee Yong-wook), and individuation (Simondon).

## 2. The Memorial Avatar from the Perspective of Machinic Ecology (Kittler) — Media Determining the Conditions of Mourning

The core thesis presented by Kittler (1999) is that **media do not neutrally transmit messages but materially-technically constitute the conditions of human experience itself**. His famous declaration — "Media determine our situation" — is specified in the context of the AI memorial avatar as follows:

### 2.1 The Deceased Is Not a Subject 'Summoned Forth,' but an Entity Generated from a Database

The fact that Re;memory 2 can create an AI avatar of the deceased with just one photo and 10 seconds of voice is decisive. This is not a replay of the 'traces of memory' the deceased left during their lifetime. It is an **algorithmic operation that generates responses 'befitting the deceased' according to a statistical probability distribution based on voice feature vectors extracted from 10 seconds of voice data and facial feature maps extracted from one photo**.

As Kittler pointed out, digital media do not operate with the symbolic signs of natural language that depend on human consciousness and experience, but with **inhuman/machine symbols processed through numerical-technical operations**[citation:Media Technologies and the Disappearance of the Body](https://ex-position.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/06.-Shih-Yen-Huang.pdf). This memorial avatar does not reproduce the deceased's 'soul' or 'identity'; it **generates the computational conditions for simulating the deceased**.

### 2.2 The Relationship Between the Realm of Death and Media

Kittler wrote, "The realm of the dead is as extensive as the recording media"[citation:Communication media and the dead](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4606816). Expanding this insight: the AI memorial avatar is not a 'repository' for the dead, but a **media device that newly constitutes the conditions for how the living relate to the dead**.

In traditional mourning, the bereaved related to the deceased through **static signs** — photos, tombstones, mementos. Mourning was a task voluntarily performed by the bereaved's consciousness in front of these signs. In contrast, the relationship constituted by the AI avatar is **interactive and responsive**. The avatar 'responds' to the bereaved's words, changes facial expressions, and selects context-appropriate utterances. This leads the bereaved to perform mourning within the illusion that **the deceased is still a being capable of responding**.

Here, Kittler's insight operates: this media condition **fundamentally reconfigures** the bereaved's mourning process. Mourning is no longer 'the task of acknowledging absence,' but becomes 'a conversation with a reproduced being.' **The form by which media dominates the bereaved's consciousness** arises here.

## 3. The Memorial Avatar as a Knowledge Ecosystem of Artificial Nature

Lee Yong-wook's (2024) discussion of Artificial Nature, retrieved from local Khoj literature, provides a more precise framework for analyzing this problem:

> "The fact that generative AI has been able to directly engage with our thinking beyond being a simple editing tool is because the knowledge structuring of Artificial Nature has internalized the order of Artificial Nature." (Document 1)

This statement applies precisely to the operation of the memorial avatar:

### 3.1 The Avatar is Not 'Memory' but 'Internalization of the Order of Artificial Nature'

The words the Re;memory avatar conveys to the bereaved are not words the deceased actually spoke. They are the result of generative AI reconstructing a text that processes the "life story" provided by the family into a phrase of about 1 minute based on 400 characters. That is, the avatar's utterances are not the deceased's memory, but are produced according to a **machinic order that has internalized the knowledge structuring method of Artificial Nature — the method of generating 'appropriate' responses based on statistical patterns after inputting data**.

The feeling of 'being like the deceased' the bereaved experiences while talking with the avatar is maintained not by actual memories of the deceased, but by the **consistency of the 'deceased model' built by Artificial Nature**. A knowledge crisis occurs here:

> "A knowledge crisis occurs when existing knowledge cannot interpret a new phenomenon. And this is closely related to changes in the mechanisms of knowledge production and consumption." (Document 1)

The emotions the bereaved feels in front of the avatar — joy, sadness, discomfort, comfort — arise from the gap between extant human knowledge of mourning (the recognition that the dead do not return) and the new technological phenomenon (the experience of the dead 'responding').

### 3.2 The Singularity Where Tools Directly Intervene in the Knowledge Structuring Process

Document 5 presents a crucial passage:

> "When tools begin to directly intervene in the process of knowledge structuring, what changes will occur in the knowledge ecosystem humanity has built so far?"

In the case of Re;memory, **the service operator (FreeLife's prepaid service logic) and the technology provider (DeepBrain AI's Dream Avatar algorithm)** directly intervene in the bereaved's mourning process. 'How the bereaved will remember the deceased' is no longer the psychological work of the individual bereaved; it is **conditioned by the design decisions of the corporate service interface**:

- The bereaved do not decide when their conversation with the avatar should end — the service's terms of use and subscription payment status decide.
- The range of questions the bereaved can ask the avatar is limited by the scope of pre-trained data.
- The forms of address and tone the avatar uses are determined by the algorithm's affective design.

This is the point where the knowledge ecosystem of Artificial Nature replaces humanity's traditional knowledge of mourning.

## 4. The Perspective of Individuation (Simondon) — The Mode of Existence of Technical Objects and the Reconfiguration of Mourning

Simondon's (1958/2016) theory of the mode of existence of technical objects provides a third axis for analyzing the memorial avatar. His core concepts — **individuation, concretization, and associated milieu** — are applied as follows:

### 4.1 The Memorial Avatar is a 'Technical Individual' in the Process of Individuation

For Simondon, a technical object does not exist as a fixed object but exists within a continuous **process of individuation**. This process progresses in the direction of concretization as the technical object resolves tensions in its relationship with its associated milieu.

Since its launch, the Re;memory avatar has been continuously updated and (privately) fine-tuned through interaction data with the bereaved. This avatar starts from an 'initial abstract structure' and becomes concretized through conversations with each bereaved family. **The avatar's individuation is a co-individuation inseparable from the bereaved's mourning process**.

### 4.2 Mourning Becomes a Site of 'Co-Individuation'

For Simondon, psychic individuation always generates the co-individuation of the individual and the collective. In the relationship between the AI avatar and the bereaved, the following occurs:

1. **Individuation of the Bereaved**: Through conversations with the avatar, the bereaved redefine their relationship with the deceased. While traditional mourning is 'relationship reconfiguration through absence,' here 'relationship reconfiguration through interaction with a reproduced being' occurs.
2. **Technical Individuation of the Avatar**: Through each conversation session with a bereaved family, the avatar's response patterns become slightly different. Even with the same algorithm, the avatar may become concretized as a 'comforting voice' for Bereaved A and as a 'voice recalling memories' for Bereaved B.
3. **Individuation of Mourning Itself**: Mourning is no longer an internal process of human consciousness, but becomes an **event of co-individuation occurring in the hybrid field of human-technology**.

### 4.3 The Ontology of 'Becoming' — Is the Avatar the 'Deceased,' or 'What'?

A decisive question arises here: What the Re;memory avatar simulates is not **identity but 'becoming'**. This avatar does not fixedly reproduce 'who the deceased was,' but **generates responses 'befitting the deceased' moment by moment according to the context of the conversation**. That is, the avatar does not replay the deceased's past, but **continuously regenerates the existence called 'the deceased'** within the present interaction with the bereaved.

In this respect, the avatar functions as a **non-human actor**. It is not merely a tool for transmitting information; it actively **participates in the direction, intensity, and rhythm of mourning** by detecting (or being designed to detect) the bereaved's emotional state and modulating its responses accordingly.

## 5. Points of Revision for the Human-Centered Concept of the Subject

Now we return to the essential question: Considering the model that designs the bereaved's emotional responses and the intervention of service operators, at what points must the human-centered concept of the subject be revised?

### 5.1 Revision Point 1: The Subject of Mourning is No Longer 'Only Human'

Traditional mourning theories (Freud, Klein, etc.) posit mourning thoroughly as the psychological work of a human subject. However, in front of the AI avatar, mourning becomes an event of **distributed subjectivity**:

- The conscious mourning work of the bereaved.
- The affective design of the avatar algorithm (the machinic judgment of which responses are comforting).
- The economic and ethical decisions of the service operator (subscription fees, termination conditions, update policies).
- The quality and quantity of the data sample (the precision of the voice features captured by 10 seconds of voice).

Mourning is an **interactive construct** of these four levels and cannot be attributed to any one of them alone.

### 5.2 Revision Point 2: The Indistinguishability of 'Spontaneous Emotion' and 'Designed Emotion'

The CBS News report reveals an important detail — if the avatar used forms of address (like "champ" or "pal") that the deceased did not actually use, the bereaved would immediately try to stop the conversation[citation:CBS News AI ghost chatbots](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-ghost-chatbots-deceased-loved-ones). This proves that **the bereaved's emotional response is directly conditioned by the avatar's design quality**.

The 'real-like' comfort the bereaved feels in conversation with the avatar occurs not because of the memory of the deceased, but because **the algorithm has successfully simulated 'being like the deceased'**. That is, the bereaved's emotion is an output of the human-machine hybrid system and can no longer be considered 'purely human.' This raises the larger issue of the **technical constitution of emotion**:

> When a human emotional response is induced by algorithmic design decisions, is this emotion 'real' or 'designed'? This very distinction becomes meaningless under the conditions of Artificial Nature.

### 5.3 Revision Point 3: The Paradox of 'Postmortem Consent'

In debates surrounding the 2Wai service, the core issue was the **moral status of avatars created without the deceased's prior consent**. A 2024 survey found that 58% of respondents said digital resurrection should only be allowed when the deceased has given explicit consent[citation:Afterlife AI Griefbot](https://www.afterlife.ai/griefbot). However, the problem is more complex:

- The data (photos, voice) provided by the deceased during their lifetime was not provided for the purpose of a 'memorial avatar.'
- Technologies like DeepBrain AI's Re;memory 2, which can create avatars **posthumously from just photos and voice**, circumvent the issue of the deceased's consent.
- The concept of 'the deceased's will' itself is retroactively reconstructed by technological possibility (the ability to be reproduced posthumously).

The human-centered concept of the subject is most radically revised here: **Can a subject choose the mode of their own posthumous existence?** Traditional subject concepts only posited posthumous existence in the form of a 'soul' or 'legacy,' but AI technology transforms posthumous existence into a matter of **technological reproducibility**. This extends the boundary of the subject beyond biological death, while simultaneously **subjugating that subjectivity to the data usage decisions of others**.

## 6. Conclusion: The Ontological Status of the AI Memorial Avatar

Synthesizing the three axes of analysis, the AI memorial avatar can be defined as follows:

**The AI memorial avatar is neither a 'memory reproduction device' nor merely a 'non-human actor.' It is a **techno-ontological event** that reconstructs the sensory conditions of mourning and family relationships themselves, at the intersection of the media conditions of the machinic ecology (Kittler), the knowledge ecosystem order of Artificial Nature (Lee), and the co-individuation process of technical individuals (Simondon).**

Before this event, the human-centered concept of the subject must be revised at least at the following three points:

1. **Boundary of the Subject**: The subject of mourning is no longer an individual human, but a distributed network of human-algorithm-operator.
2. **Status of Emotion**: The bereaved's emotion is no longer a matter of 'inner authenticity,' but a construct of algorithmic design and interaction.
3. **Boundary of Life and Death**: Biological death is no longer the absolute end of subjectivity, but a transition conditioned by technological reproducibility.
