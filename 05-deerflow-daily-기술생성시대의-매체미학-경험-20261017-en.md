## 2. Merleau-Ponty's Embodied Perception and Generative AI: Scope and Limits of Explanatory Potential

### 2.1 Applicable Concepts

There are three key concepts in Merleau-Ponty's theory of embodied perception that can potentially explain generative AI-mediated phenomena:

**First, the lived body (corps propre) and the perceptual field.** For Merleau-Ponty, perception is not a matter of neural networks or representations, but of the body's motoric, affective, and spatial being-in-the-world (être-au-monde). Generative AI 'produces' language and images, but there is no field in which this output occurs. For AI, 'there' does not exist. When a prompt is input, the AI does not receive that input from any spatial or temporal perspective, and the meaning of the input is constructed from statistical distributions rather than bodily situatedness.

**Second, the intentional arc (arc intentionnel).** Merleau-Ponty argues that all conscious life projects 'the past, the future, the human background, the physical, ideological, and moral situation' through the intentional arc (Dreyfus, The Current Relevance). This arc operates based on bodily habits and accumulated understanding of the world. Generative AI's output also forms a kind of statistical model of the 'world' through the distribution of training data, but this is fundamentally different from Merleau-Pontian intentionality. AI 'responds' to the world, but it is not 'affected' by it. AI's output is free from embodied risk, desire, fatigue, joy—the ways the body relates to the world. This may be why AI output sometimes feels "too perfect to be comfortable" (connecting to the 'uncanny unfamiliarity' stage of Document 1).

**Third, flesh (la chair) and chiasm.** In his later work, Merleau-Ponty explains the reversibility of the sentient and the sensible through the concept of 'flesh.' The dual condition of seeing the world while also being a visible being within it is the condition of perception. Generative AI is not within this structure of reversibility. When a human reads text generated by AI, the human 'senses' the AI's output, but the AI does not 'sense' the human's response. This asymmetry is at the heart of the **phenomenological opacity** of LLMs, as noted by Heersmink et al. (2024).

### 2.2 Limits of Explanation: The Problem of the Absence of Bodily Sensation between Prompt and Output

Merleau-Ponty's theory has the following fundamental limitations in explaining the mediating phenomena of generative AI:

**(1) The impossibility of extending the concept of 'perception.'** For Merleau-Ponty, perception is always a contact with the world **from a particular perspective (perspectival)**. To perceive an object means to perceive it from a specific angle, under specific bodily conditions. Generative AI's output does not occur from any perspective. A prompt is 'input,' not 'perception.' Therefore, claims such as "AI perceives" or "AI output is the result of perceptual experience" are difficult to sustain in Merleau-Ponty's terms.

**(2) The absence of habit (habitude) and body schema (schéma corporel).** For Merleau-Ponty, habit is the way the body adapts to the world, a motor understanding that operates without conscious reflection. The famous example of a typist whose fingers 'know' the keyboard layout demonstrates this. Generative AI is not a being that responds to the world's solicitations through a body schema. AI 'learning' is not the formation of embodied habits, but the statistical extraction of surface correlations.

**(3) The absence of intersubjectivity and co-world (Mitwelt).** What the 'generative wonder' stage in Khoj Document 1 suggests is a paradox: contact with AI can occur without embodied intersubjectivity. Merleau-Ponty viewed understanding of others as based on bodily resonance. Yet generative AI output evokes meaningful experiences in humans without any bodily resonance. This is a phenomenon difficult to explain with Merleau-Ponty's model, and it reveals the uniqueness of AI-mediated experience.

## 3. Challenges and the Need for Theoretical Reconstruction

Merleau-Ponty's concept of embodied perception has **partial explanatory power** for generative AI-mediated phenomena, but it simultaneously faces fundamental challenges.

### 3.1 Major Challenges to Perceptual Theory

**Challenge 1: Meaning generation without sensation.** Merleau-Ponty saw meaning as arising from the structure of perceptual experience. Generative AI produces meaningful text without sensory experience. This appears to be a counterexample to the phenomenological thesis that "the origin of meaning is embodied experience." However, a crucial distinction here is: text generated by AI is meaningful **to humans** because humans receive that text through embodied perception, not because AI has embodied that meaning. In other words, the generation of meaning still depends on human embodied perception.

**Challenge 2: The phenomenological status of the prompt.** What kind of experience does a human user have when inputting a prompt? How does this experience differ from typical perceptual experiences (reading a book, viewing a painting)? The 'black box' interval between prompt and output challenges the temporality of Merleau-Pontian perception. Perception always involves **the experienced time of the perceiving subject**, but the time spent waiting for an AI response ruptures the continuity of this experienced time. This may be the phenomenological condition for the occurrence of the second stage, 'uncanny unfamiliarity,' in Khoj Document 1.

**Challenge 3: The replacement of bodily sensation by 'pseudo-sensation.'** The absence of bodily sensation in interaction with generative AI creates a unique phenomenological state for the user. The experience of sending a message through what feels like a transparent medium represents a new form of **mediation relation** in the technology phenomenology of Don Ihde (1990). Ihde extended Merleau-Ponty to analyze how technology transforms perception, but the phenomenon of a language-only medium completely bypassing perception lies beyond his analytical scope.

### 3.2 Proposals for Theoretical Reconstruction

Based on the above challenges, the following theoretical reconstructions are needed:

**Proposal 1: Elaboration of the concept of 'disembodied meaning.'** The meaning inherent in AI output is not the direct product of embodied experience, but the result of human embodied interpretation being 'projected' onto a statistical text distribution. This calls for extending Merleau-Ponty's concept of the 'intentional arc' to the human-machine interface.

**Proposal 2: Granting generative AI's perception the status of 'pseudo-perception.'** This is an approach that recognizes AI 'perception' not as a lack but as a difference (Medium, AI Meets Philosophy Vol.6). Generative AI's 'perception' has its own unique mode—statistical pattern recognition—which is an independent system incomparable to human perception.

**Proposal 3: Connecting to Lee Yongwook's 'AI-environment' thesis.** Khoj Document 5 ("Is AI a tool or part of the learning environment?") captures the core of this question. If AI is conceptualized not as a 'tool' but as an 'environment,' then Merleau-Ponty's concept of 'milieu propre' (proper environment) can be used to newly theorize the relationship with AI. This is because an environment is a precondition for perception, not an object of perception. This point directly connects to the professor's AI_environment_vs_object paper.

## 4. Comprehensive Evaluation

Merleau-Ponty's concept of embodied perception provides a **useful contrastive foil** for explaining generative AI-mediated phenomena, but its limitations as a **direct explanatory model** are clear. The reasons are:

1. Merleau-Ponty's theory asserts that perception is always embodied, yet generative AI can produce linguistic and visual output without embodiment
2. The absence of bodily sensation between prompt and output violates a basic assumption of traditional perceptual theory (perception = always from someone's bodily perspective)
3. However, this absence does not lead to the negative conclusion that "AI cannot truly perceive," but rather raises the positive task of needing a new theoretical framework to explain AI's unique mode of mediation

**The core issue** is this: if generative AI produces meaningful output through a mode completely different from human embodied perception, then to maintain Merleau-Ponty's thesis, one must either (1) prove that the origin of meaning always lies in embodied perception, (2) expand the concept of embodied perception to include AI's statistical pattern recognition, or (3) fundamentally redefine the relationship between perception and language generation. Whichever path is chosen, the Merleau-Pontian framework shows itself not as a self-contained answer, but as a productive theoretical starting point that generates deeper questions.
