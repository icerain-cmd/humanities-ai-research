## Analysis Results

# Media-Aesthetic Reconstruction of Generative AI Audiovisual Creation and the Concept of the Author
## — Beyond Benjamin and Barthes: Toward the Responsible Subject of the AI Player

---

The production process demonstrated by Google's 2024 generative AI audiovisual experiments (Veo, Lumiere, Dream Screen, etc.) reveals a paradox. Human creators perform highly meta-level operations such as prompt design, selection, and editing, but the formal conditions and semantic horizons of the output are not directly drawn by them. In this gap, Benjamin's concept of the author and Barthes' "death of the author" each remain valid in different ways while simultaneously demanding new explanatory power. Below, I analyze **to what extent** these two theories explain generative AI creation today, and **what they miss**, before proposing a third theoretical path—the responsible subject theory of the AI Player.

---

## 1. Benjamin's Author: Producer of Technical Conditions — What It Explains and What It Misses

### 1.1 What It Explains: The Author of Technical Reproduction, Extended to the Prompt Designer

The core insight Benjamin highlighted in *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* is that technical means of reproduction dismantle the aura of art and transform the public from consumers into **producers**. In "The Author as Producer" (1934), he argues that the author is not merely a creator of content but must be someone who provides **their means of production to other producers**. That is, through the advancement of technical devices, the author is redefined from a traditional "creator" into a **coordinator who transforms the conditions of production relations**.

This perspective captures the role of human creators in Google's generative AI audiovisual experiments with surprising accuracy. The prompt designer is not simply someone who decides "what to draw," but someone who designs the **space of conditions** to which the model can respond. This is structurally isomorphic to Benjamin's author as "innovator of the means of production." In particular, the definition of the AI Player presented in Khoj search result documents 3 and 4—a subject "capable of skillfully operating technology and tools to maximize their creativity, and of designing, producing, and publishing valuable content"—can be read as an extension of Benjamin's theory of the author into the generative AI environment.

### 1.2 What It Misses: The Black Box of Autonomous Generation and the Diffusion of Responsibility

However, Benjamin's framework has a critical limitation. The technology Benjamin assumed (cameras, film, printing presses) was essentially a **tool that mediates human intention**. In contrast, generative AI models go beyond the prompts designed by humans, **autonomously generating forms and content that humans did not predict**, based on the probabilistic distribution of training data. When a video output by Google's Lumiere exhibits "emergent" patterns of movement in a specific subject that go beyond the input prompt, the power to determine that form no longer lies with the prompt designer.

At this point, Benjamin's concept of the author loses explanatory power. The prompt designer **sets the technical conditions**, but the way those conditions operate—the model's weights, the biases in the training data, the platform's rendering pipeline—lies outside direct human control. This can be seen as a realization of the "self-purposiveness of technology" that Benjamin warned about in *One-Way Street*, now manifested in the generative AI environment.

## 2. Barthes' Death of the Author: Deconstruction of the Author — What It Explains and What It Misses

### 2.1 What It Explains: After the Death of the Author, the Corpus Becomes the Author

What Barthes argued in "The Death of the Author" (1967) is that the meaning of a text is not attributable to the author's intention. Language is a social structure that exists prior to the author, and the meaning of a text arises from the encounter between author and reader, as well as from the operation of the linguistic system itself. For Barthes, the author is not the "destination of the text" but merely a temporary passage through which language writes itself.

This claim is realized almost literally in the generative AI environment. The output of a generative AI model is nothing more than a **probabilistic recombination of the training corpus**. The text input by the prompt designer is merely a "scrap of paper" that directs that probabilistic path. The "meaning" of a video generated by Google's Veo is determined not by the prompt designer's intention, but by the statistical structure of the millions of hours of audiovisual data the model has learned.

This radically reinforces Barthes' thesis. In the place of the dead author sits the **corpus**. The training data is a digital embodiment of what Barthes called "already-written language," and generative AI has become an automated machine through which that language writes itself.

### 2.2 What It Misses: The Need for a Functional Responsible Subject and the Problem "After the Author"

However, Barthes' thesis fails to account for the **problem of responsibility** raised by generative AI creation. What Barthes deconstructed was the metaphysical author (a creative subject with will and intention), not the **problem of allocating functional responsibility** itself. In Google's 2024 generative AI experiment, if prompt designer A inputs a prompt for "distortion of human form" and the model generates an image that causes offense, to whom is responsibility assigned?

Barthes' framework cannot answer this question. The declaration "the author is already dead" is not a refusal to allocate responsibility, but a theoretical move to redistribute responsibility into a **distributed network of text-reader-structure**. However, lawyers, courts, publishers, and platforms demand that this distributed network be attributed to **a single point of decision**. Generative AI does not evade this demand but rather amplifies it. Because while the production of meaning is distributed, the attribution of impact still requires concentration.

## 3. Training Data and Platform Interface: The Duality of Pre-Organizing Power

Now we move to the core of the question. Let us examine whether "the fact that the training data of generative models and the platform interface pre-organize the form and meaning of the output" reinforces existing decentering theories or demands a reconstruction of a new responsible subject.

### 3.1 The Logic of Reinforcing Decentering

Training data and platform interface determine the conditions of the output beyond the cognitive scope of the prompt designer. This reinforces the decentering of Benjamin and Barthes as follows:

- **The Hidden Determinacy of Data**: Training data embeds specific cultural, historical, and ideological biases. Even if the prompt designer inputs a "neutral" prompt, the model outputs the political structure of the data. This is how what Barthes called "ideological language" operates in the AI environment.

- **The Pre-Organizing Power of the Platform**: The platform (Google Veo interface, Dream Screen, etc.) pre-sets the prompt input format, resolution, duration, style options, etc. This is what Benjamin called "technical conditions," but they are conditions set not by humans but by the platform's business model.

This dual pre-organizing power makes the decentering of the author concept an **empirical reality**. It is no longer a theoretical declaration; the production process itself structures production without an author.

### 3.2 The Logic of Demanding Reconstruction

However, the same fact also generates the opposite demand. When pre-organizing power is completely impersonal and unpredictable, the **responsibility of the subject who designs, selects, and edits those pre-conditions** becomes more important:

- **Responsible Subject of Data Governance**: Who recognizes and corrects the biases in training data? This responsibility lies not only with the model developer but is also distributed to the prompt designer who utilizes that data. As Khoj document 3 points out, the AI Player is required to have "the ability to meta-understand and control AI operating principles, data learning structures, and potential biases."

- **Responsibility of the Prompt Chain**: Not just simple input of a prompt, but in the process of iterative prompt chain-output evaluation-selection-re-input, humans function as **substantive editors**. In this editing process, humans select specific meanings of the model's output and exclude others. This selection goes beyond what Barthes called the "pleasure of the text" and enters the dimension of **ethical judgment**.

## 4. A Third Path: The AI Player — The Responsible Subject After the Death of the Author

At this point, the concept of the "AI Player" presented in the Khoj references performs a decisive function. The AI Player:

1. **Inherits and extends Benjamin's concept of the author**. It maintains the Benjaminian insight of the author as a producer who designs technical conditions, but acknowledges that those conditions operate within a co-evolution with systems (model, data, platform) that go beyond the human. As Khoj document 1 points out, "the player is a complex coordinator connecting technology and context between algorithms and platforms," and performs "a catalytic role promoting the evolution of the entire system."

2. **Accepts and overcomes Barthes' death of the author**. It acknowledges Barthes' insight that the author's will does not dominate the text, but after that deconstruction, it draws **new contours of responsibility**. This responsibility is not attributed to an individual creator but is distributed within a **co-evolutionary network** of human-model-data-platform. Khoj document 2 explains that in the process where "readers learn AI-summarized information and then reprocess it into card news or video scripts to share," role boundaries are dismantled and a new ecosystem is designed.

3. **Solves the problem of pre-organizing power from a new perspective**. The fact that training data and platform interface pre-organize the output does not mean the author is incapacitated, but rather demands a **redefinition of the conditions of the author's responsibility**. Through prompt design and editing, the AI Player partially recontextualizes the pre-organized conditions, creating a circular structure of responsibility (human choice → model response → human re-selection).

### 4.1 Comparison of Three Responsibility Models

| Category | Benjamin Model | Barthes Model | AI Player Model |
|----------|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Subject** | Producer of technical conditions | Distributed linguistic structure | Coordinator of co-evolutionary network |
| **Responsibility** | Responsibility for setting conditions | Deconstruction without conditions | Responsibility for recognizing conditions and making choices |
| **Limitation** | Cannot explain model autonomy | Cannot allocate responsibility | Depends on practical capability |

The AI Player model complements the respective limitations of Benjamin and Barthes, **accepting the reality of pre-organizing power as a condition rather than evading it**, and then establishing an ethics of responsibility within that condition.

## 5. Conclusion: The Contours of a New Responsible Subject — Not "Author" but "Coordinator"

In summary, what emerges from analyzing Google's generative AI audiovisual experiments together with human creators' prompt design, selection, and editing is the following:

**First**, Benjamin's concept of the author illuminates the role of the prompt designer as a transformer of production conditions, but fails to explain the model's autonomous generation and the inherent biases of data.

**Second**, Barthes' death of the author theoretically captures the pre-organizing power of the corpus with remarkable precision, but evades the need to allocate responsibility for generative AI outputs.

**Third**, the pre-organizing power of training data and platform interface **reinforces decentering while simultaneously demanding the reconstruction of a new responsible subject**. This is not a contradiction but the dialectical condition of generative AI creation.

**Fourth**, the concept of the AI Player provides a third path that responds to this dialectical condition. It accepts the death of the author, recognizes technical conditions, and presents a new concept of the author as a **coordinator who performs meta-cognitive choices and editing responsibilities** within those conditions.

This insight was already anticipated in the professor's 2024 paper in *Human Contents* Vol. 78. The AI Player is the **third logical term** — the responsible subject of the generative AI era — that emerges after the death of the author following technical reproduction. It neither returns to the traditional author nor settles for meaning production without an author; it designs a new geometry of responsibility.
