# The Ontological Status of Firefly Petunia: Extension of Artificial Nature or Sensory Mimicry?

---

## Introduction: Ontological Specificity of the Case

Light Bio's Firefly Petunia is not simply a genetically modified plant. It is a synthetic biological product designed by transplanting the luminescence genes (luciferase production pathway) from the fungus *Neonothopanus nambi* into petunias, enabling the plant to convert energy accumulated through photosynthesis into continuous green luminescence at night. The trajectory of USDA approval in 2024, TIME selection, and full-scale household distribution in 2025 demonstrates that this plant has established itself as a **biotechnological product-experience complex**.

This case is ontologically significant due to the following three characteristics:

1. **Duality of the Medium**: The transgene originates from a **fungus**, not an animal (firefly), and the luminescence mechanism is **integrated** into the plant's internal metabolic pathway, self-sustaining without external substrate supply.
2. **Design of the Sensory Environment**: Consumers are not 'observing' the plant; they are guided to **experience** the light emerging gradually after 5-10 minutes of visual adaptation in darkness.
3. **Control of Product Form**: Distributed only as **living plants**, not seeds. This serves both as intellectual property control over biological replication and as a blockade of 'natural' reproductive possibility.

## 1. Extension of Artificial Nature or Sensory Mimicry?

### 1.1 Analysis through the Four Elements of Artificial Nature

Analyzing Firefly Petunia through the four elements proposed by Lee Yong-wook's concept of Artificial Nature—**Database, Algorithm, UI, UX**—yields the following.

**Database**: The gene sequence of Firefly Petunia is base sequence data extracted from the genome of a bioluminescent fungus. This is a typical case of **decontextualizing** information found in nature into digital code for storage and reuse. However, while the database of traditional Artificial Nature was a digital archive of images and text, here the database is an information infrastructure that **reprograms the metabolic pathway of a living being itself**. This signifies that the concept of the database has **expanded into the realm of biological code**.

**Algorithm**: The luciferase/luciferin biosynthesis pathway of the fungus was transplanted as a **biochemical algorithm** operating within the petunia's cells. What is interesting is that this algorithm is not a mechanical command but a **self-sustaining organic process**. In other words, the algorithm does not require an external computing device to run; it becomes **naturalized** within the plant's cycle of photosynthesis-metabolism-luminescence.

**UI (Interface)**: The interface of Firefly Petunia is dual. By day, it is a normal ornamental plant with white flowers; by night, it transforms into a luminescent body. This **temporal duality** is the core interface design of this plant. Light Bio's marketing provides **usage instructions**—"view after 5-10 minutes of visual adaptation in complete darkness"—thereby defining the very conditions under which the consumer experiences the plant.

**UX (User Experience)**: The consumer experience is not merely a visual event of 'seeing a glowing plant', but a sensory event involving a temporal rhythm of **darkness-waiting-adaptation-manifestation**. This experience evokes the "training of the senses" and "synthesis of perceived parts" mentioned in Khoj document 4. The consumer does not perceive the luminescence instantaneously, but experiences it only after adapting their senses to the environment.

**Evaluation**: The above analysis shows that Firefly Petunia does not merely **satisfy** the four elements of Artificial Nature, but **extends** each element into the biological realm. This goes beyond simple 'sensory mimicry' of nature. If it were mimicry, it would have remained at reproducing 'fake firefly light', but Firefly Petunia reprograms the actual metabolic pathways of a living being to create **a new kind of luminous life form**.

### 1.2 Beyond 'Mimicry': Generative Turn

Khoj document 2 mentions cases of mimicry in nature—the eye-spot wings of the hawkmoth. This is a strategy where nature itself creates 'fake eyes' to deceive predators. However, Firefly Petunia is fundamentally different from this.

- **Mimicry in Nature**: Operates through similarity in form and function to create 'deception'. Mimicry presupposes an ontological hierarchy of original and copy.
- **Firefly Petunia**: Transplanting a fungus's genes into a plant is not 'mimicry' but **functional transfer**. The plant is not 'imitating' a firefly or a mushroom; it acquires a completely new physiological capability. This is **poiesis (generation)**, not mimesis (representation).

It is no coincidence that Ginkgo Bioworks CEO Jason Kelly mentioned "the dream of living on Pandora from Avatar." This dream implies the project of synthetic biology: not to represent nature, but to **extract nature's constitutive principles as data and design new life forms**. This signifies that Artificial Nature has shifted from mere representation to **ontological production**.

> **Tentative Judgment**: Firefly Petunia is not a sensory mimicry of nature, but a case of **ontological transition** where Artificial Nature expands into the biological realm. However, this expansion is not complete and involves specific tensions. These tensions are the core of the next question.

## 2. Ontology of 'Natural' and 'Artificially Generated': Where Should It Be Strained and Revised?

### 2.1 Tension Point 1: Reversal of the Status of 'Livingness'

In traditional ontology, the 'natural' possesses 'livingness' as its most important attribute. A living organism is qualitatively different from a mechanical artifact. However, Firefly Petunia implodes this distinction.

| Distinction | Traditional Ontology | Firefly Petunia |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| Natural Living Being | Self-generating, evolutionary origin, 'naturalness' | Human design of gene sequence |
| Artificial Artifact | Manufactured, purposive, 'artificial' | However, **actually alive**, photosynthesis/metabolism/reproduction |

Firefly Petunia embodies the paradox of a **living artifact**. Livingness no longer guarantees 'natural origin'. This prompts a rethinking of the issue raised in Khoj document 5—the possibility of beings with a "physical basis truly different from humans through digital technology, not the replication of biotechnological wet-life."

**Need for Revision**: The dichotomy of 'natural' vs. 'artificial' must be deconstructed, along with the dichotomy of 'organic' vs. 'mechanical'. Instead, analysis based on **modalities of being**—how something is constituted, how it is maintained, and in what relationships it exists—is required.

### 2.2 Tension Point 2: The Problem of 'Authenticity' of Sensory Experience

Light Bio markets the luminescence of Firefly Petunia as "witness the vitality of nature." This creates an interesting paradox.

- An artificially designed luminescent ability is claimed to testify to **'nature's vitality'**
- The luminescence itself is 'real' (resulting from actual biological processes), but the **reason** for its existence lies in human product design
- Is the 'wonder' experienced by the consumer natural or artificial?

This contrasts with the experience of starlight implied in Khoj document 1. The stars in the night sky exist independently of humans, and humans project 'traces of nature' onto them. However, the light of Firefly Petunia is **light designed from the outset for human sensory experience**. This light is not a trace of nature, but a trace of human design. Nevertheless, the light is 'real'.

**Need for Revision**: The criterion of 'authenticity' must be redefined from origin to **process**. The light of Firefly Petunia is 'real' because it is the product of an **actual biochemical process**. The artificiality of the origin does not nullify the naturalness of the process.

### 2.3 Tension Point 3: Collision between Product Distribution and Biological Temporality

Firefly Petunia is a **living commodity**. This creates the following ontological tensions.

1. **Standardization vs. Individuality**: All Firefly Petunias share the same genetic design, but each individual grows under different environmental conditions and exhibits different luminescence intensities. 'Commodity' demands standardization, but 'living being' demands individuality.
2. **Seed Control and Biological Reproduction**: Light Bio sells only living plants, not seeds. This is a deliberate measure to block the possibility of biological reproduction. However, plants are inherently self-reproducing beings. This tension reveals an ontological conflict between the **artificial immortality** of the commodity and the **natural reproducibility** of the living being.
3. **Death and Product Lifespan**: Firefly Petunia eventually dies. But is its death a 'natural' death or 'product disposal'? The ontological meaning of death becomes ambiguous.

**Need for Revision**: The ontological contradictions arising from commodifying living beings must be acknowledged, and a new ontological category of **'living artifact'** must be established. This category must simultaneously capture the autonomy of a living being and the designedness of an artifact.

### 2.4 Tension Point 4: Luminescence as Environment vs. Plant as Object

The most significant ontological tension concerns whether Firefly Petunia is an **object** or an **environment**.

**As Object**: Firefly Petunia is a material object that is purchased, owned, and displayed. It is sold potted, with a specific price ($29-$49.99). This is how traditional ontology understands artifacts.

**As Environment**: However, the luminescence of Firefly Petunia is not merely a property of an object. It **reconfigures the sensory order** of the surrounding space. A glowing plant in a dark room changes the ambience of the room, induces the viewer's process of visual adaptation, and incorporates the temporal rhythm (day-night) into the experience. This is not a change of an object, but a change in **environmental conditions**.

Khoj document 3 metaphorically describes film as a "world wound on a spool," a "magic carpet of fantasy." Firefly Petunia operates in a similar manner. It creates a small **sensory world**. The luminescent plant is not simply 'something to be seen', but forms an **environment that the viewer enters**.

This connects to the concept of 'media as environment' presented in Lee Yong-wook's theory of Artificial Nature. Firefly Petunia demonstrates an **environmental mode of being** that is difficult to capture with traditional object ontology.

**Need for Revision**: Beyond the dichotomy of 'object' and 'environment', an **object-environment continuum** must be introduced. Firefly Petunia exists as an object under certain conditions (daytime) and as an environment under others (nighttime). Its mode of being is not fixed but shifts contextually.

## 3. Synthesis: Six Revision Proposals for the Ontology of Artificial Nature

Synthesizing the above analysis, the following revisions are proposed based on the Firefly Petunia case.

**1. Plurality of Ontological Modalities**
The dichotomy of nature/artifice cannot be maintained as a single ontological distinction. Instead, multiple ontological modalities must be recognized, depending on the constitutive process and relational context of each entity.

**2. Procedural Authenticity**
The criterion of authenticity must shift from origin to process. The light of Firefly Petunia is 'real' because it is the product of an actual biochemical process, and this does not contradict the fact that its origin lies in human design.

**3. Category of Living Artifact**
Beyond the traditional divide between living beings and artifacts, an ontological category possessing the characteristics of both must be established. This category must simultaneously capture biological autonomy and designed intentionality.

**4. Object-Environment Continuum**
Entities like Firefly Petunia move between object and environment depending on context. Ontological analysis must be based on relational context, not fixed categories.

**5. Temporal Ontology**
The mode of being of Firefly Petunia transforms according to daytime (object) and nighttime (environment). While existing ontology has focused on spatial categories (material/immaterial, natural/artificial), this case demonstrates that **temporal rhythm** determines the mode of being.

**6. Ontological Tension of Commodity-Life**
The tension between the logic of commodity distribution (standardization, control of reproduction, disposability) and the logic of living beings (individuality, self-reproduction, death) must be established as a core agenda for ontological analysis.

## Conclusion

Firefly Petunia is a case demonstrating that Artificial Nature is not merely sensory mimicry but an **ontological expansion into the biological realm**. This plant does not imitate nature's light; it embodies a process of **ontological production**, where data (gene sequences) are used to design new life capabilities, and these capabilities are realized within commodity distribution and sensory experience.

However, this expansion does not destroy the existing distinctions between nature/artifice, object/environment, and living being/artifact, but rather places them in a state of tension. Firefly Petunia is a hybrid being: **living yet designed, object yet environment, natural process yet artificial origin**. The direction for the ontology of Artificial Nature should be to utilize this tension as an analytical resource rather than resolving it.

**Core Revision Request**: The Firefly Petunia case demands that existing ontology reconceptualize the 'natural' and the 'artificial' based on **mode** (how something exists) rather than **origin**. Whether the origin is natural or artificial, the role an entity performs and the relationships it forms within an environment should become the criteria for ontological judgment. This implies a shift from traditional **substance ontology** to **relational ontology**.
