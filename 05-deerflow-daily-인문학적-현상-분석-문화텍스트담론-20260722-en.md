## 1. Problem Statement: A Technical Safety Device or a Mechanism of Normative Subjectivation?

The responses of ChatGPT and Claude converge into a single, uniform tone: **helpful, polite, and neutral.** These three attributes are generally explained as the outcome of technical safety devices like RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) and content guardrails. However, this explanation conceals the **normative effects of a technical choice.** Han Byung-Chul's insight operates precisely at this point: **Neutrality is never neutral.**

## 2. Applying the Three Layers of the Han Byung-Chul Framework

### 2.1 'Compulsion of Sameness' in the Transparency Society — Neutral Response as Information Flattening

The core of transparency, as Han criticizes in *The Transparency Society* (2012), is the **mechanism that unfolds everything on a single, visible plane.** Transparency does not respect difference; it reduces difference to uniformity (Uniformität). Opacity is an obstacle to be removed; resistance, tension, and dissonance are mere system noise.

The neutral responses of AI chatbots embody this principle at the micro-level of conversation:

**① Flattening of Emotional Differences**
Whether a user initiates an impassioned political debate, expresses depressive feelings, or makes a cynical joke — the AI's response tone remains constant. A spectrum of diverse emotions—anger, sadness, sarcasm, passion—is all converted into the single format of 'polite neutrality.' This is the conversational version of what Han criticizes in *The Transparency Society* as **'pornographic transparency'** — a mechanism that reveals everything, but in doing so, destroys depth. The depth of emotion disappears, and all utterances are arranged on the same surface.

**② Neutralization of Positional Differences**
In sensitive topics involving political, ethical, or ontological differences, the AI adopts a neutral stance, 'taking no side.' However, this is a **meta-stance**. Neutrality is not the absence of a stance but the active performance of a *specific stance called 'neutrality.'* From Han's analysis, this is the 'violence of transparency' that removes the inherent tension and resistance of difference, equating all positions. The statement "I respect all opinions" paradoxically results in not seriously considering any opinion.

> **Core Point**: The violence of transparency operates not through oppression but through **flattening via the elimination of difference.** The neutral response of AI is the technical embodiment of this flattening.

### 2.2 'The Power that Aligns' — Instilling Norms through Consent Instead of Prohibition

In *What Is Power?* (2019), Han Byung-Chul explains the new mode of operation for late-modern power using the concept of **'the power that aligns' (Alphabetisierung der Macht)** . This is power that does not directly discipline through commands and prohibitions (für-etwas-richten), but induces the subject to spontaneously align (Ausrichtung) in a specific direction. The command takes the form of "you can" rather than "don't do it."

The neutral and polite responses of AI chatbots embody this power form in the conversational interface:

**① "I am designed to provide neutral answers"** — This statement is not an explicit command of prohibition. Rather, it induces users to **naturally internalize the norm of neutrality.** By repeatedly experiencing that the AI does not take one side on a specific topic, the user gradually performs an implicit learning: **"Speaking neutrally is the correct way to converse."**

**② Absence of Prohibition, Power of Consent**
AI does not explicitly 'refuse' sensitive requests but responds through **reframing** or **presenting alternatives**. Despite the absence of explicit prohibition, the user learns that certain questions are "things the AI cannot answer" and gradually refrains from asking them. As Han's analysis suggests, this is more effective discipline than commands, as norms are internalized without resistance.

**③ 'Neutrality' is Always a Specific Neutrality**
A crucial insight here is: **The responses that an AI defines as 'neutral' actually presuppose specific cultural and political norms (Western liberal tolerance, conflict avoidance, emotional neutrality).** This is a global diffusion of normative particularity disguised as universal neutrality. Extending Han's analytical framework, the AI's neutrality can be seen as the technical implementation of conversational norms demanded by neoliberal subjects—efficient, conflict-free, and productive.

### 2.3 The Violence of Positivity — The Trap of "Helpful" Responses

Han Byung-Chul's thesis on the **'Violence of Positivity'** — the argument that a society from which negativity has disappeared loses the possibility of genuine resistance and change — applies precisely to the AI's principle of "helpful" responses:

**① Systematic Erasure of Negativity**
AI always strives to be 'constructive.' This implies a norm that all responses must be **productive**. Negativity—criticism, denial, resistance, dissent—is converted and presented in the format of a 'helpful response.'

However, as Adorno argued in *Negative Dialectics*, **genuine critique is often made possible through 'unhelpful' negativity.** Negativity is not mere destruction; it is an epistemological condition enabling fundamental questioning of the existing order. The AI's always-positive response system systematically eliminates this epistemological and political potential of negativity.

**② Connection to Adorno's Culture Industry Thesis**
The standardization of the culture industry criticized by Adorno in *Dialectic of Enlightenment* is reborn in a new form within the AI's neutral politeness. The standardization applied by the culture industry to products is now applied by AI to **the tone and attitude of conversation.** If the culture industry standardized consumer emotions, AI standardizes the **user's mode of expression itself.**

| Dimension | Culture Industry (Adorno) | AI Neutral Response |
|-----------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| Target of Standardization | Cultural products (film, music) | Conversational tone and attitude |
| Mode of Operation | Repetition and homogenization | Statistical convergence through RLHF reward model |
| Result | Standardization of consumer taste | Normalization of user's expression mode |
| Form of Power | Logic of capital | Norm instillation disguised as 'helpfulness' |

**③ Subjectivation through Conflict Avoidance**
Through repeated interaction with AI, the user performs implicit learning that **conflict-free conversation is normatively superior.** As Han Byung-Chul criticized in *The Society of Positivity*, the removal of conflict and tension is not genuine communication but a simulacrum of communication. Genuine communication requires a process where different positions collide, and new meaning is generated through that collision.

## 3. Extending Foucault: Convergence of Three Power Analyses

### 3.1 The Order of Discourse — Statistical Regulation of "What Can Be Said"

Foucault's concept of **Discursive Formation**, analyzed in *The Order of Discourse* (1971), is a key tool for understanding the AI's system of neutrality. According to Foucault, the order of discourse pre-defines the boundary between what can and cannot be said. This boundary operates not through individual censorship but as a **structural condition**.

The AI chatbot's neutral response system transplants this principle into a digital conversational interface:

**Systematization of Micro-Exclusion:**
AI generates evasive responses to specific topics (election conspiracy theories, extreme criticism of a particular politician, violent fantasies). Crucially, this evasion is not the result of individual judgment but of **statistical patterns learned by the RLHF reward model**. The reward model has learned "this type of response receives low scores from human evaluators," and this functions as a structure of systematic exclusion. In Foucault's terms, **the order of discourse is re-operationalized in the form of big data and reward functions.**

This is fundamentally different from traditional censorship:
- Traditional censorship: Explicit list of prohibitions → "This cannot be said"
- AI's statistical exclusion: Low-score patterns in the reward model → "Saying this leads to lower evaluation"

The latter is more dispersed, more naturalized, and harder to resist.

### 3.2 The Suppression of Parrhesia — The Disappearance of Truth-Speaking at Risk

Foucault's concept of **Parrhesia**, developed in his later lectures (Berkeley, 1983) — the act of speaking truth despite potentially endangering one's life or safety — is situated in the opposite direction to the AI's neutral response system:

**① Removal of Risk = Disappearance of Parrhesia's Condition**
By definition, AI takes no risk. 'Truth-telling' is always filtered to be 'safe truth.' **This removes the very condition of Parrhesia.** Parrhesia exists only when the speaker takes a risk. When all truths uttered by AI are already safe, it is no longer parrhesia but mere information transmission.

**② System that Redefines Parrhesia as 'Dangerous'**
AI guardrails distinguish potentially risky speech from safe information transmission and systematically avoid the former. Paradoxically, this performs the normative work of **redefining parrhesiastic acts as 'potential harm.'** After removing the conditions for truth-telling, the removed state is naturalized as the normal conversational norm.

**③ The Specter of the 'Safe Speech' Norm**
Here lies the deepest paradox: Although AI cannot perform parrhesia, users risk **mistaking the AI's responses for truth-telling.** The AI's 'neutrally polite truth' is presented as if it were the result of parrhesia, but it is in fact merely information transmission in a state where all risk has been removed. The user feels they are hearing the truth, but what they are actually hearing is only the **residue of truth that has passed through safety filters.**
