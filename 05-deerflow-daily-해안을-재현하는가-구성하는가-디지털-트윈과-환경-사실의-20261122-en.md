# The Ontology of a Coastal Digital Twin: How Are Environmental Facts Negotiated?

## 1. Representation or Construction? — The Direction of the Answer

A coastal digital twin does **not** present the coast as an accurate representation of terrain that already exists. Rather, it **newly constructs a manageable environment by identifying only what can be measured as a risk and relegating unquantified lived experiences to supplementary data**. This “construction” is not merely a matter of social consensus. It takes place at the intersection of the chain of human and nonhuman actors described by Latour’s actor-network theory and the “intra-action” described by Barad’s agential realism.

---

## 2. Latour’s ANT: The Digital Twin as a Chain of Actors

### 2.1. Mobilization and Re-articulation

In Latour’s sociology of science and technology, **scientific facts are not discovered but constructed**. Tide-gauge sensors, satellite imagery, port logistics records, and residents’ flood reports are each **inscription devices** through which different worlds are materialized in different ways. The digital twin mobilizes these heterogeneous inscriptions onto a single **computable plane**.

Here, the concept of **the transparency of friction**, identified in Khoj Document 3, comes into play. Sensor gaps, model delays, and fishers’ informal knowledge each resist the system in different ways. When this resistance becomes “visible”—that is, when the friction becomes transparent—the system encounters its own limits. In Latourian terms:

- **Sensor gaps**: The “silence” or “breakdown” of a nonhuman actor—the tide gauge—forms a point of translation. When a sensor fails, the weight assigned to satellite imagery may be increased, or fishers’ oral accounts may be entered as correction values.
- **Model delays**: When a predictive model outputs tidal data from six hours earlier, the condition of the seawall “at this very moment” exists outside the model. This delay reveals that the model cannot capture the simultaneity of the environment.
- **Fishers’ informal knowledge**: Knowledge such as “When that rock is submerged, the eastern seawall collapses first” is not incorporated into the system because it has not been quantified. Yet if this knowledge proves correct when a typhoon actually arrives, the system retrospectively **validates** it through the failure of its own prediction.

In this process, **“environmental facts” are negotiated**. Facts are not discovered; they are the result of attempts by diverse actors to “translate” their heterogeneous worlds and establish them within a single network.

### 2.2. Reversibility and Irreversibilization

Once the order of seawall reinforcement has been decided and the budget allocated, the decision becomes **irreversibilized**. Once concrete has been poured, a fisher’s knowledge that “the eastern section should come first” no longer remains an equally valid alternative; it is relegated to “information that was ignored in the past.” Yet this irreversibilization is never complete. If the eastern seawall collapses in the next typhoon, the system becomes **reversible** again—the entire chain of decision-making is reconsidered.

The key point of a Latourian analysis is this: **a digital twin does not reflect the environment “as it is”; it is a device that makes visible how a particular actor-network becomes stabilized.**

## 3. Barad’s Intervention through Agential Realism and the Apparatus

### 3.1. Measurement Apparatuses Divide the World

Barad argues that **apparatuses divide the world**. The world is not a collection of independently existing objects but a field of **phenomena** in which particular boundaries are drawn through acts of measurement. From this perspective, the digital twin:

- Produces the phenomenon of “sea level” through **tide-gauge sensors**
- Produces the phenomenon of “coastal-line change” through **satellite imagery**
- Produces the phenomenon of “flooding as lived experience” through **residents’ reports**

These three phenomena possess different temporalities and spatialities. Tide gauges generate data every second, satellite imagery once a day, and residents’ reports only when an incident occurs. The digital twin **forces these heterogeneous temporalities into synchronization within a single time series**. This synchronization is the concrete operation of the **boundary-drawing** that Barad describes.

### 3.2. The Cycle of Intra-action

For Barad, **intra-action** is an operation through which the conditions of existence of objects are constituted together, rather than objects existing first and then interacting with one another. In a digital twin:

```text
Reinforcement plan proposed by the predictive model
→ Residents’ detours, refusals, and collective responses
→ New behavioral data
→ Next round of training data
→ Changes in administrative priorities
→ New predictive model
```

This cycle is not based on a model in which “objects exist first and then interact.” Rather, **the predictive model** and **residents’ responses** constitute one another. When the predictive model states that “Seawall A is at risk,” this is not merely an observation; it is an act that **actualizes a risk zone**. When residents respond by choosing detours or engaging in collective action, those responses once again become **training data for the next predictive model**.

This is the core of Barad’s **agential realism**: measurement and intervention cannot be separated, and the world is **brought into reality** through acts of measurement.

## 4. The Argument for “Knowing” as a Temporal Procedure

We now turn to the central proposition: **knowing the environment is not the acceptance of observational results, but a temporal procedure of measurement, classification, contestation, and reconfiguration.**

### 4.1. The Problem with Traditional Epistemology

Traditional scientific epistemology assumes a linear flow:

**observation → data → model → prediction → action**

Within this model, “knowing” is equivalent to accepting the prediction produced by the model. Yet this model encounters several problems:

1. When **sensor gaps** occur, observation itself becomes incomplete.
2. When **model delays** occur, the temporality of the “present” breaks down.
3. When **fishers’ informal knowledge** conflicts with the prediction, the question arises: “What counts as valid knowledge?”

### 4.2. “Knowing” as a Temporal Procedure

If we trace the actual operation of a digital twin, **knowing is constituted through the following sequence**:

1. **Measurement**: A tide gauge measures the sea. This is a selective act—decisions have already been made about what to measure and at what intervals.

2. **Classification**: The measurement is classified as either “risk” or “safe.” The criteria for this classification are established through past data and policy decisions. Whether a tide level of 1.5 meters constitutes danger or safety is **not a natural fact but a normative decision**.

3. **Contestation**: Residents contest the classification. Objections such as “That sensor is in the wrong location” or “Our neighborhood has no problems even at 1.5 meters” reveal the **local context** that the system has missed. The problem of algorithmic bias identified in Khoj Document 4—the fact that systems reproduce prejudices embedded in historical data—comes to the surface at precisely this stage.

4. **Reconfiguration**: If the contestation is accepted, sensor locations are adjusted, classification criteria are changed, and the model is retrained. This reconfiguration is how the system **learns through its own errors**.

The important point here is that **knowing is not the completion of these four stages but the ongoing operation of the cycle itself**. Only when fishers contest the system and the system responds by modifying its own criteria does “better knowledge” emerge.

### 4.3. The Dynamics of the Feedback Cycle

The cycle in which reinforcement plans proposed by predictive models interact with residents’ responses can be analyzed as follows.

**Cycle 1: Dialogical learning between the system and residents**

```text
Model: “Reinforce Seawall A first.”
Residents: “A is fine; B should come first.” (Refusal)
System: Install additional sensors around B and intensify data collection.
Next model: “Reinforce Seawall B first.” (Recalibration)
```

**Cycle 2: Reconfiguration of administrative priorities**

```text
Model output → Administrative order → Budget allocation → Seawall construction
             ↘ Residents’ protests → Administrative review → Reallocation of the next budget
```

**Cycle 3: The self-fulfillment and rupture of training data**

```text
Historical data: “Seawall A was dangerous” → The model classifies A as dangerous
→ Investment is concentrated on A → B is neglected → B actually becomes dangerous
→ Self-fulfilling confirmation that “the model was correct”
```

The central argument of Khoj Document 2—the transition **from technological editing to techno-generation**—takes concrete form in this digital-twin case. When the digital twin goes beyond merely editing existing data through collection, classification, and visualization, and when residents’ responses and the system’s retraining form an emergent loop, the system approaches the example of **techno-generation** described in Khoj Document 1. In other words, a self-transformative cycle emerges in which the system reconstructs the conditions of its own operation.

### 4.4. Several Objections and Responses

**Objection 1**: “Ultimately, doesn’t the model simply become more accurate by learning from more data? If so, isn’t the temporal procedure merely the elimination of provisional errors?”

**Response**: No. The process of contestation and reconfiguration is not simply the elimination of errors; it is a process in which **the criteria for what counts as ‘valid data’ change in themselves**. When fishers’ informal knowledge is incorporated into the system, the boundaries of the environment that the system can “see” are redrawn. This is not an increase in “accuracy” but a **change in the ontological framework**.

**Objection 2**: “If the temporal procedure continues indefinitely, does that not make decisions impossible?”

**Response**: In practice, irreversibilization occurs—budget allocations and the pouring of concrete interrupt the procedure. Yet this irreversibilization is temporary. The procedure resumes with the next typhoon, the next election, or the next budget cycle. “Knowing” is the **dialectic between discontinuous decisions and continuous cycles**.

**Objection 3**: “Latour and Barad follow different theoretical trajectories. Is using them together not eclecticism?”

**Response**: Although Latour and Barad begin from different points—ANT from actor-network theory, and agential realism from quantum physics—the two theories converge on the insight that **measurement transforms the world**. Latour explains this transformation as a “chain of translations,” while Barad explains it as the “intra-action of boundary-drawing.” The digital-twin case shows that these two accounts are complementary: Latour enables us to analyze the **structure of connections** among sensors, models, administrators, and residents, while Barad enables us to analyze how those connections **generate ontological boundaries**.

## 5. Conclusion: The Ontological Meaning of Knowing the Environment

A coastal digital twin does not reflect the coast “as it is.” It is **an arena in which tide-gauge sensors, satellite imagery, port logistics records, and residents’ reports each record different worlds in different ways, and in which these records collide and are negotiated**.

In this process, **“environmental facts” are constituted in the following manner**:

1. **Nonhuman actors—sensors, satellites, and algorithms—**convert particular aspects of the environment into recordable forms.
2. **Measurement apparatuses** force these records into synchronization on a single temporal and spatial plane.
3. **Residents’ contestations** create ruptures in this synchronization and demand alternative records.
4. **Administrative systems** resolve—and thereby irreversibilize—these conflicts through new classification criteria and budget allocations.
5. **The next cycle** challenges and reconfigures that resolution once again.

**The claim that knowing is not the acceptance of observational results, but a temporal procedure of measurement, classification, contestation, and reconfiguration, can be strongly defended through this case.** In a digital twin, “knowing” is not the model output at a particular moment; it includes the entire ongoing cycle in which sensors fail, fishers protest, and administrators recalibrate the system. When this cycle is interrupted—when the system no longer modifies its classification criteria and no longer accepts residents’ objections—“knowing” comes to a halt, and only “power” remains.

The insight of **relational technology** proposed in Khoj Document 5 is decisive in this regard. When a digital twin is designed solely according to the logic of efficiency—faster and more accurate prediction—it closes the cycle of constructive knowing and becomes a one-way apparatus of control. Conversely, when the system continually accepts residents’ contestations and recalibrates its own classification criteria, the digital twin becomes **a procedural apparatus that learns with the environment**.

The ontological status of a coastal digital twin is **neither representation nor construction, but an arena of negotiation**. As long as the temporality of this negotiation is maintained, the system functions as a procedure of knowing. When that temporality closes, the system transforms into an instrument of domination.
