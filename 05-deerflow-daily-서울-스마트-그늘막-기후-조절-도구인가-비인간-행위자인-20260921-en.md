# Seoul Smart Shade Structure, Ontological Analysis — Tool or Environmental Producer?

---

## Introduction: Specific Conditions of the Case

The smart shade structure first installed in Gwangjin-gu, Seoul in 2019 operates with the following technical configuration:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Sensor Input** | Temperature sensor (ambient temperature detection), wind speed sensor (wind strength) |
| **Algorithm** | Temperature ≥15°C + after sunrise → extend; wind speed ≥7m/s for 2 seconds → retract |
| **Drive System** | Solar power generation + motor-driven automatic opening/closing |
| **Additional Features** | Nighttime LED security light (using solar charge) |
| **Installation Context** | Pedestrian-heavy crosswalks and intersections, areas without street trees |

This device connects **natural variables (temperature, wind, solar time)** with **technical variables (algorithm, motor, IoT communication)** to actively produce the urban microclimatic condition of 'shade'.

---

## 1. Interpretation as a 'Tool' — Present but Insufficient

The smart shade structure clearly operates according to human design and purpose. As part of Seoul's heat wave response policy, a clear **human intention** to prevent pedestrian heatstroke lies at the foundation of the system's construction. From this perspective, the device is a simple tool:

> A technical extension created by humans to cope with nature (sun, heat) — essentially no different from existing umbrellas, awnings, or fixed shade structures.

However, this interpretation reveals limitations at two critical points. **First**, unlike fixed shade structures, this device 'judges' in real time. Even if the sun is covered by clouds, if the temperature remains at 15°C, it does not immediately retract — this is not a simple physical reaction but **algorithmic decision-making based on thresholds**. **Second**, the moment this device extends, the temperature, solar radiation, and perceived temperature of the pedestrian space change on site. This goes beyond the moment the tool is 'used' — it is the moment **the tool produces a condition**.

---

## 2. Interpretation as a Non-Human Actor — Four Layers of Agency

Within the framework of Actor-Network Theory, the smart shade structure possesses the following **four layers of actant agency**:

### 2.1 Sensing Agency
The temperature sensor converts the molecular motion of air into electrical signals. This conversion does not **replace human senses** but **continuously captures minute temperature fluctuations** that humans cannot perceive. Pedestrians feel "hot," but the sensor measures down to 0.1°C increments. This non-human sensing becomes the condition for extension/retraction.

### 2.2 Decisional Agency
The algorithm is more than a simple if-then. It combines multiple sensor inputs (temperature × wind speed × time) to **evaluate the situation**. In particular, the fact that it does not immediately retract when clouds pass (hysteresis) shows that **state-maintenance judgment** intervenes, not a simple reaction. This judgment is not made by humans in real time — humans only designed the rules (algorithm), while **individual situational judgment is delegated to the system**.

### 2.3 Material Agency
When the motor extends the shade canopy, the surface temperature at the shaded point drops sharply. This temperature change alters pedestrian behavior (moving to the shade, stopping, lingering). That is, **the device's material operation triggers human spatial behavior**. This is not "the tool is used," but a reversed causality: **the device produces the conditions for human behavior**.

### 2.4 Environmental Agency
This is the most important layer. The smart shade structure does not simply create 'shadow'; it produces **local microclimatic variation in the urban environment**:

- When sunlight is present: shade extended → reduced surface radiant heat → perceived temperature reduction of 3–5°C
- When strong wind is present: shade retracted → minimized wind load → structural safety and pedestrian protection
- At night: solar-powered LED → creation of lighting environment → crime prevention effect

These three modes mean that a single device **produces completely different environmental conditions depending on time and weather**. The shade structure exists less as an 'object' and more as a **'environment' that occurs**.

## 3. Ontological Shift: From Tool-Object to Environment-Condition

A key ontological distinction emerges here:

| Distinction | Instrumental Perspective | Environmental Perspective |
|-------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Mode of Existence** | Object manipulated by humans | Condition within which humans act |
| **Relationship with Humans** | User — Tool (direct manipulation) | Inhabitant — Environment (mediated experience) |
| **Mode of Operation** | Command execution | Production of state through feedback loops |
| **Outcome** | Function performance (providing shade) | Condition generation (microclimate modulation) |

A pedestrian passing under the smart shade structure does not 'use' the device. They do not press a button, pull, or manipulate it. **They merely walk within the condition (shade, cooling) that the shade structure has produced.** This is a characteristic feature of **technology as environment** — technology becomes not the foreground (object) but the background (environment) that shapes human behavior.

---

## 4. Reconstructing the Ontological Scope of Artificial Nature

This case offers three implications for the ontological scope of Artificial Nature:

### 4.1 Hybridity of Nature and Artificiality
To what extent is the 'shade' produced by the smart shade structure natural, and from where is it artificial? The sun's position and intensity are natural, but the timing, duration, and spatial range of the shade's appearance are determined by the algorithm. This shade is **neither pure nature nor pure artifice; it is a hybrid**. Borrowing Latour's terms, we stand in a shade that 'was never modern'.

### 4.2 Mechanism of Environmentalization
What this device demonstrates is the **automatic production of the environment**. Humans no longer 'make' or 'manipulate' shade. Humans (designers) set the **initial conditions** for environmental production, and thereafter the system autonomously maintains and modulates the environment. Pedestrians participate in this process but function not as **primary agents but as beneficiaries of the environment and simultaneously as components of the environment** (the 'pedestrian volume' that sensors detect).

### 4.3 Asymmetric Responsibility
An interesting point is the distribution of responsibility. If a typhoon damages the shade structure, whose responsibility is it? Did the algorithm misjudge the wind speed? Is it the installer's design error? Is the natural phenomenon of the typhoon the 'cause'? This asymmetry reveals the **ontological ambiguity of artificial nature** — the shade structure is both an object for which humans bear responsibility as a 'tool' and simultaneously an 'actor' that acts according to autonomous judgment, diffusing responsibility.

## Conclusion: 'Neither Simply a Tool Nor Merely an Object' — Condition Producer

Seoul's smart shade structure **cannot be reduced** to a human tool, nor **need it be exaggerated** as a fully autonomous non-human actor. This device occupies the following ontological position:

> **A generator of environmental conditions produced by a hybrid network of human-nonhuman-nature-technology.**

This device:
1. Possesses **distributed agency** of sensing, judgment, and operation,
2. **Modulates urban microclimate in real time**,
3. Causes pedestrians to **live within that condition without manipulation**,
4. **Inextricably hybridizes** natural variables (sun, wind, temperature) with technical variables (algorithm, motor, IoT).

Thus, the answer to the original question is: **The smart shade structure does not remain a human tool; rather, it should be seen as an actant-network that newly produces the environmental condition of 'shade' itself within the interaction network of climate, sensors, algorithms, and pedestrians.** However, this agency appears not as subjective will in the traditional sense, but in the form of **distributed causality** and **condition production**.

This ontological shift could extend beyond smart shade structures to an analytical framework for various automated environmental control devices in cities (smart streetlights, automatic irrigation systems, smart crosswalks).
