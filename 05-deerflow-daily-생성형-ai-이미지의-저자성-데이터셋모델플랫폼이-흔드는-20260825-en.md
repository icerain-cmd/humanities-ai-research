## Case Analysis of Refik Anadol's *Unsupervised*: The Rupture of Traditional Authorship Theory and the Aesthetic Status of Generative AI Images

### 1. Case Overview: The Joint Operation of Three Layers

*Unsupervised* is a large-scale generative AI image exhibition installed in the MoMA Gund Lobby, an image event co-created by the following three non-human actors:

| Layer | Components | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Dataset** | Public metadata of approximately 200,000 works from MoMA's collection (paintings, sculptures, photographs, architecture, design) | The condition 'learned' by the generative model — pre-structuring the space of possible images |
| **Generative Model** | GAN-based machine learning model (NVIDIA technology infrastructure) | Infinite image generation along probabilistic trajectories over the learned distribution |
| **Exhibition Interface** | 24-foot high-resolution LED screen, real-time environmental data input (light, motion, sound, weather) | The condition under which generated results become visible to viewers — environmental feedback recursively intervenes in the generation process |

These three layers are not a simple instrumental superposition. The curation of the dataset determines the 'vocabulary' of the images; the model's probabilistic generation operates the 'grammar' of the images; and the environmental feedback of the exhibition interface transforms the 'pragmatics' of the images in real time. In other words, the artwork does not exist as a fixed object but as an **event** where these three axes intersect.

---

### 2. Three Premises of Traditional Authorship Theory and Their Rupture

Traditional authorship theory rests on three premises: (a) the artist's intention is the ultimate origin of the artwork's meaning; (b) the viewer's interpretation completes that meaning; (c) aesthetic value and responsibility are attributed to this intention-interpretation loop. *Unsupervised* implodes each of these premises.

#### (a) The Centrality of 'Intention' — From Author to Condition-Setter

Anadol certainly acts as an 'author.' He collaborates with the MoMA curatorial team to curate the dataset, designs the architecture and hyperparameters of the learning model, and adjusts the visual style of the generated results. However, in the traditional sense, Anadol does not possess an **intention** for each individual generated image. None of the tens of frames per second generated by the model were pre-visualized or intended by Anadol. He is not the 'author' of individual images, but rather a **'condition-setter'** who establishes the conditions for image generation.

This precisely corresponds to the concept of the **AI Player** defined in *Journal of Humanities Contents* Vol. 78. The AI Player is a subject who integrates technology into their cognitive system through four behavioral categories: questioning, editing, curation, and publishing (Document 1, p.226). As a typical AI Player, Anadol poses the question 'a dream about the MoMA collection' to the model, edits and curates the generated results, and publishes the final output. However, his intention does not govern the **specific form** of the generated outputs but only defines the **probabilistic conditions** under which they arise.

A decisive tension emerges here. As an AI Player, Anadol simultaneously possesses greater authority (control over the dataset, model, and interface) and exercises lesser authority (inability to control individual images) than a traditional author. This constitutes an **inversion between the scale and precision of control**.

#### (b) The Sovereignty of 'Interpretation' — From Viewer to Affector of the Generative Process

In the traditional model, the viewer stands before a fixed artwork and exercises their interpretive autonomy. However, the viewer of *Unsupervised* faces a different set of conditions.

First, the image the viewer encounters is **not fixed**. Because the model continuously generates new images, even the same viewer will have a completely different visual experience a minute later. This demands not an interpretation of an 'object-work' but an **affection** for a **process**.

Second, the viewer's own movement and presence are **fed back in real time** into the generative process through the exhibition interface. Changes in Gund Lobby's lighting, viewer trajectories, sound, and external weather data all affect the model's generative trajectory. The viewer is no longer an 'interpreter' of meaning but becomes a **parameter** of meaning generation. The sovereignty of viewing is replaced not by interpretive freedom, but by the condition of being able to physically **affect** the generative process.

This fundamentally overturns the temporality of meaning formation. In the traditional model, meaning originates in the **past** (the artist's intention) and is completed in the **present** (the viewer's interpretation). In a generative AI exhibition, meaning remains open toward the **future** (the next image to be generated), and the present is merely a transient point of passage in that process.

#### (c) Attribution of 'Responsibility' — The dilemma of distributed responsibility

In traditional authorship theory, aesthetic responsibility is attributed to the author. However, the images of *Unsupervised* are the product of a distributed network of actors:

- **Dataset bias**: The MoMA collection is skewed toward Western art history. This bias structures the 'unconscious' of the generative model. Just as Anadol cannot control the content of individual generated images, he cannot fully control how the historical conditions of the dataset (Western-centricity, gender imbalance, racial bias) permeate the images. **[Epistemic asymmetry]**
- **Model opacity**: The path by which images are generated in the latent space of a GAN is close to a black box. Anadol's studio (in collaboration with NVIDIA, Google) has only partial access to this black box. **[Ontological asymmetry]**
- **Platform conditions**: The exhibition was made possible through the sponsorship of Hyundai Card and the technological infrastructure of NVIDIA. These companies are not merely sponsors; they set the **material conditions** of image generation itself. The computing capacity of GPUs, the method of blockchain recording, and the NFT structure of the media art market predetermine the mode of existence of the images. **[Institutional asymmetry]**

Artnet critic Ben Davis sharply points this out: the metaphors of 'dream' and 'hallucination' used by Anadol "mystify what the technology is actually doing"[^2]. Responsibility for the generated images cannot be attributed solely to Anadol, or MoMA, or NVIDIA. It exists in a state of being **distributed, yet at the same time not fully attributable to anyone**.

### 3. Toward a Redefinition of Aesthetic Value: Three Axes of Generative Aesthetics

*Unsupervised* not only reveals points where existing authorship theory fails but also suggests the axes along which the unique aesthetic value of generative AI images might be evaluated.

**First, the richness of data**: How diverse is the dataset used? How complex are the relationships within it? The data from 200,000 MoMA works are not mere 'raw material' but a condition that determines the space of possible image generation.

**Second, the indeterminacy of the generative path**: To what extent does the model generate images along unpredictable trajectories? Anadol's design of the model to explore "fantasy, hallucination, and irrationality" rather than "classification, processing, and realistic reproduction"[^1] shows that this axis is deliberately chosen.

**Third, responsivity to the environment**: How open is the generative process to the physical conditions of the viewer and the space? This structure, where the viewer becomes part of the generative condition rather than a mere object of viewing, makes the traditional aesthetic notion of 'disinterested contemplation' impossible.

---

### 4. Conclusion: The Phase Shift of the Author Concept

The most fundamental premise of traditional authorship theory that this case challenges is the **causal chain between intention and meaning**. In the traditional model, A (author's intention) → B (artwork) → C (viewer's interpretation) forms a linear causality. In *Unsupervised*, this chain is reconfigured as follows:

```
[Condition-setting] → [Probabilistic generation] → [Environmental affection] → [Recursive feedback] → ...
     (Anadol)             (Model + Data)             (Viewer + Space)           (Interface)
```

In this chain, there is neither a fixed 'starting point' (author's intention) nor a fixed 'end point' (interpretation). Instead, the cycle of **condition-setting, probabilistic generation, environmental affection, and recursive feedback** continuously reconfigures meaning.

This extends the implications of the AI Player definition from the Khoj reference in a different direction. The AI Player (Documents 1, 5) is a subject "possessing the capacity to skillfully operate technologies and tools to design, produce, and publish valuable content." However, *Unsupervised* poses an additional question to this definition: **Does the AI Player design, produce, and publish individual images, or the conditions under which images occur?** If the latter, then the AI Player concept is not a new theory of the subject replacing traditional authorship theory, but rather a concept that induces a **phase shift** in the concept of 'author' itself—transforming it into a condition-setter, an infrastructure-designer, or a curator of probabilistic processes.

**Core proposition**: The generative AI image exhibition simultaneously collapses the three premises of traditional authorship theory (centrality of intention, sovereignty of interpretation, attribution of responsibility) and demands that aesthetic value be redefined from 'a property of fixed objects' to 'the dynamic arrangement of distributed conditions.' This is a more radical transformation than the death of the author (Barthes) or the openness of the text (Eco). For what collapses here is not only the authority of the author but also the identity of the artwork and the determinacy of meaning itself.
