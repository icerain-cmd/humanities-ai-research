### 2. The Temporal Structure of Nesting: A Reinterpretation of the SR Formula

#### 2.1. The Basic Temporal Structure of the SR Formula

The SR (Stimulus-Response) formula originates from behaviorist psychology, but here it needs to be understood as extended into a broader sense of temporal cyclical structure. Fundamentally, the SR formula assumes a linear temporal structure in which a stimulus (S) is given and a response (R) follows. However, in the nesting relationship between the Anthropocene and the Machinocene, this formula possesses a cyclical and multi-layered temporal structure that goes beyond simple linearity.

The material conditions of the Anthropocene (S₁) operate on a relatively long timescale. Climate change, biodiversity loss, and the cumulative effects of fossil fuel use are slow stimuli spanning decades to centuries. In contrast, the conditions for the reconstruction of sensation, attention, and judgment in the Machinocene (R₁) demand real-time responses on the order of milliseconds to seconds. Algorithmic feeds, recommendation systems, and real-time data processing induce immediate cognitive responses.

These two timescales do not simply exist in parallel; rather, R₁ forms a feedback loop that generates a new S₂. That is, nesting occurs in the way that the real-time responses of the Machinocene accumulate and alter the material conditions of the Anthropocene. For example, consumption patterns induced by algorithms (R₁) lead to large-scale resource consumption and environmental destruction (S₂).

This cycle is not simple repetition, but includes a process in which conditions are transformed and reconstructed at each stage. What is important here is that 'nesting' is not simple overlap, but a structural coupling in which different timescales operate simultaneously within a single event. ### 3. Simondon's Concept of Concretization and the Evolution of Technical Objects

#### 3.1. The Open Temporal Structure of Concretization

Gilbert Simondon's concept of concretization (concrétisation) explains the process by which a technical object gradually becomes concrete through functional overdetermination from its initial abstract state. An abstract technical object has separated functions and is dependent on the external environment, but a concretized technical object has functions that are mutually coordinated and acquire internal coherence, actively reconstructing its relationship with the environment.

Simondon's concretization has a temporally open structure. That is, the technical object continuously integrates new functions and redefines its relationship with the environment in the course of its evolution. This can be seen as an open version of the SR cycle described above. S (environmental conditions) and R (technical responses) move toward higher levels of integration through mutual coordination.

From this perspective, the nesting of the Anthropocene and the Machinocene can be interpreted as a kind of 'technical concretization' process. If the material conditions of the Anthropocene (resources, climate, ecosystems) are the initial abstract environmental conditions, then the sensation-attention-judgment reconstruction of the Machinocene forms a progressively concretized response to these conditions. Algorithms process environmental data in real time, adjust human cognitive responses to this data, and through a cycle that again changes environmental conditions, the technical object evolves.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Data Feedback Loops in Generative AI

However, the emergence of generative AI adds a new dimension to this concretization process. Generative AI learns from existing data to generate new synthetic data, and this synthetic data is then used as training data, forming a feedback loop. This can lead to a closed cycle that contrasts with the openness of Simondon's concretization.

The synthetic data feedback loop can be expressed in the SR formula as follows:

S₁ (original data) → R₁ (generative AI output) → S₂ (synthetic data) → R₂ (output of retrained AI) → ...

The key point in this cycle is that S₂ no longer accurately reflects the distribution of the original data. As generative AI uses its own output as input again, it tends to amplify or lose rare patterns or noise from the original data. This leads to the phenomenon of model collapse.

#### 3.3. Model Collapse and the Regressive Closed Loop

Model collapse is a phenomenon in which, when generative AI is repeatedly trained on self-generated data, the diversity of outputs decreases and errors are amplified, eventually rendering the model unable to generate useful information. This forms a regressive closed loop in the SR cycle. That is, the more it repeats, the simpler and more uniform the system's state becomes, losing its original diversity and complexity.

This process proceeds in the exact opposite direction from the functional overdetermination that Simondon's concretization pursues. While concretization creates higher complexity through the integration and mutual coordination of diverse functions, model collapse reduces complexity and reinforces homogeneity through the feedback loop. This suggests that the evolution of technical objects is not always progressive and can fall into a regressive closed loop under certain conditions.
