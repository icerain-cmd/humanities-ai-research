## Question

Is the 'transparency compulsion' and 'violence of positivity' that Han Byung-Chul critiques in *The Transparency Society* and *The Expulsion of the Other* being reproduced in the way knowledge-sharing platforms like the PLM Knowledge Bank design themselves — in the very premise that all knowledge should flow? In other words, how should we identify the risk that a commons oriented toward the free circulation of knowledge might instead become yet another transparency apparatus accelerating the very 'extinction of negativity' that Han problematizes?

# Han Byung-Chul's Transparency Critique and the PLM Knowledge Bank — An Analysis of Conceptual Tension

## I. Coordinates of Analysis: The Encounter Between Two Theoretical Terrains

The first task required for this analysis is to precisely locate the point of encounter between two philosophical discourses. Han Byung-Chul's transparency critique and Lee Yong-wook's PLM Knowledge Bank project are situated on apparently different terrains — one a diagnosis of digital capitalism's pathologies, the other a design philosophy for a technological platform — yet both share a common problematic: **how media environments structure the conditions of human thought and communication**.

Central to this encounter is the tension between Han Byung-Chul's concept of the **'dispositif of transparency'** and Lee Yong-wook's concept of **'hermeneutic friction'**. The following analysis aims to identify the theoretical dilemma confronting the PLM Knowledge Bank's design premises at the point where these two concepts intersect.

---

## II. Han Byung-Chul's Critique of Transparency: A Reconstruction of Core Theses

### 2.1 The Compulsion of Positivity and the Expulsion of Negativity

For Han, 'transparency' is not merely the visibility of information. It is a **neoliberal dispositif** that systematically converts everything into information and expels whatever cannot be informationalized — otherness, secrecy, strangeness, resistance, silence, hesitation, distance (Abstand).

> "Transparency makes humans like glass. Therein lies violence. Unlimited freedom and communication transform into total control." (The Transparency Society, Preface)

The key is the 'violence of positivity' (die Gewalt der Positivität). For Han, negativity does not simply mean 'badness' but the **experience of resistance, gap, delay, concealment, and threshold (Schwelle) necessary for thought and communication**. The transparent society eliminates all these negativities, leaving only 'smoothness' (Glätte).

> "Smoothness is the signature of the present age. It connects the sculptures of Jeff Koons, the iPhone, and Brazilian waxing." (Saving Beauty)

### 2.2 Elimination of Threshold, Extinction of Eros — And Pornography

One of Han's sharpest insights is the structural analogy between transparency and pornography. Eros is founded upon concealment, distance, delay — that is, negativity. Pornography, by contrast, reveals everything and eliminates every threshold.

> "The negativity of intermittence bestows radiance upon nakedness. The positivity of nakedness without a veil is pornography. It has lost its erotic radiance."

This metaphor extends directly to the problem of knowledge. **When knowledge circulates 'transparently', it means knowledge has lost all hermeneutic depth and become 'pornographic'** — when everything is visualized and immediately consumable as information, knowledge ceases to be an event (Ereignis) that provokes thought and becomes an object (Objekt) to be exhausted.

### 2.3 From Trust to Control, and the Society of the Same

The paradox of the transparency compulsion: the more transparency there is, the less trust there is. Because information is immediately available, social systems shift from trust to control. Furthermore, transparent communication produces a 'smoothing and homogenizing effect' (eine glättende und gleichschaltende Wirkung). It eliminates otherness and enforces synchronization and uniformity.

> "Transparency stabilizes the existing system. It forces everything into the interior, converting it into information."

Ultimately, the transparent society is the completed form of the **expulsion of the other** — a society where only the same (das Gleiche) circulates.

## III. Design Premises of Lee Yong-wook's PLM Knowledge Bank

### 3.1 Core Design Philosophy: "Data to the Individual, Knowledge to the Community"

The design premise of the PLM Knowledge Bank (Jiseong Gongbang) is clear: individual data is protected as privacy, but the knowledge generated from it must circulate as the community's knowledge commons. This presupposes both the **extractability of the personal** and the **formability of the communal**.

### 3.2 PLM's Four Conditions (C1-C4) and Hermeneutic Friction

Among the four conditions of PLM presented in Lee's recent papers, C3 (hermeneutic feedback) and C4 (resonant update) point to the need for a **hermeneutic relationship** that goes beyond mere information storage and retrieval. The concept of 'hermeneutic friction' intervenes decisively here — the claim that thought arises at the moment when the medium resists the human.

### 3.3 Artura and Artificial Nature

In Lee's theory of Artificial Nature, the media environment functions not as a mere tool but as a **second nature** that conditions human perception and thought. The design of this artificial nature — the four-element system of database, algorithm, UI, UX — itself structures a particular mode of perception and thought. The PLM Knowledge Bank is a concrete instantiation of this artificial nature, and its design is not merely a technical choice but a **determination of ontological conditions**.

## IV. Identifying Conceptual Tension: Five Layers of Analysis

### Layer 1: The Premise of Flow and the Compulsion of Positivity

**Problem:** If the PLM Knowledge Bank is designed on the premise that "all knowledge must flow" (die Positivität des Fließens), could this be another form of the positivity compulsion Han critiqued?

**Analysis:** The free circulation of knowledge appears to be an unequivocally positive value. But according to Han's analysis, when 'flow' itself is presupposed as a value — when the norm operates that all knowledge must be fluid, accessible, and transmittable without barriers — a problem emerges. This **compulsion of flow inevitably forms a dispositif that systematically excludes what does not flow**: concealment, silence, indeterminacy, resistance, strangeness.

**Core Tension:** When the PLM Knowledge Bank aims for the 'free circulation' of knowledge, the positivity of this goal paradoxically risks **invalidating the existence of knowledge that cannot circulate**. The formula "data to the individual" guarantees the non-circulatability of data, but at the level of 'knowledge,' a **normative pressure that all knowledge must circulate** may still arise.

### Layer 2: The Homogenization of Communal Knowledge and the Expulsion of the Other

**Problem:** Does the premise "knowledge to the community" lead to the homogenization of knowledge within the community?

**Analysis:** When Han claims that "transparent communication produces flattening and homogenization," he points to the paradox that the free circulation of information actually reduces diversity. Because information — regardless of its content — circulates through **formally identical channels of circulation (visualization, documentation, shareability)** .

**Core Tension:** **The knowledge commons, by its very mode of constitution, already privileges specific forms of knowledge.** This is, in Han's terms, how the 'society of the same' is formed. When only knowledge shareable by the community is recognized as knowledge — the knowledge that Michael Polanyi called "we know more than we can tell" — is systematically excluded from the status of knowledge.

### Layer 3: The Elimination of Hermeneutic Friction and the Loss of the Conditions of Thought

**Problem:** When the PLM Knowledge Bank aims for a 'smooth user experience (smooth UX),' does this conflict with Lee Yong-wook's own concept of 'hermeneutic friction'?

**Analysis:** This layer reveals the most significant theoretical tension. Lee argues through the concept of 'hermeneutic friction' that thought arises at the moment the medium resists the human. This aligns remarkably with Han's assertion that negativity (resistance, distance, delay) is the condition of thought and eros.

But when the PLM Knowledge Bank is designed as a platform, it inevitably follows the logic of **friction minimization**: immediate access to desired knowledge, algorithmically optimized search and recommendation, intuitive and smooth user experience, elimination of unnecessary resistance or delay. All these design principles aim at the **systematic elimination of hermeneutic friction**.

**Core Tension:** If the UX design of the PLM Knowledge Bank prioritizes 'immediate accessibility' and 'smooth flow,' it becomes a **design that negates Lee's own theoretical starting point** (that hermeneutic friction generates thought). The dilemma: *Can hermeneutic friction be intentionally designed into a platform? Or can friction exist only as a 'bug' of the platform?*

### Layer 4: The Problem of Temporality — Delay, Ripening, and Silence

**Problem:** When the knowledge bank presupposes 'real-timeness' or 'synchronized updating,' what temporal violence does it exercise?

**Analysis:** For Han, the transparent society imposes a specific temporality — the temporality of the **accelerated present (akzelerierte Gegenwart)** . Everything must be available 'here and now,' and time for delay or ripening is systematically eliminated.

**Core Tension:** The PLM Knowledge Bank's update mechanism inevitably enforces the **synchronization of knowledge** — the norm that the 'version' of knowledge within the community must be maintained consistently. This is precisely the implementation of what Han calls "the synchronizing and homogenizing effect of transparent communication."

### Layer 5: The Knowledge Bank as a Control Society — Connecting Foucault and Deleuze

**Problem:** Although the PLM Knowledge Bank professes 'free circulation of knowledge,' is its mode of operation structurally analogous to the Foucauldian panopticon or the Deleuzian control society?

**Analysis:** Han connects Foucault and Deleuze directly in the final chapter of *The Transparency Society*: **Transparency is a form of control that operates not through external prohibition but through internal self-driving.** The positivity command "You can" replaces the negativity command "You must."

The PLM Knowledge Bank's 'voluntary contribution' model appears free on the surface but can function in depth as a control apparatus producing a specific knowledge-subject. The user has the 'freedom' not to share knowledge, but exercising that freedom results in invisibilization within the platform.

## V. Synthesis: Conditions Under Which the PLM Knowledge Bank Becomes a Transparency Apparatus

### 5.1 Diagnostic Conditions Matrix

| Design Dimension | Condition for Transparency Apparatus Conversion | Alternative Design Principle for Negativity Preservation |
|-----------------|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **Fluidity** | Premise that all knowledge must flow → exclusion of non-circulatability | Spatial allowance for non-flowing knowledge (incomplete, unshared, silent) |
| **Hermeneutics** | Friction-minimized UX → loss of thought conditions | Intentional inclusion of delay, strangeness, resistance in design |
| **Community** | Recognition of visible contributions only → expulsion of invisible knowledge | Evaluation system that acknowledges non-contribution, observation, ripening time |
| **Temporality** | Synchronized update requirement → suppression of asynchronous understanding | Institutionalization of latency, indeterminacy, incompletion |
| **Subjectivation** | Contribution-reward cycle → production of neoliberal knowledge worker | Space for 'purpose-free engagement' (zweckfreier Umgang) beyond use |

### 5.2 The Paradox of Hermeneutic Friction — A Problem the Platform Cannot Solve

The most important conclusion of this analysis: **Hermeneutic friction cannot fundamentally be designed.**

If friction is intended as part of platform design, it becomes 'controlled friction' — another form of positivity. Genuine hermeneutic friction occurs only as a **non-intentional event**. It is possible only through system malfunction, unexpected resistance, or the intervention of an uncomprehended other. Yet a platform is essentially a device that systematizes these exceptions.

Thus: **The more perfectly the PLM Knowledge Bank functions, the more effectively it eliminates hermeneutic friction, and therefore the more thoroughly it dismantles the conditions of thought.** This is the paradoxical point where Lee's Artificial Nature theory and Han's transparency critique meet: **The more perfect the artificial nature, the more difficult thought becomes within it.**

### 5.3 The Duality of Knowledge Commons — Between Liberation and Control

"Data to the individual, knowledge to the community" has obvious emancipatory potential — an intention to prevent the commodification of personal data and make knowledge a communal resource. But this analysis shows that **any positive value, when systematized, can be converted into violence against negativity.**

> "Within transparency, freedom and control no longer oppose each other. Rather, transparency makes freedom feel like control, and control feel like freedom."

---

## VI. Conclusion: Minimum Conditions for Preserving Negativity

**1. Guarantee of the Right to Non-Circulation (Recht auf Nicht-Zirkulation)**
The premise that all knowledge must circulate must be made explicit, and the right not to circulate — the right to non-sharing, concealment, silence — must be institutionalized as a basic right of the platform.

**2. Intentional De-Optimization**
Rather than making UX 'smoothness' the highest value, experiences of intentional delay, complexity, and strangeness should be included in design. This requires a fundamental re-examination of the value of 'user-friendliness.'

**3. Institutionalized Asynchrony**
The premise that all knowledge should be on the same temporal axis must be deconstructed. An asynchronous structure must allow different speeds of understanding, ripening times, and periods of silence to coexist.

**4. Preservation of Indeterminacy**
The knowledge bank should be able to store questions rather than answers, indeterminacy rather than decisions, incompletion rather than completion. If the system records only the state of 'knowing' (wissen), the productive state of 'not-knowing' (nicht-wissen) is lost.

**5. Space for the Face of the Other**
In Levinas's terms, the PLM Knowledge Bank must secure space for the face of the other that cannot be reduced to the 'same' (das Selbe) of knowledge — heterogeneity that cannot be fully captured by the system's code. This means the platform must include **non-capturability** as a design principle.

---

This analysis is a transcendental examination of the PLM Knowledge Bank's design — not a critique of specific technical implementations but a **theoretical identification of the tensions and risks implicit in the platform's ontological premises**. These tensions are not 'bugs' to be fixed but a **philosophical condition (conditio philosophica)** to be continually reflected upon in the design process.
