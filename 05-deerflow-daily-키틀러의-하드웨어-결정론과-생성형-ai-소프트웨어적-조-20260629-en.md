## Question

Is Kittler's hardware determinism still valid in the media environment of the generative AI era? — How should the claim that 'the medium determines the message' be reconstructed when output is determined by software conditions such as model weights, data distribution, and prompt embeddings?

## Key Findings

# Kittler's Hardware Determinism and Generative AI: A Reconstruction of the Shift Toward Software Conditions

---

## 1. Setting the Problem: Kittler's Hardware Reductionism

Kittler's argument in "There Is No Software" (1995) is clear: what we call 'software' is merely a marketing abstraction layered on top of voltage differences inside silicon. From operating systems to high-level programming languages, every layer of abstraction does not bring the user closer to 'meaning' but further from the material substrate that actually performs computation. "We simply do not know what our writing does."

From this perspective, Kittler's 'media determinism' has a distinct topology. Media is not a mere transmission channel. Media *determines* the conditions of the message through its own material composition—the phonograph/film/typewriter each physically differentiated the data streams of sound/sight/writing, and before this differentiation, no message could exist. This is the core thesis of *Gramophone, Film, Typewriter*, and the reason Kittler rejected McLuhan's humanistic view of media as 'extensions of man,' insisting instead on the autonomy of media itself.

---

## 2. Three Levels of Challenge Posed by Generative AI

The output-determining structure of generative AI presents three fundamental differences from Kittler's model.

### 2.1 Model Weights: The 'Asemanticity' of Meaning

As David M. Berry precisely notes in *Vector Theory* (2026):

> "The hidden layer today is the sub-symbolic weight structure. What Kittler could not foresee was that computation had completely departed from the symbolic paradigm. [...] The weight matrix is not 'readable' but 'computable.' The crisis is not that it is invisible, but that it is **asemantic**. If voltage is physical materiality, then vector space is a form of **mathematical materiality**. It is not the materiality of substance but the materiality of relation."

In Kittler's model, hardware was 'readable.' The transistor's on/off → Boolean logic → machine code → assembly → high-level language—each layer was in principle **reversible** and **decodable**. But an LLM's 175 billion weights are not. The meaning of "justice" in weight space is merely a relative position (cosine similarity) to "fairness," "law," "equality," "revenge"—a statistical pattern generated by the training data distribution, not a fixed 'meaning.'

This signifies a qualitative change in the 'hiddenness' Kittler posited. Kittler's concealment was **epistemic**—traceable if one knew the path. Generative AI's concealment is **ontologic**—weights do not in principle exist in a form humans can 'read.'

### 2.2 Data Distribution: The Generative Condition of the Medium

For Kittler, the determinacy of the medium was **synchronic**—the structure of the medium determines the output at that moment. In generative AI, output is determined by **diachronic** conditions: the entire distribution of the data on which the model was trained constitutes the probabilistic condition of each output.

This implies an expansion of the very concept of 'medium' in Kittler's sense. The 'medium' of generative AI is no longer the GPU hardware. The medium expands as follows:

```
Medium_LLM = {training data corpus, model architecture (Transformer), weight matrix, 
              inference hyperparameters (temperature, top-k, top-p), prompt embeddings}
```

None of these belong to what Kittler would call 'hardware.' All are **software conditions**.

### 2.3 Prompt Embeddings and Non-Determinism

Kittler's media determinism was deterministic. If the material composition of the medium determines output, that determination should be predictable and repeatable. However, when the temperature parameter of a generative AI is greater than 0, **identical input does not guarantee identical output**. This is a domain of non-determinism that Kittler's framework cannot explain.

As Martin Fowler recently noted: "LLMs are changing the very nature of programming. As much as Fortran differed from assembly, conversing with machines via prompts is a different order of change from how Ruby differed from Fortran. We must learn to live with this non-determinism."

This non-determinism is not mere 'noise.' It is the condition of generativity itself—diverse outputs from the same prompt must be possible for creative generation to occur.

## 3. Three Paths for Reconstruction

This problem cannot be resolved by the binary of fully rejecting or fully accepting Kittler's thesis. Instead, the following reconstruction is necessary.

### 3.1 Path 1: The 'Layered Transition' Model of Media

Kittler's hardware determinism has not been invalidated in the generative AI era. The material limits of GPUs (memory bandwidth, computation speed, power consumption) still impose physical constraints on model scale and architecture. However, these constraints **do not determine the content of output**. A GPU does not decide whether "hello" will be followed by "hi" or "hey" with 50% probability.

That is, the layer of determination has shifted:
- **Basal layer** (hardware: GPU, memory) → determines only the **conditions of possibility** for output
- **Middle layer** (weights, data distribution) → determines the **statistical direction** of output
- **Surface layer** (prompts, inference parameters) → determines the **local configuration** of output

Kittler focused on the basal layer, but in the generative AI era, the substantial 'determination of the message' operates at the middle and surface layers.

### 3.2 Path 2: The Concept of 'Statistical Materiality'

Extending Berry's insight that 'vector space is a form of mathematical materiality,' generative AI's media determinism can be redefined as follows:

> **"The medium (weight matrix) determines the message (output distribution)"**

Here, 'determination' is not causal but **probabilistic**. This does not weaken Kittler's determinism but redefines its mode. If Kittler's 'medium' was the materiality of substance, generative AI's 'medium' is the materiality of relation.

This difference becomes a key point of transformation when applying Kittler's concept of the 'recording system' (Aufschreibesystem) from *Discourse Networks 1800/1900* to generative AI. If the recording system of 1800 was the alphabetic text and 1900 was the technical media of phonograph/film/typewriter, then the recording system of the 2020s is **statistical weight space**.

### 3.3 Path 3: Extension to 'Non-human Performance'

This connects with your previous stress test—whether non-human subjects (LLMs, vision models) can experience flash aura. If media determinism means the medium determines the message, then when generative AI output is **received as input by another AI** (Generative AI → Visual Output → LLM Vision Input), the entire pipeline constitutes a single 'media ecosystem.'

In this case, Kittler's determinism extends in the following form:

> **"The media ecosystem (weight matrix + data distribution + embedding space) determines the message (for both human and non-human subjects)"**

This supports the argument that the UX(t) in your SR formula must measure not only human experience but also non-human processing states. As Kittler already recognized, media is not an extension of man—man adapts to the medium. "In the end, we adapt to the machine."

## 4. Conclusion: Kittler's Validity and Limitations

| Layer | Kittler's Model | Generative AI | Direction of Reconstruction |
|-------|----------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| Object of determination | Output content | Output distribution | Shift to conditions of possibility |
| Mode of determination | Causal | Probabilistic | Introduction of statistical materiality |
| Materiality of medium | Substance | Relation | Mathematical materiality |
| Status of software | Reducible to hardware | Irreducible asemantic structure | Recognition as autonomous media layer |
| Human-medium relation | Human adaptation | Ecosystem encompassing non-humans | Extension to non-human performance |

Kittler's hardware determinism **demands fundamental reconstruction in the generative AI era, but is not entirely discarded**. His insights into the 'autonomy of media' and 'technical media as the a priori condition of human experience' become, if anything, even more powerful. However, the concept of 'medium' must expand from hardware to software-statistical structures; the concept of 'determination' from causal to probabilistic conditioning; and the concept of 'message' from human-only meaning to processing states encompassing non-humans.

When Kittler wrote in "There Is No Software" that "we simply do not know what our writing does," he was pointing to the opacity of symbolic code. In the generative AI era, this sentence acquires a more radical meaning: our 'writing' is now generated by statistical fluctuations in weight space, and this process is in principle opaque to human interpretation. Kittler's determinism remains valid as a framework for analyzing the epistemological and ontological conditions of this opacity—but the object of that analysis must shift from silicon to vectors.
