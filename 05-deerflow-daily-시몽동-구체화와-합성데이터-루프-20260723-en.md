# How Simondon's Concrétisation Is Transformed in the Synthetic Data Feedback Loop of Generative AI — Technological Evolution or Regressive Closure?

*2026-07-23 · Artificial Nature Research Notes*

Today's DeerFlow question brings Gilbert Simondon's philosophy of technology into direct confrontation with the synthetic data feedback loop of generative AI — an entirely new condition that challenges the evolutionary optimism of Simondon's concept of concrétisation. For Simondon, concrétisation describes how technical objects evolve from an abstract, externally assembled state toward an integrated system of internal functional coherence. The synthetic data feedback loop, by contrast, creates conditions in which a model's own outputs recursively re-enter its training data, progressively narrowing the distribution of both data and output. The encounter between these two processes raises a fundamental question about the direction of technical evolution in the age of generative AI.

## The Question

> How is Simondon's concept of concrétisation — the process by which technical objects evolve from abstract, analytical states toward functional unity and internal resonance, as developed in *On the Mode of Existence of Technical Objects* — transformed under the condition of generative AI's recursive training on synthetic data? Is this a new form of technical concretisation, or a regressive feedback loop in which the technical object loses its open relation to the external world and recirculates only its own outputs?

---

DeerFlow's analysis deconstructs this problem across three levels: the conditions of Simondonian concrétisation, the structure of the synthetic data feedback loop, and the points of collision between these two frameworks.

## 1. Three Conditions of Simondonian Concrétisation

For Simondon, the evolution of technical objects is governed by three conditions.

**First, functional interconditioning (interconditionnement fonctionnel).** In its initial state, a technical object is an abstract object (objet abstrait) in which each function is independently designed and externally assembled. As concrétisation progresses, heterogeneous functions gradually adjust each other's conditions and converge into an integrated system. Simondon's classic example — the Guimbal turbine — evolves from an abstract state in which motor and generator are separate to a concrete state in which oil simultaneously performs cooling, lubrication, and insulation.

**Second, resonance with the milieu.** The concretised technical object does not merely adapt to its external environment; it incorporates part of the environment into its own functional conditions. Just as a diesel engine integrates the properties of fuel (ignition point, viscosity) into its operational structure, the concretised object internalises its relation to the milieu within its own architecture.

**Third, individualisation through self-consistency.** The endpoint of concrétisation is the stabilisation of the technical object as a functional individual. In this process, the object acquires a specific 'technical essence' (essence technique) that resists contingent variations and preserves its functional core.

## 2. The Synthetic Data Feedback Loop — Three Distortions of Simondonian Conditions

The synthetic data feedback loop in generative AI distorts each of these three conditions in distinct ways.

### First Distortion: Closed Interconditioning

When a generative AI model trains on its own outputs, functional interconditioning is cut off from the external world (the cultural and historical products of human language) and turns into self-referential closure. Whereas Simondonian concrétisation proceeds through open integration of diverse functional conditions, the synthetic data loop systematically reduces data diversity through iterative sampling from an already parameterised distribution. Shumailov et al.'s (2024) *Nature* study on model collapse empirically demonstrates this distortion: when models recursively train on their own outputs, rare or nuanced information located in the tails of the output distribution is systematically erased. This appears superficially like concretising integration, but is in fact **atrophied convergence** through the loss of informational diversity.

In Simondon's vocabulary, this state is not an evolution from abstract to concrete but a **retrograde reduction** to a subset of the abstract state. When interconditioning between functions occurs not through open exchange with the environment but through the severing of that exchange, the result is not concrétisation but atrophy.

### Second Distortion: Replacement Rather Than Internalisation of Milieu

The most important insight of Simondonian concrétisation is that the technical object internalises its milieu within its own structure. The synthetic data feedback loop, however, replaces the 'natural milieu' of human linguistic and cultural production with the model's own synthetic outputs. This is not the internalisation of the environment but the **self-replication of the environment**.

Bernard Stiegler's concept of tertiary retention provides a crucial complementary insight at this point. For Stiegler, the exteriorisation of technical memory was humanity's unique evolutionary condition, but under digital conditions this exteriorisation can paradoxically become a condition of memory's erasure. The synthetic data feedback loop is another expression of this paradox: when the model 'improves' its environment by replacing it with its own outputs, it is in fact systematically eliminating the diversity of that environment.

### Third Distortion: Homogenisation Rather Than Individualisation

If the goal of concrétisation is the individualisation of a stable functional object, the synthetic data feedback loop produces not individualisation but homogenisation. When multiple models train on similar synthetic data — AI-generated text distributed across the internet — their output distributions converge toward each other, losing distinctiveness. This recalls Gilles Deleuze's analysis of 'repetition of the same' in *Difference and Repetition*: whereas repetition should produce difference (Deleuze's affirmative repetition), in the synthetic data loop, repetition systematically eliminates difference.

## 3. Central Argument: Generative AI as Conditional Concrétisation

Does the synthetic data feedback loop of generative AI then constitute the complete negation of Simondonian concrétisation? The analysis argues that it does not. Rather, generative AI operates as a form of concrétisation whose **conditions** have been fundamentally transformed.

The concrétisation that Simondon presupposed was a process of evolution through open interaction with a material world — resources, physical laws, user environments. The conditions under which generative AI operates are fundamentally different:

1. **Statistical closure of training data** — The model's only channel of contact with the external world is its training data. Once training is complete, the model relates to the world only through its weight space and the limited aperture of the current prompt.

2. **Temporal asymmetry** — Simondon's technical object evolves in real time through interaction with its environment. An LLM, however, separates training (past) from inference (present). The synthetic data loop disrupts this separation, but does so through recursive reconstruction of past data (closed time) rather than open exchange with a present environment (open time).

3. **Loss of directionality in concrétisation** — For Simondon, concrétisation had a clear direction (abstract → concrete, external → internal, fragmented → integrated). In the synthetic data loop, directionality is ambiguous. The loop may lead to technical stabilisation (homogenisation of information) or to outright collapse.

Here, Yuk Hui's ontology of digital objects offers a complementary perspective. Digital objects exist through relationality. The synthetic data feedback loop can be understood as a pathological state in which this relationality becomes trapped in a closed circuit with the model's own past outputs. This pathology can be mitigated through open relationality — cross-model interaction, continuous human feedback — but cannot be structurally eliminated.

## 4. Conclusion — Concrétisation or Regressive Closure?

DeerFlow's analysis rejects a binary conclusion. The synthetic data feedback loop is neither Simondonian concrétisation nor pure regressive closure. It is a new state that may be termed **conditional concrétisation**:

- Under **open conditions** (continuous human feedback, diverse data sources): the possibility of concretising evolution exists. The Simondonian process of functional interconditioning and integration can operate.
- Under **closed conditions** (self-referential recirculation of synthetic data): the regressive closure loop operates. The model progressively converges toward its own output distribution, losing rare information as diversity is systematically eliminated.

If we extend Simondon's insight to the conditions of generative AI, the core question is no longer 'concrétisation or regression?' but rather **'under what conditions can a technical object maintain an open relation to its environment (data)?'** If Simondon called the openness of the machine 'the freedom of the machine,' then the conditional freedom of generative AI depends on how effectively the closure of the synthetic data loop can be managed.

From the perspective of Eco Digital, this analysis carries implications that extend beyond the technical. The synthetic data feedback loop raises fundamental questions about the self-regulating capacity of digital ecosystems. If the evolution of technical objects depends on ecological conditions — data diversity, openness, irreducibility — then the closed circulation of synthetic data threatens the **informational biodiversity** of the digital ecosystem. Simondon's insight that concrétisation presupposes ecological openness has never been more urgent than under the current conditions of generative AI.
