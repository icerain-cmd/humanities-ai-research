# Daily DeerFlow Analysis: 2026-07-08

> Exploring the structural isomorphism between Han Byung-Chul's 'pornography of self-identity' and the statistical repetition of generative AI images

## Question

In *The Agony of Eros*, Han Byung-Chul diagnoses that the neoliberal achievement society destroys Eros by eliminating alterity, leaving only pornographic self-exhibition trapped in the circulation of self-identity. A similar circulation of self-identity can be observed in how generative AI images occupy the public's visual unconscious — the 'AI aesthetic' produced by Midjourney (soft lighting, idealized figures, overly balanced composition) always repeats already-known aesthetic patterns to satisfy user expectations. Is the statistical nature of AI image generation — predicting the most probable next pixel — structurally isomorphic to what Han calls the 'pornography of self-identity'? That is, is AI a medium that systematically eliminates visual Eros (strangeness, shock, incomprehensibility) by repeating only the predictable statistics of training data without any encounter with alterity?

## Key Findings

### 1. Introduction: Structure of the Question and Direction of Analysis

The core of the question lies in the thesis of **structural isomorphism**: does the 'pornography' diagnosed by Han in the neoliberal achievement society — a state where the alterity of the Other is eliminated and only the circulation of self-identity remains — share the same ontological structure as the statistical generation mechanism of AI images?

This analysis does not aim at 'case verification' but at the **generation of conceptual tension**. Rather than simply 'applying' Han's framework to AI, it explores new problematics emerging at the contact surface between two domains.

### 2. Han's Diagnosis: The Death of Eros and Pornographic Self-Identity

Han's argument can be reconstructed across three levels:

**① Ontological level: The dialectic of the negation of the Other**
Eros operates through Hegel's negative dialectic. The Other is experienced as a fundamental strangeness (das Fremde) that cannot be fully subsumed by the 'I'. This strangeness is a shock and a wound that possesses the power to dismantle and reconstitute the subject. In *The Agony of Eros*, Han cites Plato's *Symposium* to emphasize that Eros arises from 'lack' — an unfillable gap that opens the subject toward the Other.

**② Socio-structural level: The achievement society (Leistungsgesellschaft)**
The neoliberal achievement society is not a society of 'duty' but of 'can'. It systematically eliminates negativity (Negativität) — otherness, resistance, obstacle — and replaces everything with positivity (Positivität) — availability, accessibility, efficiency. In this society, the Other is no longer an object of encounter but an **object of consumption and optimization**.

**③ Aesthetic level: Pornography**
For Han, pornography is not the 'exposure of nudity'. Pornography is **an operation that eliminates the alterity of the Other itself**. In pornographic images, the body is not a site of otherness but a showroom of self-identity. Everything is visible, everything is accessible, no concealment or mystery exists. This is the aesthetic expression of what Han calls the 'transparency society'.

> "In pornography, the Other is thoroughly objectified and consumed. Pornography does not show the nudity of the sexual object; through that nudity, it eliminates the alterity of the Other itself." (*The Agony of Eros*)

The conclusion across these three levels: neoliberal digital capitalism replaces **encounter** with **consumption**, **alterity** with **identity**, and **Eros** with **pornography**.

### 3. The Ontological Condition of Generative AI Images

Analyzing the ontological condition of generative AI image models (particularly diffusion models like Midjourney, DALL-E, and Stable Diffusion):

#### 3.1. Statistical nature: The repetition of probability distributions
Generative AI models the probability distribution of training data. The image generation process is a chain of conditional probabilities predicting the most probable next token/pixel. The diffusion process of restoring an image from noise is a regression toward the 'mean' of the training data's statistical patterns.

#### 3.2. Satisfaction of user expectations: The predictability of prompts
When a user inputs "beautiful woman, soft lighting, cinematic", the model outputs the combination of visual patterns with the **highest conditional probability** in the training data. This output is essentially **regressive** — it confirms the aesthetic expectations the user already has.

#### 3.3. Formation of 'AI aesthetics': Statistical normativity
Over time, repeated generation patterns form a new normative aesthetic system — 'AI aesthetics': soft lighting, idealized facial proportions, overly balanced composition, specific color palettes. This is a state where **statistical average becomes the norm**. Rare outliers are systematically suppressed, and only the most probable patterns are reproduced.

### 4. Examining the Isomorphism Thesis

#### 4.1. Similarities: Three structural correspondences

| Han's Pornography | AI Image Generation | Structural Correspondence |
|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Elimination of the Other's alterity | Suppression of statistical outliers | Systematic exclusion of strangeness |
| Circulation of self-identity | Regression to probability distribution | Repetitive confirmation of identity |
| Transparency (no concealment) | Complete visibility of conditional probabilities | Aesthetics of predictability |
| Consumptive objectification | Satisfaction of user expectations | Closed circuit of self-confirmation |

These three correspondences support a **partial isomorphism**. AI image generation possesses a mechanism that systematically suppresses images-as-Other — unpredictable images that resist existing aesthetic categories and deliver shock — which resembles, in its mode of operation, the 'elimination of alterity' that Han identifies as the essence of pornography.

#### 4.2. Differences: Pornography is the death of Eros, AI is the a priori blocking of Eros

However, important differences exist:

**① The problem of intentionality**
Han's pornography presupposes an intentional act of 'reducing' alterity to a consumable object. AI image generation has no such intentionality. AI merely repeats statistical patterns; the suppression of alterity is **a consequence of the mechanism, not an intention**. This difference creates an ontological distinction, not merely an ethical one. Pornography is the 'death' of Eros, but AI generation is a condition where Eros 'cannot arise in the first place'.

**② The location of the subject**
In Han's diagnosis, the subject of pornography is the human subject who objectifies the Other. In AI image generation, the subject is split: the human who trains the model (data engineer, platform owner), the user who inputs prompts, and the model itself (non-human actor). This 'split subjectivity' is a new condition that Han's framework fails to capture.

**③ The possibility of flight (pre-existing noise)**
It is significant that the diffusion model recovers images from noise. The 'starting point' of generation is pure noise. This noise introduces uncontrolled micro-variations (random seed, contingency in latent space) into the generation process. Theoretically, this noise implies the possibility of the Other's intrusion. In other words, AI image generation is **not completely trapped in self-identity** — but in reality, this possibility is systematically suppressed by platform filters, community norms, and user expectations.

#### 4.3. Interim judgment

AI image generation is not fully isomorphic to Han's 'pornography' in the strict sense, but possesses a **'pornographic tendency'** operating in the same direction. This is not structural identity but **tendential similarity**. AI does not 'kill' alterity; it creates conditions where alterity cannot arise in the first place. This may be a more severe diagnosis — not the death of Eros, but the congenital impossibility of Eros.

### 5. Philosophical Context Expansion

#### 5.1. Deleuze & Guattari: BwO and desiring-machines

If Han diagnoses pornography as a closed circuit of self-identity, Deleuze and Guattari's *A Thousand Plateaus* and *Anti-Oedipus* enable a different analysis.

**Desiring-production and machinic connection**
For Deleuze and Guattari, desire is not 'lack' but 'production'. The desiring-machine constantly connects with other machines, producing new reality through these connections. Viewing AI image generation models as 'desiring-machines', the question is not that AI 'repeats self-identity', but **what kind of desire AI produces**.

**BwO (Body without Organs) and de-territorialization**
AI's latent space can be seen as a BwO — a plane of intensity where all possible images potentially exist but are not yet actualized. AI generation is a process of 'actualizing' specific images from this BwO. The question is **how emergent this actualization is**, i.e., how de-territorializing it is.

From the Deleuze-Guattarian perspective, the 'pornographic self-identity' diagnosed by Han can be reinterpreted as **excessive territorialization of machinic desire-production** — a state where flows of desire are captured and fixed by capitalist codes (market, consumption, normative aesthetics). The problem is not the ontology of AI itself, but the **socio-economic conditions** in which AI operates.

This provides an important analytic pivot: **the 'pornographic' character of AI image generation is not a consequence of the technical mechanism (statistical prediction), but of the way technology is deployed within capitalist relations.** The possibility of the same AI technology operating as a de-territorializing machine of desire remains theoretically open.

#### 5.2. Stiegler: Symbolic misery and the destruction of industrial memory

Stiegler's argument in *Technics and Time* and *Symbolic Misery* mediates the connection between Han and generative AI more deeply.

**Tertiary memory and exteriorization**
For Stiegler, technology is the 'exteriorization of memory'. Humans are essentially technical beings (epiphylogenetic), mediating ontogenesis and phylogenesis through technical memory (tertiary memory). The training data of AI image generation models = the entire visual culture of humanity externalized as one vast tertiary memory.

**Destruction of industrial memory and symbolic misery**
The problem arises when this tertiary memory is **industrially controlled**. Stiegler called the phenomenon where the culture industry destroys sensory-symbolic diversity and enforces the circulation of standardized affect 'symbolic misery'. Generative AI images represent a new stage of this symbolic misery:

- **Previous stage (Stiegler)**: Limited signs and narratives selected and edited by the culture industry occupy the public imagination.
- **AI stage**: The generative mechanism that regresses to the 'statistical average' of the entire training dataset produces **statistical normativity itself**, beyond selection and editing.

Extending Stiegler's framework, AI image generation is the most sophisticated form of the 'industrial destruction of memory'. The entire visual culture of humanity is databased, and the statistical average of that database is presented as a new aesthetic norm. In this process, rare patterns, strange compositions, incomprehensible images — i.e., visual alterity — are treated as statistical noise and systematically suppressed.

### 6. Conclusion: The generation of conceptual tension

**First, AI is not pornography but the 'a priori condition' of pornography.** Han's pornography still presupposes 'death' — the death of a once-living Eros. In the world of AI image generation, Eros **could never arise in the first place.** This is a more fundamental diagnosis. AI does not kill Eros; it blocks the ontological condition under which Eros can emerge.

**Second, this blocking is not a technological necessity but a political choice.** From Deleuze and Guattari's perspective, AI's statistical repetition is not the essence of technology but the result of how AI is deployed within capitalist relations. AI's latent space, as a BwO, always contains the possibility of de-territorialization. The question is the **socio-economic conditions** under which this possibility is actualized.

**Third, Stiegler's therapeutic vision — 'symbolic abundance' and the 'aesthetic of practice' as resistance to symbolic misery — remains valid for AI image generation.** If AI images are not simply consumed but their conditions of generation are transformed and used differently, the pornographic circuit can be broken.

**Conclusive problematization:**
The answer to whether generative AI images systematically eliminate visual Eros (strangeness, shock, incomprehensibility) is **'technically yes, but politically no'**. AI's statistical nature operates to suppress Eros, but this direction is not a technological necessity but a **product of capitalist deployment**. The real question must shift from 'Is AI pornographic?' to **'What are the conditions for de-pornographizing AI?'**
