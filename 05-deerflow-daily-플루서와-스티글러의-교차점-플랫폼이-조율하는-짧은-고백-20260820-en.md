## 2. In-Depth Analysis

### 2.1. Flusserian Apparatus and Program — The Formal Conditions of TikTok/Shorts

Vilém Flusser defines the apparatus not as a mere tool but as a **black box that automates human intentions and converts them into predictable outputs**. As with the camera, the apparatus accepts the human gesture of pressing a button as input, but its internal operations belong to the realm of a program that humans can no longer control. Crucially, Flusser's concept of 'program' is not simply software code but **a system of formal conditions that makes certain things possible while rendering others impossible**.

TikTok and YouTube Shorts' auto-captions, emotion filters, and recommended sounds are typical implementations of this Flusserian apparatus/program concept:

- **Auto-captions**: Not a simple technology that converts speaking into text. By reducing the rhythm, stress, intonation, and silence of speech to standardized text, it **eliminates the concrete sensory texture of confession**. At the same time, this text is transformed into a scrollable information object, dividing the viewer's attention.
- **Emotion filters**: They classify facial expressions into specific emotion categories (sadness, joy, anger) and overlay corresponding visual effects. This is not the typology of emotions that Spinoza warned against in the *Ethics*, but rather **an abstraction into algorithmically processable emotional data**.
- **Recommended sounds**: These are programmatic options that have already determined which music suits which confession scene. While it is not physically impossible to choose a fast-tempo funk track as BGM for a 'sad confession', the platform's recommendation structure 'naturally' excludes that choice.

Here, Flusser's key insight operates. Even at the moment when humans believe they are 'doing something', the apparatus already makes that action possible only within the categories of the program. Each gesture of the creator — turning on auto-captions, applying an emotion filter, placing a recommended sound — is, regardless of the subjective conviction of "expressing my experience," **an act of repeatedly performing the apparatus's program**.

### 2.2. Stiegler's Exteriorization of Memory and the Attention Industry

Bernard Stiegler analyzes technology as **exteriorization of memory**, i.e., as prosthesis (*prothèse*). Specifically, the concept of tertiary retention — memory that is neither individual memory nor the genetic memory of the species, but memory exteriorized in technical objects — is the decisive key to understanding this issue.

Viewing the production process of TikTok/Shorts confession videos through Stiegler's framework reveals the following multi-layered exteriorization:

1. **Primary exteriorization**: The creator's experience (memory) is exteriorized as a technical object — the video. This is the surface content of the confession video.
2. **Secondary exteriorization**: The conditions for *how* that confession should be exteriorized (length in seconds, caption style, filter type) are already exteriorized in the platform's interface.
3. **Tertiary exteriorization**: Data showing that a particular confession format received 'good responses' accumulates through the algorithm's feedback loop back into the platform's recommendation structure. This is a cycle where exteriorized memory updates the conditions for further exteriorization.

Stiegler's discussion in *The Industrialization of Attention* (2010), following *Technics and Time*, intervenes more directly here. The entire system — where auto-captions fix the viewer's eyes to a specific location, emotion filters pre-direct the viewer's emotional response, and recommended sounds set the mood — is not a mere 'tool for expression' but a **mechanism that industrially organizes both attention and memory simultaneously**.

The more the creator feels they are 'authentically' confessing their experience, the more that confession operates within an attention-memory circuit already pre-designed by the platform. To borrow Stiegler's phrase, this is **the moment when individual confession is captured by industrial temporality**.

### 2.3. Flusser × Stiegler — The Intersection of the Two Theories

Although the two theories stand in different traditions (phenomenology/media philosophy vs. philosophy of technology/epistemology), they intersect at the following points when analyzing the production process of TikTok/Shorts confession videos:

| Layer | Flusserian Reading | Stieglerian Reading | Intersection Effect |
|-------|--------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Auto-captions** | Apparatus reduces sensory texture of speech to programmed text | Tertiary retention replaces oral memory with technical object | Bodily and sensory dimensions of experience are converted into formal data |
| **Emotion filters** | Apparatus's program determines visible categories of emotion | Attention industry organizes emotional response as predictable commodity | 'True emotion' is algorithmically produced |
| **Recommended sounds** | Apparatus pre-narrows options, reducing creator's decision to function | Exteriorized memory (data) reorganizes conditions for new memory production | Freedom of choice is reconstituted as repetition of program |

The most important insight at this intersection is that **both theories point to the fact that human intentionality is pre-organized by technical conditions**. If Flusser sees the apparatus's program reducing human creativity to a 'function' (*Funktion*), Stiegler sees tertiary retention as reconstructing the very conditions of personal memory and identity. The TikTok/Shorts confession video is a concrete case where these two processes operate superimposed within a single interface.

### 2.4. Relocation of Authorship — Individual Intention or Iterative Tuning of Apparatus-User?

Here we reach the core of the question. **If even the moment when users believe they are expressing their own experience involves repeatedly performing the platform's formal options, where does the authorship of that video lie?**

The traditional concept of authorship (the 'author-God' criticized by Roland Barthes, where individual inner intention is transferred to external expression) cannot explain this problem. Following Flusser's framework, authorship is no longer attributable to a human subject. Humans are closer to **functional nodes** for the operation of the apparatus's program. However, Flusser does not fall into simple determinism because he thought more subtly about the relocation of authorship through the concept of **mutual tuning** between apparatus and human.

The analytical framework I propose is as follows. Authorship is a **position generated within a triadic cycle of apparatus (platform's program) — user (content and gestures of confession) — viewer (feedback through data)**. This should be called not the absence or end of the traditional author, but the **distribution of agency**.

Specifically, authorship is relocated on three levels:

1. **Flusserian level**: The apparatus, within the illusion that humans believe they have 'created something', converts that act into a repetition of the program. The author becomes both an agent of the apparatus and an object of the apparatus.
2. **Stieglerian level**: Tertiary retention (the platform's accumulated data and interface) not only determines the form of the confession but also reconstructs the memory conditions of that confession. The author becomes a 'prosthetic being' who depends on an already exteriorized technical system for the conditions of memory production.
3. **Cyclical level**: Each formal element the creator chooses (caption style, filter, sound) is both a repetition of patterns chosen by previous creators and a variation that updates those patterns. Authorship is a **temporary stabilization point that emerges within this cycle of repetition and variation**.

**In conclusion**: The author neither remains in individual intention nor is completely absorbed by the apparatus. Authorship is a **processual entity that is newly generated at every moment within the iterative tuning of apparatus and user**. To borrow Flusser's expression, the author, while performing the 'function' of the apparatus, is also the site of a **minor creativity** that slightly pushes or bends the apparatus's program through that very performance. The problem is how persistently this minor creativity is captured and re-industrialized within the platform capitalist system.

### 2.5. Extension to Benjamin and Foucault

To reinforce this analysis, briefly linking the additional context provided by the question:

- **Benjamin's artwork in the age of mechanical reproduction**: TikTok/Shorts confession videos go beyond the loss of aura, revealing the **paradox that the authenticity of confession itself is produced by technical conditions**. If Benjamin saw that photographic reproduction destroys the aura of the original, here the original confession itself exists only within a reproducible format (captions, filters, sounds).
- **Foucault's apparatus and subjectivation**: Intersecting the disciplinary power of *Discipline and Punish* with the confession technology of *The History of Sexuality*, the TikTok/Shorts platform can be read as a **new apparatus where confession simultaneously performs discipline and voluntary subjectivation**. Each act of turning on auto-captions, choosing an emotion filter, and selecting a recommended sound is a performance that expresses oneself while also aligning oneself with the subject form desired by the platform.
