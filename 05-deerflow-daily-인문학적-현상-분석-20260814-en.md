# Generative AI and the 'Restoration' of Colonial Photographs — Analysis Through Four Theoretical Frameworks

## Introduction: Generation in the Name of 'Restoration'

The phenomenon where colonial-era black-and-white photographs are colorized and background-corrected by generative AI, then circulated as 'historically restored images' on online platforms, entails not merely technological progress but an **epistemological, media-theoretical, and ethical shift**. Analyzing this phenomenon through four theoretical axes—Benjamin's historical actualization, Foucault's evidentiary system, Kittler's media materiality, and Haraway's situated knowledge—reveals what 'restoration' conceals.

---

## 1. Benjamin's Dialectical Image or Closed Evidence?

### 1.1. The Possibility of Historical Actualization

In his *Theses on the Philosophy of History*, Benjamin viewed history not as reconstructing "the past as it actually was," but as an act of **flashing up the potential of the past in the present crisis (Jetztzeit)**. From this perspective, AI colorization of colonial photographs holds one possibility. In a case study on colorizing Indonesian independence war photographs, colorizers argue that they "reveal the past that had been deactivated in monotonous black and white in the Dutch archive database" [Tandfonline, 2024]. This can be seen as a **Benjaminian moment where the present political demand (postcolonial identity formation) newly illuminates past images**.

A similar movement is found in the digital colorization of Sámi photographs. Per Ivar Somby colorizes colonial photographs and shares them on social media, demonstrating a practice of "decolonial repatriation of colonial photographs produced by non-Sámi photographers" [Cambridge, 2024]. This resembles Benjamin's **tradition of the oppressed** summoning past images in the present struggle.

### 1.2. However: The Closing of the Evidentiary System

The problem lies in the specific operation of AI colorization. Generative AI **'generates' visual information that never existed** through probabilistic inference. According to a 2025 study, AI-based colorization tools account for approximately 42% of all digital restoration orders, automatically identifying and restoring "over 40 types of damage" using GANs and CNNs [Dataintelo, 2025]. What matters here is:

- The colors AI generates are **results of probabilistic inference**, not material traces of the original.
- This inference is based on **training data biased toward the WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) visual system** [Henrich et al., 2010; DeHerrera, 2025].
- When the output circulates as a 'restored historical image,' **the boundary between inference and generation is concealed**.

At this point, Benjamin's warning is invoked. What Benjamin cautioned against in *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* was that **reproduction technology destroys 'aura' while simultaneously creating new 'exhibition value'**. AI colorized images destroy the aura of the original photograph (the unique material trace of a historical moment) while creating a new aura of **sharper, more complete, more 'realistic' images**. In Benjamin's terms, this can function as exhibition value for a 'reactionary' historical narrative.

## 2. Foucault: The Closure of the Evidentiary System

### 2.1. The Law of the Archive and AI

For Foucault, the archive is "the law of what can be said." The colonial photograph archive was a system that regulated the conditions under which specific statements emerge, persist, and disappear. Generative AI colorization adds a new layer to this system:

- **What is recognized as evidence?** AI-'restored' images appear more 'realistic' than original black-and-white photos, risking higher status as evidence.
- **What organizes what is seen?** AI colorization algorithms operate according to specific visual rules (lighting direction, skin tone range, material texture), following **implicit norms of training data**.

### 2.2. The Politics of 'Evidence'

The debate surrounding colorization of Indonesian independence war photographs illustrates this point. Academic historians worry that "colorized photographs undermine their status as primary sources," while amateur colorizers argue they "provide a more authentic perception of the past than monotonous black and white" [Tandfonline, 2024]. This is precisely a contemporary version of the Foucauldian **truth game**—a power struggle over what constitutes valid evidence.

A more serious issue is that AI performs **closure of evidence**. The **incompleteness, deficiency, and interpretive space** of black-and-white photographs are also conditions for historical understanding. When AI fills this deficiency in the name of 'restoration,' the **visualization of power** that Foucault analyzed in *Discipline and Punish* enters a new phase. The black-and-white nature of colonial photographs was not merely a technical limitation but a **material condition of how colonial power 'recorded' the colonized**. When AI converts this into 'natural' colors, the visual conditions of colonial power are concealed, and a more 'realistic' postcolonial visual system is constructed instead.

## 3. Kittler: Material Traces and Their Erasure

### 3.1. The Materiality of Recording Media

In *Gramophone, Film, Typewriter*, Kittler defines photography as a medium that **"stores the physical effects of light."** Photography directly records **physical traces of the real** without symbolic mediation. This is what fundamentally distinguishes photography from writing. For Kittler, photography is a technology that stores and transmits "the realm of the dead," and its material traces are irreplaceable.

### 3.2. The Erasure of Materiality in AI Colorization

What AI colorization performs is precisely this **erasure and replacement of material traces**:

1. **The original's material traces (grain, damage, analog continuity of light and shadow) are treated as 'noise.'**
2. AI replaces this 'noise' with 'corrected' pixels through probabilistic inference.
3. The output generates a **purely calculated visual surface unrelated to the original's materiality**.

Applying Kittler's analytical framework, AI colorization is problematic because it **conceals media specificity**. Kittler argued that media separate different data flows (optical, acoustic, textual) and each has its own storage and processing conditions. AI colorization crosses this difference, **denying the material conditions of the black-and-white photograph medium (the characteristic of silver halide photography that records only light intensity)** .

## 4. Haraway: Situated Knowledge and the 'God Trick'

### 4.1. Reconstructing Objectivity

In *Situated Knowledges* (1988), Haraway criticizes traditional scientific objectivity as a 'god trick'—a strategy that **pretends to be a universal, neutral viewpoint while actually absolutizing a specific position (white, male, Western)** . Haraway demands **responsible objectivity of partial perspective** in response.

### 4.2. The 'God Trick' of AI Colorization

The images presented by AI colorization perform a classic 'god trick':

- **The label 'restored' blurs the boundary between what AI 'inferred' and what 'was in the original.'**
- **AI output is presented as neutral, objective 'historical fact,'** but actually reflects biases in specific training data.
- **Information about who, when, with what data, and for what purpose performed the colorization is concealed.**

Specific case: In AI restoration of Mexican-American family photos, AI performed 'correction' by **narrowing noses, lightening skin, and altering bone structure** [DeHerrera, 2025]. This is not mere error but a result of **Western aesthetic norms embedded in training data operating as 'normalcy.'** In this process, "subtle but essential markers of ancestral true heritage" are erased.

In Haraway's framework, the core issue is **accountability**. When AI colorized images circulate:
- **Who is responsible for the color choices in this image?**
- **How is the image's uncertainty indicated?**
- **Can the viewer know whether what they see is 'restoration' or 'generation'?**

## 5. Specific Criteria Distinguishing Interpretation from Manipulation

Synthesizing the four theoretical frameworks, the criteria distinguishing interpretation that sensuously reveals past deficiencies from manipulation that fabricates non-existent visual certainty are as follows.

### Criterion 1: Media Transparency — Kittlerian Criterion

| Category | Interpretation | Manipulation |
|---|---|---|
| Material traces | Original traces (grain, damage, light/shadow limits) are preserved or at least **their existence is indicated** | Original traces are treated as 'defects' and completely removed |
| Indication of inference | Color choices are **explicitly stated as inference** | Colors are presented as **recovery** of the original |
| Uncertainty | Uncertain areas or colors are **visually distinguishable** | All areas are presented as equally certain visual surfaces |

**Kittlerian question**: "Does this image reveal or conceal what medium recorded what under what conditions?"

### Criterion 2: Dialectical Openness — Benjaminian Criterion

| Category | Interpretation | Manipulation |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal gap | The image shows **the gap between past and present** — "This is the past, but a past we cannot fully know" | The image **fully actualizes** the past — "This is exactly how it was" |
| Possibility of shock | The image **interrupts and discomforts** familiar historical narratives | The image **smoothly reinforces** familiar historical narratives |
| Visibility of deficiency | The image reveals **what is unknown** | The image presents itself as **knowing everything** |

**Benjaminian question**: "Is this image a dialectical image that explodes the continuum of history, or an ornament that reinforces it?"

### Criterion 3: Positional Accountability — Harawayan Criterion

| Category | Interpretation | Manipulation |
|---|---|---|
| Declaration of viewpoint | The **basis and source** of colorization are specified (historical documents, expert consultation, analogous cases, etc.) | Colorization is presented as a **'natural' result** |
| Observer's position | The **social, cultural, and technical position** of the restorer is declared | The restorer's position is anonymized (as 'automatically' done by AI) |
| Redefinition of objectivity | Objectivity is constructed as **'a collection of partial perspectives'** | Objectivity is pretended as **'universal viewpoint'** |

**Harawayan question**: "Who, from what position, and with what responsibility produced this image?"

### Criterion 4: Evidential Openness — Foucauldian Criterion

| Category | Interpretation | Manipulation |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions of the archive | The image specifies **which archive system it came from** | The image circulates **separated from archive conditions** |
| Visualization of power | Colonial photographs are revealed as **products of power** | Colonial photographs are presented as **neutral historical records** |
| Alternative evidence | The image's **incompleteness opens possibilities for other interpretations** | The image's completeness **forces a single interpretation** |

**Foucauldian question**: "Which evidentiary system does this image strengthen, and which does it exclude?"

## 7. Conclusion: What 'Restoration' Conceals

Analyzed through four theoretical frameworks, what the process of generative AI 'restoring' and circulating colonial photographs conceals can be summarized across four layers:

**1. Media Concealment (Kittler):** The material conditions of black-and-white photographs (characteristics of silver halide photography recording only light intensity, historical damage, analog continuity) are treated as 'noise' and erased. In Kittler's terms, **"the realm of the dead" is replaced by calculable data**, and the material traces of the real recorded by the medium disappear.

**2. Epistemological Concealment (Foucault):** The label 'restoration' disguises AI's probabilistic inference as 'recovery of fact.' This leads to **closure of the evidentiary system**. When a complete, smooth visual surface is presented, the incompleteness and interpretive space that are conditions for historical understanding disappear.

**3. Power Concealment (Haraway):** AI's 'neutral' output actually reflects norms of a specific visual system (WEIRD, Western, post-Enlightenment). This is a **new version of the 'god trick'** that absolutizes a specific position's viewpoint under the mask of technical objectivity.

**4. Historical Concealment (Benjamin):** AI colorization of colonial photographs can be either a **dialectical image that actualizes the tradition of the oppressed** or a **reactionary ornament that reinforces the continuum of history**. The difference depends on how transparently the image reveals its own incompleteness and mediation.

### Final Judgment

The process of generative AI 'restoring' colonial photographs is **ambivalent**. On one hand, it holds the **possibility of Benjaminian actualization**—dismantling the Eurocentric visual system of colonial photographs and summoning the oppressed past in present political demands. The decolonial colorization of Sámi photographs demonstrates this. However, on the other hand, the risk is greater that this process **closes the Foucauldian evidentiary system, erases Kittlerian material traces, and performs the Harawayan 'god trick'**. Particularly, AI's media characteristics—opaque training data, concealment of probabilistic inference, the 'restoration' frame—**strongly incline toward the latter direction**.

The decisive criteria distinguishing interpretation from manipulation are **media transparency and positional accountability**. The core is how transparently the image reveals its production conditions (with what data, what algorithm, by whom, for what purpose), and **whether it indicates uncertainty and deficiency or conceals them**. In Benjamin's terms, true historical actualization is not 'completely' restoring the past, but **flashing up the wounds and deficiencies of the past in the urgency of the present**. When AI smoothly covers these wounds in the name of 'restoration,' it becomes manipulation that undermines the conditions of historical understanding.
