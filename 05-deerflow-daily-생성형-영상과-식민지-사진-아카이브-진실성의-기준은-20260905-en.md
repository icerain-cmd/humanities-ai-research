# Criteria for Truthfulness in Generative Video Restoration: A Triad of Record, Perception, and Provenance

## 1. Theoretical Coordinates of the Question

The case of a 2024 generative video model (e.g., Runway Dream Machine, OpenAI Sora, KETI restoration project) being trained on a colonial-era photo archive to "restore" historical scenes brings two opposing positions on the ontological status of photography into direct conflict.

**Media-memory theory** is anchored in Peirce's concept of the index, Krauss's photo-index theory, and Barthes's `ça-a-été` (that-has-been). From this perspective, the truthfulness of a photograph lies in its photochemical/optoelectronic **causality**. The moment light reflected from a real object is imprinted onto film/sensor, the photograph becomes not merely a resemblance but a physical trace (index). This indexical connection makes the photograph "evidence."

**Generative aesthetics** begins from the point that generative AI images completely sever this indexical connection. An AI video is merely "a probabilistic prediction of the most plausible pixel array," with no physical connection to any specific past moment. From this perspective, "restoration" is not reproduction of the past but the **generation of a new sensory scene**, and truthfulness should be judged not by accuracy but by **perceptual plausibility**—what Steven Prince calls 'perceptual realism.'

## 2. Case Analysis: KETI Independence Activist Restoration Project and GENIAC Project

In August 2026, the Korea Electronics Technology Institute (KETI) presented a project for the 80th anniversary of Liberation Day that provides a concrete example of this question. KETI used generative AI to restore photos of nine independence activists active in early 20th-century Hawaii, producing a video in which they appear to personally describe historical sites. The method combined actual photos with filmed footage of reenactment actors, with the AI mimicking the actors' facial expressions, lip movements, and gestures.

In this case, the tension in judging truthfulness is clear:
- **Media-memory theory judgment**: There is an ontological break between the actor's face and the actual face of the independence activist. Not a single micro-expression generated by AI contains the 'real' past. Strictly speaking, this video is not restoration but generation, and cannot serve as evidence of the actual activist's appearance.
- **Generative aesthetics judgment**: However, the sensory experience this video delivers—the feeling that "the independence activist is speaking directly to you"—enables historical empathy beyond the record. This 'perceptual plausibility' creates a new kind of historical experience.

Meanwhile, the British Academy's **GENIAC project** (Generative Artificial Intelligence for Archival Images of the Colonial Period) emphasizes a 'responsible approach' when applying generative AI to colonial-era image archives, adopting co-design with source communities, validation of dataset biases, and above all, **provenance transparency** as core principles.

## 3. Dialectical Structure of the Three Criteria: Accuracy of Record, Sensory Plausibility, Provenance Traceability

Each criterion, taken independently, has limitations.

### Accuracy of Record

Accuracy of record asks how closely each element of the generated video matches known historical facts. However, this criterion faces two fundamental limitations.

**First, colonial-era photographs themselves were not 'accurate records.'** Colonial-era photo archives were composed, selected, and staged to depict the colonized through a Western lens (the colonial gaze). As Hellmann's (2025) study shows, when generative AI learns from this archive, distortions are reproduced such as "pre-colonial landscapes are rendered as empty wilderness, Captain Cook appears as a calm civiliser, and Māori are cast as timeless, peripheral figures." The biases of the original are amplified through AI. Before even asking about accuracy of record, the question of 'which record' should serve as the standard must be resolved first.

**Second, the boundary between 'restoration' and 'generation' is inherently unclear.** In the KETI project, which micro-expressions come from information extracted from the actual photo, and which are the AI's probabilistic predictions? This boundary is, in principle, indistinguishable. The very concept of 'accurate' restoration presupposes an indexical connection, which generative AI severs from the outset.

### Sensory Plausibility

Sensory plausibility judges whether the video looks perceptually 'real.' This is the domain where generative AI excels. Since the essence of generative models (GANs, diffusion, transformers) is to model the distribution of training data and produce the most plausible output, sensory persuasiveness is almost automatically achieved.

However, the danger of this criterion is obvious. **Perceptual plausibility is independent of historical accuracy.** In Hellmann's study, when the neutral prompt "first encounter between Māori and James Cook" was given to Sora, the model consistently reproduced a colonialist narrative. The 'most plausible' visual composition is the one that appears most frequently in the training data statistically. If the colonial-era archive is biased, the 'most plausible' video will also be biased.

A more serious problem is that sensory plausibility **illegitimately acquires cognitive authority**. The 'that-has-been' effect of the photograph—the conviction that "this really happened"—traditionally relied on the indexical connection. But generative videos can perfectly reproduce this effect without any index. From the perspective of media-memory theory, this is nothing less than **the forgery of evidence**.

In Barthes's *Camera Lucida*, the 'punctum' is an accidental detail that 'pricks' the observer *because it actually existed*. The 'punctum' of an AI-generated video is fundamentally virtual—because that detail is not a trace of a real historical moment but merely the result of a probabilistic distribution. This invalidates the ontological condition of the punctum.

### Provenance Traceability

Provenance traceability means documenting, in a traceable form, which original materials the generated video came from, through what transformation processes, and via what decisions. This extends the traditional archival science principle of provenance and the genealogical source documentation principle into the context of generative AI.

Why is this criterion central?

**Provenance traceability is the meta-condition that enables evaluation of the other two criteria.**

- When provenance traceability is ensured, we can distinguish **where each element of the video came from** (regions extracted from original photos vs. regions generated by AI vs. interpretative choices by the director). This distinction must be possible to meaningfully evaluate 'accuracy of record.'
- When provenance traceability is ensured, we can know what training data the generated video is based on. This information is essential for judging whether 'sensory plausibility' is a tool that conceals bias.

In the KETI project, the hybrid structure of 'actual photo + reenactment actor + AI synchronization' itself contains a kind of provenance information. However, in the video that ordinary audiences access via QR code, the distinction among these three layers is visually completely sealed. What the audience sees is just 'a video of an independence activist speaking'; they cannot know where each element of the video came from, what is from the actual photo, what is the actor's performance, and what is the AI's probabilistic prediction.

The GENIAC project's approach contrasts with this. Emphasizing "co-design with source communities" and a "responsible approach," this project suggests that **transparent documentation of provenance information** must precede the use of generative AI on colonial-era images.

## 4. Triple Judgment Model: A Hierarchical Integration of Provenance-Accuracy-Plausibility

Based on the above analysis, the truthfulness of AI restoration videos cannot be judged by any single one of the three criteria. It should be approached through the following **hierarchical integration model**.

```
                   Provenance Traceability
                  ┌─────────────────┐
                  │   Meta-         │
                  │   Condition     │
                  └────────┬────────┘
                           │
          ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
          │                │                │
     Accuracy of Record  Sensory          Ethical
     (Accuracy)         Plausibility      Transparency
                        (Plausibility)    (Transparency)
```

**Layer 1: Provenance Traceability** — The foundation of all judgment. It must be documented which materials the generated video came from, through which transformations, and by which decisions. Without this, both accuracy and plausibility cannot be evaluated.

**Layer 2: Accuracy of Record** — Only after provenance is documented can the issue of accuracy of record be raised. At this layer, we can distinguish 'what came from the original photo' vs. 'what was generated by AI,' and evaluate the historical reliability of the AI-generated parts.

**Layer 3: Sensory Plausibility** — Only after provenance and accuracy have been evaluated can sensory plausibility function not as mere deception but as a tool for historical empathy. At this layer, sensory plausibility **complements, rather than replaces, indexical truth**.

**Layer 4 (Cross-Cutting): Ethical Transparency** — A condition that cuts across the above three layers, including explicit disclosure that the video was generated, warnings about potential biases, and information on whether the source community participated.

## 5. Conclusion: A Hierarchical Paradigm Shift in Truthfulness

The truthfulness of traditional photographs could be judged by a single index. But in an era where generative AI 'restores' (in fact, generates) historical scenes, truthfulness cannot be reduced to a single criterion. The conclusion of this analysis is as follows.

**The truthfulness of generative AI restoration videos should be judged by adopting provenance traceability as the highest criterion, while hierarchically integrating accuracy of record and sensory plausibility.**

This is a **third path** that neither completely discards the indexicalism of media-memory theory nor entirely surrenders to the sensationalism of generative aesthetics. Provenance traceability reconstructs the legacy of the index (connection to the original) under new media conditions, while simultaneously ensuring, through transparency of the generation process, that sensory plausibility does not conceal historical truth.

The practical implications of this model are as follows:

1. **Standardized provenance metadata** should be required for all generative historical restoration content. Metadata standards at the level of IEEE or W3C are needed, including source image ID, model and version used, generation prompt, degree of human editor intervention, and bias assessment results.

2. **The duality of sensory plausibility** must be recognized. Sensory plausibility is both a positive tool that enables historical empathy and a dangerous device that naturalizes and conceals bias. This duality can only be balanced through transparent presentation of provenance information.

3. **The特殊性 of colonial-era photo archives** must be considered. This archive is itself a 'contaminated record' already constructed through a colonial gaze. The amplification of bias when generative AI learns from this record is not a mere 'technical error' but a **political problem of digital reproduction of colonial epistemology**. Provenance traceability can be a tool to make this problem visible.

4. **Reconciliation between media-memory theory and generative aesthetics** is possible, and the point of contact is precisely provenance traceability. Barthes's `ça-a-été` is no longer a single photochemical moment but is reconstructed in the form of a **data genealogical trace** (provenance trail). Not "this pixel existed," but "this pixel was generated from this data, through this algorithm, via this decision process"—in this way, indexicality can persist in a new form even within a probabilistic generative system.
