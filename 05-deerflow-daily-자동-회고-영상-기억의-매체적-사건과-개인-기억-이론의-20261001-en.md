# Smartphone Auto-Recollection Feature and the Medial Reconfiguration of Memory — In-Depth Analysis

---

## Introduction: Case Setting

Smartphone OS auto-recollection features (Apple Memories, Google Photos "Memories," Samsung Gallery "Memory Cards," etc.) select and edit users' photos, locations, and search histories algorithmically to present "memory" videos periodically. This feature goes beyond the externalization of memory; as the question accurately points out, the platform performs the entire process of memory creation, selection, and presentation, thereby transforming memory from a "practice of calling up past traces into the present" into a "re-sensorialization of data arrays." Below, we analyze sequentially the three levels implied by this transformation—medial-ontological shift, aesthetics of tension, and theoretical modifications demanded.

---

## 1. Operational Structure of Auto-Recollection: Re-sensorialization of Data Arrays

### 1.1 Topology of Three Operations

Auto-recollection can be broken down into three distinct operations, each undermining categories of traditional memory theory.

**① Selection — Shift in the Agent Specifying Memory Content**
In the traditional model, the agent selecting what to remember is the remembering self. This premise, starting with Plato's wax block analogy in *Theaetetus*, continued through Augustine's voluntary exploration of memory's "palaces" in *Confessions* to Freud's dynamic subject between repression and conscious recall. In auto-recollection, this selection power is fully delegated to the algorithm.

Correlations between photo metadata (shooting time, location), facial recognition tags, and search history become the selection criteria. Crucially, **correlation differs from causality**. That I took many photos in Jeju Island in March 2024 and searched for "Jeju Island restaurants" around the same time does not guarantee a causal connection between that search and my experience at that place. Yet the algorithm operates on this correlation as selection rules. This means **the criterion for what is memorable has shifted from experiential significance to statistical correlation**.

**② Arrangement — Automation of Narrative Construction**
In traditional memory theory, retrospection involves temporal reconstruction. As Bergson analyzed in *Matter and Memory*, memory is an operation of "duration (durée)" reorganizing the past to meet present needs, omitting useless details and compressing them into meaningful narratives. This selective compression is the creative aspect of human memory.

Auto-recollection's arrangement replaces this creative compression with **algorithmic editing**. This process consists of clustering (grouping by time, place, people), importance evaluation (weighting based on central figures, expressions, sharpness), and sequencing (chronological or thematic ordering). The problem is that while this editing aims for an "optimal recollection experience," **the criteria for optimality are optimized not for the user's experiential truth but for the platform's engagement**. Happy moments, visually clear frames, and socially shareable content are prioritized. This systematically excludes the **tragic, ambiguous, polyphonic** aspects of memory.

**③ Invocation — Elimination of Contingency and Controlled Activation**
In Proust's *In Search of Lost Time*, the involuntary memory triggered by the madeleine provides a powerful aesthetic experience precisely because of its contingency, unpredictability, and sensory triggering. This involuntariness reveals the fundamental **otherness** of memory, occurring outside the subject's control.

Auto-recollection's invocation is the opposite:
- **Predictability** → Periodic notifications (e.g., "On this day last year"), scheduled times
- **Controlled senses** → Visual focus, audio (background music) auxiliary, olfaction and touch completely absent
- **Personalized universality** → My data, but presented in the same UI format as everyone else's

This invocation structure replaces Proustian involuntariness with **designed serendipity**. That is, the algorithm determines the timing and content to present "as if by chance." This results in the platform **simulating** the otherness of memory while losing genuine otherness.

## 2. Tension Between Convenience and the Unintentional, Polyphonic Nature of Memory

### 2.1 The Duality of Convenience: Liberation of Memory Labor or Deprivation of Memory?

The convenience offered by smartphone auto-recollection is clearly, on one hand, a reduction of **memory labor**. Organizing photos, classifying albums, creating opportunities for recall require time and cognitive energy, tasks nearly impossible for individuals in today's information-overloaded environment.

However, this convenience comes with the following costs:

**A. Passivization of Memory**

In traditional memory practices, individuals:
- **Judge** what is worth remembering (active selection)
- **Fix** memories linguistically, visually, ritually (active recording)
- **Invoke** them when needed (active recall)
- **Connect** them to the present to renew meaning (active re-signification)

Auto-recollection performs all four steps. The user is placed in a passive position of **receiving** the output. This transforms memory from **'having' something** to **'receiving' something**.

**B. Positive-only Curation and Monochromatization of Memory**

One characteristic of auto-recollection algorithms is **positivity bias**. Photos with smiling faces, beautiful landscapes, celebratory moments are prioritized. This results from combining social conventions that frame memories as "precious moments" with algorithmic sentiment analysis.

However, human memory is inherently **not monochrome**. A single scene can contain joy and sadness, longing and regret, affection and resentment simultaneously. Bakhtin's "polyphonia" applies to memory as well. Autobiographical memory appears narrated in a single voice, but is actually a multilayered overlay of diverse emotions, perspectives, and self-states.

Auto-recollection removes this polyphony and amplifies only the **monophony of joy**. By systematically excluding the uncomfortable, contradictory, and unintegrated from memory, it offers the user a "comfortable" consumption experience but simultaneously **hinders the maturity of memory**. Memory matures through integrating painful experiences and contradictory self-images.

### 2.2 Involuntariness of Memory — Clash Between Proust and Algorithm

The contrast between core elements of Proustian involuntariness and algorithmic auto-recollection can be summarized as follows:

| Dimension | Proustian Memory | Algorithmic Recollection |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Trigger | Sensory (taste, smell, touch) | Data-based (images, metadata) |
| Timing | Unpredictable, random | Periodic, calculated timing |
| Content | Incomplete, fragmentary, ambiguous | Selected, edited, organized |
| Emotional response | Coexistence of joy + pain (complexity) | Primarily positive emotions (simplification) |
| Meaning formation | Requires active construction | Induces passive consumption |
| Subject's position | Discoverer (active discovery) | Audience (passive reception) |

This contrast implies that while auto-recollection **deliberately simulates involuntariness**, its essence is **controlled intentionality**. The "On this day last year" notification appears accidental, but is the result of intentional system design. This is a typical strategy where technology maximizes the effect of "naturalness" to render its intervention invisible (invisibilization).

## 3. Modifications Demanded of Person-Centered Memory Theories When Viewing Memory as a Medial Event

### 3.1 Core Premises of Person-Centered Memory Theories

The analysis of auto-recollection demands a fundamental reexamination of the conceptual foundations of memory theory. Traditional person-centered memory theories are based on the following premises:

1. **Premise of Ownership**: Memory belongs to the individual's mind (Locke's discussion of personal identity, Book 2, Chapter 27).
2. **Premise of Interiority**: Memory is an internal experience, directly inaccessible from outside (Augustine's "inner palace").
3. **Premise of Continuity**: Memory guarantees the identity of the self across time.
4. **Premise of Spontaneity**: The invocation of memory requires at least the subject's consent or activation.

Auto-recollection undermines or reconfigures all four premises.

### 3.2 From Ownership to Distribution/Environment

In auto-recollection, my memory is stored not in my mind but on Apple/Google's servers, and presented not at my invocation but at a time determined by the platform's algorithm. This means the **locus** of memory has shifted from the mind to a distributed data environment.

Furthermore, **distribution** of memory occurs. One "memory" is a composite of data fragments under different ownership and control: my photos, my friend's location information, the platform's classification criteria, my search history from that time. Memory is no longer "mine" but a phenomenon emerging from a **network effect**.

This transition is an application of Friedrich Kittler's medial-ontological thesis—"media determine our situation"—to the realm of memory. As Kittler pointed out, when media change, the nature of the information they store, transmit, and process also changes fundamentally. If letters in the print media era constituted individual interiority, memory in the algorithmic platform era becomes an **interface event** constituted by the data environment.

### 3.3 From Interiority to Surfacing/Visibilization

Augustine's memory was a "vast and infinite inner palace," an area inaccessible from outside and approachable only through the subject's introspection. Auto-recollection creates a point where this introspection becomes thoroughly unnecessary.

When a moment from three years ago that I had forgotten is presented on my screen by the algorithm, memory **is no longer something I discover by going inward, but something that reaches me from outside**. This can be called not just the exteriorization of memory, but the **surfacing of memory**. That is, memory is no longer intimate depth but an event occurring on a visible plane (interface surface).

This transition generalizes the problem of memory implantation depicted in Philip K. Dick's *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* to an everyday level. If the sense of memory being "mine" (autonoetic consciousness) can be **generated** by data arrangement and presentation, the concept of memory authenticity is no longer self-evident.

### 3.4 From Continuity to Discrete Events

In existing theories (especially the phenomenological tradition of Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Ricoeur), the continuity of memory is a condition for the identity of the self. However, the memory of auto-recollection is presented not as a continuous flow but as **discrete events**. The past is divided not into continuous duration (durée) but into discrete packets, experiencing ephemeral appearance through algorithmic invocation.

"Memory of this moment" is presented as a 30-second video, and once it ends, the memory remains in **latency** until the next invocation. This discontinuity replaces the temporal structure of memory from **continuity to a cycle of appearance and latency**.

Paul Ricoeur's argument in *Time and Narrative* that narrative constitutes the temporality of memory is well known. However, the narrative of auto-recollection is not emplotted by the self but is a **pre-emplotted narrative** by data. In Ricoeur's threefold mimesis (mimesis I, II, III), auto-recollection has **the algorithm perform mimesis I (pre-understanding) and mimesis II (configuration), leaving humans only mimesis III (refiguration)**. This reduces the active role in the construction of narrative identity.

### 3.5 From Spontaneity to Environmentalization of Triggering

The collapse of the spontaneity premise is the most radical. Auto-recollection invokes memory without any will or consent from the subject. This is not simple involuntariness but the **disappearance of the active self-triggering subject**. In other words, the answer to "Who remembers?" shifts from "I" to "the platform."

This transition demands conceptualizing digital recollection as the **environmentalization of memory**. That is, memory is no longer an action performed by the subject but an event that the configuration of the environment in which the subject is situated **generates for** the subject. Memory is no longer a "faculty" but an effect arising between the "state of the environment" and the "exposure of the subject" to it.

This precisely parallels the theoretical context cited in the question—approaching AI not as an object but as an environment. Memory being environmentalized means that the infrastructure for memory (storage, classification, retrieval, presentation) has been fully transferred to an external system that operates without the individual's cognitive effort.

## 4. Directions for Theoretical Modification: Six Theses

Based on the analysis, the modifications demanded of existing person-centered memory theories are presented in thesis form.

### Thesis 1: Expansion of the Concept of Memory Subjecthood

The subject of memory is no longer attributable to the human individual. Rather, the **emergence of memory** occurs when a **distributed network** of humans, algorithms, databases, and interfaces forms a specific arrangement. This demands introducing Actor-Network Theory (ANT) concepts into memory theory. Memory is not "something humans possess" but an effect of **human-nonhuman associations**.

### Thesis 2: Replace Memory Authenticity with Medium-Specificity

The question "Is this memory truly mine?" should be replaced with "Under what medial conditions did this memory emerge?" The authenticity of memory is no longer a matter of origin but a matter of **transparency of medial conditions**. That is, the presentability of how memory was constructed should become the new criterion for authenticity.

### Thesis 3: Ontology of Memory Beyond Correlationism

As Quentin Meillassoux pointed out, the limits of correlationism (reducing being to the correlation between human and world) apply equally to memory theory. Traditional memory theories have focused on "the way memory is given to humans," neglecting the **non-human conditions** of memory—database structures, classification algorithms, transmission protocols. Auto-recollection clearly demonstrates how these non-human conditions determine the very possibility of memory.

### Thesis 4: From Narrative Identity to Interface Identity

Ricoeur's narrative identity holds that humans form identity by narratively integrating their lives. However, in the auto-recollection environment, identity is determined by **how one accepts, rejects, or reconfigures the memory fragments presented by the interface**. This demands reconceptualizing identity formation not as the composition of a single narrative but as an **intervalic negotiation** among various interface invocations.

### Thesis 5: Introduce the Political Economy of Memory

Auto-recollection is not a free service. Users contribute to the **datafication of memory** by uploading photos, generating metadata, and being exposed to the platform. This accumulated data becomes the raw material for the memory industry. Sturken's discussion of the "technologization of memory" now extends to the **commercialization of memory**. The moment a smartphone manufacturer advertises "safeguarding precious memories," **memory is transformed from experience to service, and again to commodity**. Memory theory must include analysis of these political-economic conditions.

### Thesis 6: Re-establish the Aesthetic Dimension — Aesthetics of Data Arrays

Auto-recollection creates not just a function but an **aesthetic genre**. 30-second length, background music, slideshow transition effects, centrally aligned subjects — this formal consistency is both a digital succession of the traditional home slide show ritual and decisively different.

As Benjamin pointed out in *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction*, technical reproduction dismantles the aura of the original. Auto-recollection similarly dismantles the **aura of personal memory**. In that unique, irreplaceable individual memory experiences are homogenized into identical UI templates for all users, **mass production of memory** is occurring.

At this point, the Benjamin reference in the Khoj document has direct implications. In an "art environment characterized by the dissolution, collapse, shedding of aura," memory is no longer an experience with the uniqueness of **here and now (hic et nunc)** but **content reproducible in identical format anytime, anywhere**. According to Benjamin's thesis, this dismantling simultaneously implies a liberating moment—democratization of memory, equalization of memory access—but also entails the risk of re-appropriation by capital. We must be wary that the promise of liberation through the convenience of auto-recollection might actually result in the **colonization of memory by the platform**.

---

## 5. Conclusion: Transformation of Memory's Terrain and Theoretical Reconstruction

The transformation of memory revealed by the smartphone auto-recollection case is not simply technological progress but a **change in the ontological topography of memory**. The core of this change can be summarized along three axes:

1. **From verticality to surface**: Memory becomes an event occurring not on depth (introspection, unconscious, deep layers) but on surface (UI, interface, screen).
2. **From individuality to distributiveness**: Memory shifts from a faculty attributable to the subject to a network effect.
3. **From continuity to eventness**: The temporal structure of memory transforms from continuous flow to discrete appearance.

Existing person-centered memory theories (Augustine, Bergson, Freud, Ricoeur) demand fundamental modification in the face of this transformation. Above all, the greatest challenge is that the concept of memory "ownership"—"this is my memory"—can no longer be maintained. Memory must be redefined not as an **entity confined to the interior domain of the individual mind** but as a **medial event emerging from a specific arrangement of data, algorithms, and interfaces**.

This suggests that memory theory must shift its conceptual axis from the triad of human-self-narrative to the triad of human-data-interface. To ensure this shift does not result in simple technological determinism, we must also consider the possibilities for **critical-creative practice** that humans can perform in this new memory environment. The search for an **ecological practice of memory** that enjoys the convenience of auto-recollection while not abandoning the values of polyphony and involuntariness will be the starting point.
