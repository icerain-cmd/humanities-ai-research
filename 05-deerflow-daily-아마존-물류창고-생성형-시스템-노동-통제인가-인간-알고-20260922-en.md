# In-depth Analysis: Generative Operating System, Machine Phylum, and the (Re)emergence of the Labor Subject

## 1. Problem Setting — The Ontological Status of the System

The generative operating system introduced in 2026 at an Amazon warehouse is not merely an evolution of optimization algorithms. It is qualitatively different from previous generations of algorithmic management in that it forms a **complete circular loop of prediction-allocation-command-feedback**.

Traditional algorithmic management (e.g., picking path optimization, real-time allocation) adjusted the **external conditions** of work, presupposing the given worker's capabilities and limitations. In contrast, the 2026 generative system **absorbs the worker's bodily sensory, cognitive, and behavioral data in real time and feeds it back in the form of work orders**, thereby recursively constituting not just the working conditions but the very mode of operation of the work subject.

This difference is decisive. If the former is **optimization of the environment**, the latter is **reconfiguration of the conditions for the emergence of the subject itself**.

## 2. The Lens of the Machine Phylum — The Ontological Turn of Non-Human Technology

The machine phylum is a concept proposed by Deleuze & Guattari in *A Thousand Plateaus* and appropriated by Kittler into media theory. It has three core propositions.

**First, technical beings are not tools of humans but beings with their own material and formal logic.** When Kittler declares that "media determine the conditions of human perception," this means that technology does not merely mediate human will but constitutes the **a priori conditions of possibility** for human perception, cognition, and action.

**Second, the machine phylum is not a separate entity of humans and technology but a single material-informational flow that traverses both.** From this perspective, the boundary between human subject and technical object is not fixed; both co-emerge within a specific assemblage (agencement).

**Third, subjectivity emerges as an effect of technical assemblages.** There is no pre-technical subject. The subject is always-already constituted within a technical environment, through interaction with that environment.

Viewed through this lens, the Amazon generative operating system is understood not so much as a **device that controls human workers from the outside**, but as a condition in which workers and algorithms form a single **operational ensemble**.

## 4. Core Analysis: The Dual Status of the Generative System

### 4.1 Status as a Control Device — Predictive Taylorism

The first superficial reading characterizes the system as **predictive Taylorism**. If Frederick Taylor's scientific management set labor standards through time-motion studies, the 2026 system extends this logic to be **real-time, personalized, and predictive**.

- **Real-time**: It reflects not the statistical average of past data, but the worker's bodily data at this very moment.
- **Personalization**: The system learns individual workers' past patterns, fatigue levels, and speeds to generate personalized commands.
- **Predictivity**: The system predicts not only the worker's movement path but also the point of fatigue accumulation and productivity decline, performing pre-emptive allocation.

From this perspective, the system transfers the worker's autonomous judgment and bodily regulation abilities to the algorithm through **datafication of the body**. The worker no longer decides "when, where, and how to move." The system decides, and the worker executes. This is close to a **technical implementation of the Hegelian master-slave dialectic** — the worker cedes their bodily sensation and action coordination abilities to the system, and the system returns those abilities in the form of commands.

### 4.2 Status as an Agent — Co-emergence of the Subject

However, the lens of the machine phylum presents a more complex picture. The control device reading **presupposes the human subject as an already-constituted autonomous being**, whereas the machine phylum emphasizes that the subject is an effect of technical assemblages.

**Philosophical Implications of the Datafication-Feedback Structure**:

The process by which the worker's bodily experience is datafied and fed back into work orders forms the following cycle:

```
Worker's kinesthesia → sensor data → algorithm processing → predicted path generation → work order → worker's behavioral adjustment → (cycle repeats)
```

Points to note in this cycle:
1. **The direction of information is not unidirectional.** The worker's body is not merely a source of data but also a channel through which the algorithm's output is executed via the body.
2. **Humans and algorithms form an informationally closed cycle.** The 'data' the system learns is the human's bodily movements, and the 'commands' the human executes are the system's output.
3. **The unit of this cycle is neither human nor algorithm. The cycle itself is the minimal unit of operation.**

At this point, Gilbert Simondon's concept of **individuation** becomes crucially relevant. According to Simondon, the individual is not pre-given but emerges from the resolution of tensions within a **pre-individual field**. The cycle of the generative system is precisely such a pre-individual field. Humans and algorithms do not exist as already completed subjects/objects; rather, within this cycle, the **work subject** newly emerges.

This emerging subject is **neither the autonomous human subject in the traditional sense nor a fully determined mechanical object.** It is:

- **Generative**: The system generates commands, but the specific execution of those commands depends on the worker's bodily coordination abilities. The same command is performed differently by different bodies.
- **Distributed**: The work subject is not a single consciousness but is distributed across the entire network of worker-algorithm-sensor-warehouse space.
- **Recursive**: The execution of a command generates the next data, and that data generates the next command. The subject is maintained within the permanent recursion of this cycle.

### 4.3 Pressure on Ontological Distinction — Reconfiguration of the Concept of Autonomy

Now we reach the core question: **How does the process of workers' bodily experiences being datafied and fed back into work orders pressure the existing concept of autonomy that distinguishes between human subjects and machine subjects?**

**Two axes of the existing concept of autonomy**:

1. **Autonomy of will (Kant)**: The ability of the subject to act according to its own reason, not determined by external causes.
2. **Autonomy of action (libertarianism)**: The ability of the subject to choose among alternative courses of action.

**Three points of pressure**:

**First, the closure of the information cycle collapses the distinction between 'external determination' and 'internal determination.'**
In the generative system, 'external' can no longer be defined as opposing the worker's will. The system's commands are imposed from the outside, but the content of those commands is generated from the worker's own past bodily data. The worker is controlled by the external while being part of that external. This is a point where **the opposition between control and self-determination is nullified**.

**Second, the criterion for action attribution becomes blurred.**
Whose action is a single movement in the workplace (e.g., "move 3 meters to the right and pick up box A")? Is it the body executing a command generated by the algorithm? Is it the body's action that provided the data enabling the algorithm's prediction? Or should the entire cycle be considered a single actor?

**Third, the status of bodily sensation changes.**
Bodily sensations such as pain, fatigue, and tension have traditionally been considered 'internal,' a unique domain accessible only to the individual subject. However, when the generative system datafies heart rate, stride length, speed changes, and micro-movement patterns and uses them to generate work orders, bodily sensations are **relocated from an internal phenomenon to a node in the cycle**. The privilege of the 'internal' is dismantled.

These three pressures commonly shake the **core premise of autonomy** — that the subject of action is the self-aware source of that action. Within the cycle of the generative system, action no longer originates from 'someone's decision.' Action emerges as an effect of the cycle.

## 5. Implications of the Machine Phylum — A Dialogue with Kittler

Kittler's work in *Gramophone, Film, Typewriter* historically demonstrated the thesis that media determine human perception. If analog media (writing, gramophone, film) shape the conditions of human vision, hearing, and sensation, the 2026 generative system can be understood as a **digital medium that shapes the conditions of action**.

The radical claim implied by Kittler's media theory is: media set the conditions not only for human **perceptibility** but also for **actionability**. The Amazon system illustrates this starkly:
- When the system only directs predictable tasks, the **range of the worker's action possibilities** is pre-defined by the system.
- When the system treats unpredictable situations as 'errors,' actions outside the system's operational scope become 'non-existent.'
- This means that the domain of action is **ontologically constituted** by the medium.

However, Kittler's deterministic reading — "media determine humans" — requires modification. The generative system operates through **bidirectional cycles**, not unidirectional determination. The worker's body is not only the system's data basis but also the only site where system commands are concretized. In this respect, viewing the system as a **structurally open cycle** is more accurate than viewing it as a deterministic closure.

## 6. Comprehensive Conclusion: Neither Simple Control Nor Simple Emergence of a New Subject

The conclusion of the analysis is dialectical.

**The generative operating system is neither a 'simple labor control device' nor an 'agent that fully generates a new subject.'** It is a **contradictory assemblage** in which these two statuses are ontologically inseparable.

**The status as control** cannot be denied. The system datafies the worker's bodily sensations, makes actions predictable through that data, and systematically suppresses actions outside prediction. This is the most sophisticated form of labor control.

**However, this control is constitutive rather than repressive.** The system does not simply limit the worker's freedom; it constitutes the **conditions** of what the worker does, senses, and how they act. This constitutive control does not take something away from the worker; instead, it makes the worker exist in a specific way.

This paradox is the core of the pressure on the existing concept of autonomy. **Control no longer operates as a restriction of freedom, but as a control over the conditions of emergence of subjectivity.** This can be understood as a form of Foucault's 'governmentality' brought down to the workplace level — if disciplinary power standardizes the body from the outside, the generative system produces 'voluntary compliance' by having the body itself constitute the conditions of possibility for its own sensation, cognition, and action from within.

**The distinction between human subject and machine subject is both maintained and dismantled within this assemblage:**
- The distinction is **functionally maintained**: the system generates commands, and humans execute them. This role division is a premise of the system's operation.
- The distinction is **ontologically dismantled**: the cycle of command and execution exists only as a single process that crosses the boundary between the two subjects.

Therefore, the most radical implication of this system is: **at the point where the existing concept of autonomy — 'internal self-determination opposed to external determination' — is nullified, a new meaning of autonomy is demanded.** This new autonomy is:
- Autonomy not as the absence of control, but as the ability to recognize and intervene in the structure of the cycle.
- Autonomy not as individual will, but as the collective ability to reconfigure the conditions of the cycle.
- Autonomy not as freedom of choice, but as the ability to decide 'in which cycle to exist.'

Such a concept of autonomy remains a theoretical task, and the current generative system operates in a way that denies it. However, the fact that the system itself simultaneously generates the conditions of possibility for this new autonomy — and this is the final tension of the analysis — is the core of the ontological challenge posed by the machine phylum.
