# Memory of What Never Was: A Conceptual Stress Test of Stiegler's Tertiary Retention

This rupture along both axes poses a fundamental question to Stiegler's concept of tertiary retention: **Can tertiary retention also be a memory of 'that which never was'?** If so, can the concept of 'retention' itself be sustained any longer?

## 2. The Conceptual Structure of Stiegler's Tertiary Retention: A Reconstruction

### 2.1 From Husserl to Stiegler: The Triple Structure of Retention

The starting point of the analysis is Husserl's analysis of time-consciousness. In *Lectures on the Phenomenology of Internal Time-Consciousness*, Husserl posited that the perception of a temporal object is composed of three layers:

- **Primary Retention**: The immediate grasping of perception. The phenomenon where a just-heard note 'still' lingers in the present when a current note of a melody is sounding.
- **Secondary Retention**: Recollection. The ability to re-present a past perception in the present.
- **Urimpression (Primal Impression)**: The pure present sensory impression.

In Volume 1 of *Technique and Time*, Stiegler decisively expands this Husserlian framework. He argues that Husserl did not acknowledge tertiary retention. According to Stiegler's reading, while Husserl hinted at the possibility of tertiary retention through concepts like 'retention of retention' in *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis*, he did not systematize it.

Stiegler's decisive contribution lies in **inverting tertiary retention into the constituent condition of primary and secondary retention**. This is not merely an expansion of memory storage but an ontological claim about the very conditions of time-consciousness.

### 2.2 Epiphylogenesis and the Constitution of Time

Stiegler's core thesis can be summarized as follows:

> **Human = Technical Being (être technique)**. Humans become human only by exteriorizing their memory into tools. This exteriorized memory is tertiary retention, enabling the transmission of a 'past that we have not lived.'

Epiphylogenesis is situated at the intersection of three memory layers:
- **Phylogenetic Memory**: The genetic heritage of the species.
- **Epigenetic Memory**: Neuroplastic changes through individual experience.
- **Epiphylogenetic Memory**: Technical memory exteriorized in tools, signs, and media.

For Stiegler, tertiary retention is the condition for the emergence of a 'past we have not lived' into the present. The use of stone tools, written records, photographic images — all transmit past knowledge and sensations to later generations who never experienced them.

### 2.3 Core Properties of Tertiary Retention: Three Conditions

The minimum conditions for Stiegler's tertiary retention to be established can be extracted as follows:

1.  **Material Exteriorization**: Memory must escape the individual's neuro-bodily interior and be fixed onto a material substrate.
2.  **Temporal Transmission**: This material memory must persist even after the original perceiving subject is gone and be accessible to subsequent subjects.
3.  **Conditioning of Primary and Secondary Retention**: This material memory must not merely be a repository but must influence the formation of new primary and secondary retentions. In other words, tertiary retention participates 'retroactively' in the constitution of primary and secondary retentions.

## 3. The Ontological Singularity of Generative AI Images

### 3.1 The Operational Structure of 'Memory of What Never Was'

The process by which generative AI (DALL·E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion) creates images has the following structure:

```
Training Data (billions of image-text pairs)
    → Compressed into weights of a Latent Diffusion Model
        → Stored as a statistical distribution (probabilistic latent space)
            → Sampling based on a prompt (text condition)
                → Decoding into a new image
```

Key points to note in this process:

1.  **The training data are images that 'actually existed'** — exteriorizations of memory generated through some human's primary perception (photography, painting).
2.  **The model weights are the statistical patterns of this data** — a compressed representation of the structural and visual regularities shared by billions of images.
3.  **The generated result is a sampling of this statistical pattern** — a decoded output of the most probable pixel arrangement under specific prompt conditions.

### 3.2 Three Types of 'That Which Never Was'

The 'unreal memories' produced by generative AI can be categorized into at least three types:

| Type | Example | Ontological Status |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| **Type A: Factual Synthesis** | '1900s Steampunk Seoul Street' | Each element (1900s Seoul, steampunk aesthetics) existed, but this combination never existed. |
| **Type B: Visualization of the Impossible** | 'A butterfly with elephant wings' | Visualization of beings that violate physical laws of reality. |
| **Type C: Virtual Memory** | 'Reconstruction of my parents' wedding before I was born' | Never experienced by the individual, but the historical event may have existed. |

Your question focuses on a special case of **Type A** — scenes like '1900s Steampunk Seoul Street,' where each component existed, but the specific combination never existed.

## 4. Conceptual Stress Test: Examining the Applicability of Tertiary Retention

### 4.1 Aspects Capturable Within the Framework (In-Framework Capture)

**Condition 1: Material Exteriorization** — Generative AI model weights are clearly a form of material exteriorization. Billions of human visual experiences are compressed and stored in terabytes of weight matrices. In Stiegler's terms, this is the pinnacle of 'organized inorganic matter.' This condition is met.

**Condition 2: Temporal Transmission** — The statistical patterns stored in the model weights persist even after the original perceiving subjects (photographers, painters) are gone, and new subjects can access them via prompts. This condition is also met.

**Condition 3: Conditioning of Primary and Secondary Retention** — Humans viewing the generated image form new primary retentions (perception) and secondary retentions (recollection) about that image. A person who sees an image of 'Steampunk Seoul' can remember it as if it 'was.' As seen in the Synthetic Memories project, cases where generated images function as substitutes for actual memories prove this. This condition is also met.

**Tentative Conclusion 1**: All three conditions are met. Therefore, generative AI images **can be positioned within Stiegler's category of tertiary retention**.

### 4.2 Aspects Beyond the Conceptual Framework (Beyond the Framework)

However, this 'tentative conclusion' is only superficial. At a deeper level, three fundamental tensions emerge.

**Tension 1: The Ontological Implication of 'Retention'**

Stiegler's tertiary retention carries the meaning of 'to retain.' For something to be 'retained' presupposes that it 'previously existed.' The problem with generative AI is: **What is being retained?**

- Traditional tertiary retention (writing, photography): 'Meaning' or 'patterns of light' are retained. This is the trace of an initial perception.
- AI model weights: A 'statistical distribution' is retained. This is not the trace of an initial perception but **the relational pattern among numerous traces**.

In other words, what the AI model retains is not **individual event-memory but the formal structure of multiple memories**. This collapses the premise implicitly presupposed by Stiegler's concept of 'tertiary retention' — the 'exteriorization of an individual past.'

**Tension 2: The Absence of Perception and the Collapse of Temporality**

In Stiegler's *Technique and Time*, tertiary retention always presupposes the existence of an 'initial perception.' In the case of a photograph, there is the object that existed before the camera and the reflection of its light. In the case of writing, there is the moment of the act of writing. In the case of generative AI:

- **No human perception intervenes** in the generation process. The model generates 'without seeing.'
- The generated image is not the exteriorization of any human's primary retention (immediate grasping of perception).
- This creates an ontological paradox: **'representation without an initial perception.'**

At this point, the fundamental assumption of the Stiegler-Husserlian framework — 'all representation is a modification of an initial perception' — faces a crisis.

**Tension 3: The Temporality of a 'Past That Never Was'**

Stiegler's epiphylogenesis explains the transmission of a 'past that was not lived.' However, this 'past that was not lived' is still **a past that actually existed**. The experience of using an ancestor's stone tool, which I did not live, was a past that actually existed for that ancestor.

In contrast, generative AI's 'Steampunk Seoul' is **a past that never existed for anyone**. This is not a 'past that was not lived' but an 'unlivable past.' This fundamentally transforms the temporal condition of tertiary retention.

## 5. The Need for Conceptual Expansion: Three Proposals

### 5.1 Proposal 1: Introduction of the Concept of 'Quaternary Retention'

To capture the singularity of generative AI images, I propose the concept of **quaternary retention**, expanding Stiegler's triple structure:

| Category | Definition | Example |
|----------|------------|---------|
| Primary Retention | Immediate grasping of perception | Current note of a melody |
| Secondary Retention | Recollection, imagination | Re-presentation of yesterday's melody |
| Tertiary Retention | Materially exteriorized memory | Photographs, recordings, written records |
| **Quaternary Retention** | **Formal structure of multiple memories compressed into a statistical distribution** | **AI model weights** |

The decisive differences of quaternary retention are:
- While tertiary retention is the **exteriorization of individual memory**, quaternary retention is the **exteriorization of the relational pattern among numerous memories**.
- While tertiary retention is the **storage of 'what was'** , quaternary retention is the **condition for generating 'what could be'** .
- While tertiary retention enables the **presentification of the past** , quaternary retention enables the **generation of a virtual past**.

### 5.2 Proposal 2: Expansion of the Stiegler-Montani Path — From 'Techno-Imagination' to 'Techno-Generation'

The concept of 'techno-imagination' by Pietro Montani, which you referenced, captures an important part of this issue. In *Technological Destinies of the Imagination*, Montani explores the technical conditions of Kantian imagination (Einbildungskraft) and argues that digital technology enables the automation of imagination.

The phenomenon Montani calls 'inert syncretism' — the automatic combination of text and image — precisely indicates the image generation process of generative AI. However, Montani still uses human imagination as a reference point. His position is that generative AI does not 'replace' human imagination but 'technically extends' it.

In response, I propose the following expansion:

> **'Techno-imagination' → 'Techno-generation'**

While Montani's 'techno-imagination' focuses on the technical extension of human imagination, 'techno-generation' refers to visual generation at a completely non-human dimension — the production of images that have not passed through any human perception or imagination. This is a concept for the generative capacity of technology itself, transcending the category of Kantian imagination.

### 5.3 Proposal 3: The Ontology of 'Post-Perceptual Representation'

I propose **'post-perceptual representation'** as an ontological category for generative AI images. It has the following properties:

1.  **Form of Representation**: Visually takes the form of representation (indistinguishable from photography/painting).
2.  **Absence of Perception**: Does not correspond to any human's primary perception.
3.  **Statistical Origin**: Generated from the statistical patterns of numerous perceptions.
4.  **Virtual Temporality**: Brings a 'past that never was' into the present.

This concept demands a redefinition of the existing concept of representation along the axis of 'the existence or non-existence of perception.' Representation should no longer be 'Re-präsentation' (re-presentation of perception) but should be redefined as **'a visual construction that imitates the formal conditions of perceptual experience.'**

## 6. Conclusion: Results of the Stress Test

### 6.1 Confirmation of Essential Tension

Stiegler's concept of tertiary retention is valid for capturing the **material, transmissible, and conditioning aspects** of generative AI images. AI model weights are clearly a form of 'organized inorganic matter,' enabling temporal transmission and conditioning human primary and secondary retentions. In this respect, Stiegler's framework still functions.

However, in the **ontological and temporal aspects**, a new phenomenon is emerging that transcends the conceptual boundaries of tertiary retention:

1.  **Change in the Meaning of 'Retention'**: Tertiary retention meant 'the retention of an individual past,' but the AI model retains 'the formal structure of multiple memories.' This is an ontological change in 'what is retained.'
2.  **Change in the Meaning of 'Past'**: Tertiary retention presupposed the transmission of 'a past that actually existed,' but AI generates 'a past that never existed.' This is a transformation of 'pastness' itself.
3.  **Change in the Meaning of 'Representation'**: Tertiary retention presupposed 'the representation of an initial perception,' but AI produces 'representation without perception.' This is an ontological rupture of the concept of representation.

### 6.2 Final Judgment: 'Applicable, but Conceptual Expansion is Necessary'

Stiegler's tertiary retention can **partially** theorize generative AI images. However, this theorization comes at the following cost:

- **Generalization of the Concept**: Overly expanding the concept of 'tertiary retention' dilutes the meaning of 'retention,' erasing the ontological differences between writing, photography, and AI models.
- **Transformation of Temporality**: For AI images to be captured as tertiary retention, the concepts of 'past' and 'memory' must be fundamentally redefined.

Therefore, my answer is as follows:

> **Stiegler's concept of tertiary retention is a useful starting point for theorizing the phenomenon of 'memory of what never was' in generative AI, but it is insufficient. This phenomenon operates at the conceptual boundary of tertiary retention, and to fully capture it, we need (1) the introduction of the concept of quaternary retention, (2) an expansion from 'techno-imagination' to 'techno-generation,' and (3) the establishment of an ontology of 'post-perceptual representation.'**
