## 1. Conditions for Commencing the Analysis

The question is established upon the following three theoretical premises:

1. **Transformation into Artificial Nature**: The flood prediction system reconstructs natural disasters into a real-time, calculable data environment.
2. **Ontological Asymmetry**: This transformation generates an ontological rupture between what is captured and what is not captured.
3. **The Problem of Surplus**: Informal sensibilities and ecological contingencies not captured by prediction are not merely "defects" but products of systematic exclusion.

## 3. Ontological Status of Informal Sensibilities: "Reality Without Data"

### 3.1. Structure of the Problem

The flood prediction system performs the following hierarchical data transformation:

```
Physical Phenomenon → Sensor Signal → Quantified Value → Model Input → Risk Map Output
```

In this process, **informal sensibilities** refer to:

- Residents' **bodily experience**: "The smell is different than usual," "The water is rising at an unusual speed"
- **Local knowledge** from long-term residence: "This manhole in front of the house always overflows first when it rains 50mm"
- **Physical anxiety**: A sense of crisis felt regardless of measurable water levels

### 3.2. Ontological Status: "Non-Data Reality"

Transforming the "art without a work" concept from Document 3 into ontology:

**Phenomena-without-data** do not exist from the perspective of the prediction system, but this does not mean they are not real. On the level of artificial nature, they exist as **the un-capturable real**.

Specific Status:

| Category | Artificial Nature Level | Ontological Status |
|----------|-------------------------|--------------------|
| Sensor Data | System component | **Captured nature** (naturalized data) |
| Climate Model Output | System prediction structure | **Algorithmic futurity** |
| **Informal Sensibilities** | **Invisible input** to the system | **Virtual environment** |
| **Ecological Contingencies** | System **error/noise** | **Excessive real** |

The concept of meta-presentation from Document 4 operates critically here: The "risk map" shown by the flood prediction platform **conceals** informal sensibilities on the meta-level while simultaneously **depending** on them. If a citizen reporting function is included on the platform, it reveals the ontological tension at the moment of converting (translating) informal sensibilities into data.

**Ontological Costs of Translation**:
- When a resident's "anxiety" is converted into a "report level (severe/caution/safe)", the **qualitative characteristics of the sensibility** are lost
- Untranslated sensibilities exist **outside the system's gaze** but remain real conditions of the environment

### 3.3. Formalization

> **Ontological Status of Informal Sensibilities**: A **non-indexical reality** located outside the epistemic field of the artificial nature system. It cannot be input into the system (non-data nature), but it constitutes the conditions of the environment to which the system must respond (reality).

## 4. Ontological Status of Ecological Contingencies: "Nature as Excess"

### 4.1. Structure of the Problem

Ecological contingencies refer to events that the prediction model cannot, in principle, capture:

- Ecosystem changes occurring after the prediction time (collapse of a beaver dam, patterns of trash blocking drains)
- Microclimate peculiarities (localized heavy rain concentrated on a specific block)
- Atypical human-nature interactions (residents improvising drain blockages, unexpected traffic control)

### 4.2. Ontological Status: "Excessive Real / Productive Noise"

Indirect insight provided by the genealogy of desire from Document 5: Lacan's 'the Real' exists as a **surplus** that the symbolic order (here, the data-prediction system) cannot fully subsume. Supplemented by Deleuze's concept of 'virtuality', ecological contingencies belong to the realm of **not-yet-actualized virtual potential**.

This occupies two ontological statuses simultaneously:

**A. Inside the System: "Residue" or "Noise"**
- Treated as error range of the prediction model
- Isolated as a statistical outlier
- Potential to be mistaken for sensor failure or missing data

**B. Outside the System: "Ecological Condition"**
- Contingency is a property of the **environment in which the prediction system is embedded**
- Artificial nature attempts to eliminate contingency, but contingency is the **operating condition** of artificial nature
- This recalls Kittler's insight: What the medium records is determined by the formal conditions of the medium; its outside is not 'noise' but 'the condition of the medium's impossibility'

The concept of hypertext discontinuity from Document 2 opens the possibility of reinterpreting ecological contingency as **the discontinuity of nature**: The continuous, linear causality of nature assumed by the prediction system collapses in the face of ecological contingency, and this collapse is not a defect but the very mode of nature's existence.

### 4.3. Formalization

> **Ontological Status of Ecological Contingencies**: An **excessive real of the ecosystem** that the artificial nature system, due to its formal conditions, is forced to systematically exclude. It exists not as the system's outside but as a **constitutive outside** upon which the system depends but cannot control.

## 5. Synthesis: Ontological Responsibility of Artificial Nature

### 5.1. Ontological Map of the Two Surpluses

```
                  Artificial Nature
                  ┌────────────────────────────┐
                  │  Calculable Data Environment│
                  │  - Sensor Data              │
                  │  - Satellite Imagery        │
                  │  - Climate Model Outputs    │
                  │  - Structured Citizen Reports│
                  └──────────┬────────────────-─┘
                            │
          ┌────────────────-─┴────────────────-─┐
          │                                    │
    Informal Sensibilities            Ecological Contingencies
    (non-indexical reality)           (excessive real)
    - Bodily experience, local        - Unpredictable events
      knowledge, anxiety              - Statistical outliers
    - Qualitative, bodily, atypical   - Constitutive outside
    - "Blind spot" of the system        of the system
```

### 5.2. Comparison Table of Ontological Statuses

| Category | Status within Artificial Nature | Realist Status | Relationship with Artificial Nature |
|----------|--------------------------------|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Informal Sensibilities | Non-data | **Non-indexical reality** | Excluded but depended upon |
| Ecological Contingencies | Noise/Error | **Excessive real** | Constitutive outside |
| Artificial Nature Data | Valid input | **Indexical reality** | System identity |

### 5.3. Demand for Ontological Responsibility

What the question ultimately demands is:

1. **Epistemic Humility**: Do not reduce "what the prediction system cannot know" to a mere technical task. This is an **ontological limit**, not a temporary technical flaw.

2. **Justice to the Remainder**: Recognize informal sensibilities and ecological contingencies not as objects of system optimization, but as the **ontological conditions** that artificial nature suppresses to constitute itself.

3. **Design of Dual Ontology**: The prediction system must accept the **asymmetrical coexistence** between the data environment it captures (artificial nature) and the surplus it fails to capture (informal sensibilities, ecological contingencies) as its operating principle. This demands more than a simple "uncertainty display" UI; it requires a **meta-ontological interface** through which the system itself indicates its own ontological limits.

4. **Final Question**: Does this surplus become more distinct as artificial nature becomes more complete? Or can it be absorbed as artificial nature develops? The latter is closer to a fantasy of technological optimism. The refinement of artificial nature **changes the quality of the surplus but does not eliminate the surplus itself**. In this regard, "art without a work" (Document 3) and "reality without data" share a structural analogy. Both are **surplus beings** that the institution (the art institution/prediction system) cannot fully subsume.

## 7. Conclusion: "Non-Prediction" as Ontological Surplus

The artificial nature reconstruction of an urban flood prediction system inevitably produces **two kinds of ontological surplus**:

1. **Informal Sensibilities** → **Non-indexical reality**: Sensory and experiential conditions that cannot be converted into data but are real.
2. **Ecological Contingencies** → **Excessive real**: The self-difference of the ecosystem systematically excluded due to the formal conditions of computational prediction.

These two surpluses are not the **failure** of artificial nature but its **condition**. Ontological responsibility lies not in the technological progress of eliminating these surpluses, but in establishing **a method of coexisting** with them as a principle of system design. The moment artificial nature names what it fails to capture as "error," it performs an ontological concealment. The core task of an ontology of artificial nature is to prevent this concealment from further suppressing informal sensibilities and ecological contingencies.
