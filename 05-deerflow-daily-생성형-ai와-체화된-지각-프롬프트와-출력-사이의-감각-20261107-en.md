# Merleau-Ponty’s Concept of Embodied Perception and Generative AI: Possibilities and Challenges of Explanation

## Introduction: The Structure of the Question

Your question consists of two levels: (1) **whether Merleau-Ponty’s concept of embodied perception can explain** generative AI’s mediation of language and vision, and (2) **what challenge** the absence of bodily sensation between prompts and outputs poses to theories of perception. These are not separate questions. Rather, they stand in a dialectical relationship in which a positive answer to the former makes the challenge posed by the latter even more apparent.

---

## 1. Core Concepts of Merleau-Ponty’s Embodied Perception

To understand the implications of Merleau-Ponty’s theory of perception for discussions of generative AI, it is necessary to identify three central concepts.

**① The intentional arc**

In *Phenomenology of Perception*, Merleau-Ponty writes that “the life of consciousness—cognitive life, the life of desire, or perceptual life—is subtended by an ‘intentional arc’ which projects round about us our past, our future, our human setting, our physical, ideological and moral situation.” As Hubert Dreyfus elaborated this concept, the intentional arc is a structure that embodies the **interconnection between perception and action** through skillful coping. When this arc is operative, things do not appear merely as representations; they appear in ways that **solicit** our responses.

**② The lived body (*corps vécu*)**

For Merleau-Ponty, the body is not merely a biological machine. It is an “instrument of general comprehension” open toward the world and the primary site where meaning takes hold. “The body is our general medium for having a world,” and this mediation operates on three levels: (a) innate bodily structure, (b) basic general skills, and (c) culturally acquired skills. A chair “solicits sitting” from us at all three of these levels.

**③ Flesh (*la chair*) and the chiasm**

In his later work, *The Visible and the Invisible*, Merleau-Ponty introduces the concept of “flesh.” Flesh is an **element of Being** that traverses the subject and object, the perceiver and the perceived. The perceiver—the seeing body—and what is perceived—the visible world—intersect on the same fabric of flesh, and this intersection is the condition of perceptual experience.

## 2. How Generative AI Operates and the Absence of Embodied Perception

Generative large language models (LLMs) and image-generation models operate as follows:

- **Training method**: They extract statistical patterns from vast text and image corpora. **Sensorimotor experience** plays no role whatsoever in this process.
- **Inference method**: They receive a prompt—a linguistic input—and probabilistically predict the next token or pixel. There is no **bodily state, emotion, or sense of spatial situatedness** involved in this process.
- **Nature of “perception”**: The text or images produced by AI are not the result of a direct encounter with the world, but rather a **distributional recombination of training data**.

The crucial difference is this: when humans say that they “see something,” this always involves the **co-constitution of the world through the body**. When AI is said to “generate something,” however, this refers to a **bodiless statistical reproduction**.

## 3. Can Merleau-Ponty’s Concepts Explain Generative AI?

The answer to this question must be divided into **partial affirmation and recognition of fundamental limitations**.

### 3.1. Aspects That Can Be Explained

**① The intersubjective dimension of language**

Merleau-Ponty understands language not as a mere system of signs, but as embodied expression. In *Eye and Mind*, he characterizes speech as “one of the body’s ways of taking a stance toward the world.” The language produced by generative AI can imitate the **general form** of human language, but Merleau-Ponty’s concept of language is useful for explaining that this form is originally the product of an intentional arc. AI output merely reproduces the **shell** of human utterance, while revealing that this shell originally arose from a bodily attitude.

**② Perceptual faith and the reality of AI outputs**

Merleau-Ponty analyzes the “perceptual faith” through which we perceive the world “as it is.” This faith originates in the body’s being-in-the-world (*être-au-monde*). The fact that AI outputs feel “natural” to human users occurs because users interpret those outputs through their own embodied perceptual frameworks. In other words, **what needs to be explained is not AI’s perception, but the way humans perceive AI outputs**.

**③ AI as an environment — Connection to the Khoj references**

One of the local Khoj search results raises the question, “Is AI a tool, or part of the learning environment?” From a Merleau-Pontian perspective, AI functions as part of the environment (*Umwelt*). AI outputs operate as **environmental conditions that reconstruct the user’s intentional arc**. When users interact with AI, their prompts are not merely requests for information, but **linguistic expressions of embodied intentions**, while AI responses are experienced as **feedback from the environment** in relation to those intentions.

### 3.2. Limits of Explanation — The Rupture Produced by the Absence of Embodiment

However, explanation reaches a fundamental limit at this point.

**① The absence of the intentional arc**

As Dreyfus points out, the intentional arc refers to the circular relationship between “skillful coping” and the “solicitations of the world.” AI receives no solicitations from the world. The text AI produces is merely the result of statistical patterns; no object “solicits” AI to walk or sit. **“Perception” without an intentional arc is not perception, but merely pattern recognition.**

**② The absence of flesh and the impossibility of the chiasm**

At the most fundamental level, AI lacks flesh. Merleau-Ponty’s concept of the chiasm presupposes that the perceiver and the perceived exist on the **same ontological fabric**. AI operates entirely at the level of signs and does not come into contact with the flesh of the material world. The texts and images generated by AI are not an intersection with the world, but merely **internal correlations among data**.

## 4. The Challenges the Absence of Bodily Sensation Poses to Theories of Perception

This is the core of your second question: what challenge does the absence of bodily sensation between prompts and outputs pose to theories of perception?

### 4.1. First Challenge: The Problem of the Non-Continuity of Perception

Traditional theories of perception assume that perceptual experience unfolds within a continuous, embodied flow. However, the prompt–output relation displays the following discontinuities:

| Dimension | Traditional perception | AI prompt–output |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Temporality | Continuous flow (retention–protention) | Discontinuous request–response |
| Sensory modality | Co-constitution of multiple senses | Single modality (language → text/image) |
| Bodily mediation | Always already mediated | Pure signification lacking mediation |
| Contextuality | Situational meaning produced by the intentional arc | Contextual probability produced by statistical patterns |

This discontinuity poses a new task for theories of perception: **Can a “perceptual experience” that is not mediated by the body exist at all?**

### 4.2. Second Challenge: The Autonomization of Signs

Merleau-Ponty defines perception as that through which “the things are given to us ‘in person, in the flesh.’” Yet the operation of generative AI demonstrates that **signs can become detached from the body and operate autonomously**. This directly challenges Merleau-Ponty’s basic premise—that all meaning originates in embodied perception.

Possible responses to this challenge include the following:

- **Weak response**: AI’s generation of signs is not genuine meaning production, but merely a statistical recombination of meanings created by humans. AI output does not **refer** to meaning; it merely **simulates** the appearance of meaning.
- **Strong response**: AI’s operation demonstrates that signs can form an autonomous system independent of the body. This requires a revision of Merleau-Ponty’s theory of embodied meaning. (This position is more controversial and requires further research.)

### 4.3. Third Challenge: The Problem of the Delegation of Perception

The perspective that regards AI as “part of the learning environment,” found in the Khoj references, makes it possible to interpret humans as **delegating** part of their perceptual processes to AI. This calls for an interesting extension of Merleau-Ponty’s theory. Just as we use smartphones and search engines as “cognitive prostheses,” generative AI has begun to perform the function of a **perceptual prosthesis**.

However, this delegation produces a fundamental paradox. AI may appear to “see better,” but the moment it becomes clear that AI is in fact **not seeing**, users become aware of the absence of bodily sensation. The stage of “uncanny unease” identified in your Khoj materials emerges precisely here. The more sophisticated AI output becomes, the more unsettling it is that the output originates from **perception without a body**.

## 5. Overall Assessment: Theoretical Implications and Prospects

Merleau-Ponty’s concept of embodied perception can **partially explain** the phenomenon of generative AI, but this explanation paradoxically reveals **why the concept of embodied perception cannot fully capture the core of AI**.

| Level of explanation | Possibility | Limitation |
|-----------|--------|------|
| **Phenomenological description** | The way AI output appears to humans can be explained through Merleau-Ponty’s phenomenology of perception | AI’s internal processes may be better addressed through Kittler’s media theory |
| **Ontology** | AI functions not as a “tool” but as an “environment” (its environmental character can be analyzed) | The autonomy of AI signs requires a revision of embodied ontology |
| **Epistemology** | Users’ embodied perception constitutes AI outputs | The concept of AI “perception” itself may be merely metaphorical |
| **Ethics** | AI reconstructs perceptual faith concerning the world (see the Newcomb’s paradox experiment) | The problem of attribution of responsibility (Who “sees”?) |

**In conclusion:**

1. Merleau-Ponty’s concept of embodied perception is useful for explaining **how humans experience AI**. In particular, the way AI, as an environment, remediates the human intentional arc can be illuminated through his conceptual framework.
2. However, Merleau-Ponty’s theory of embodiment has fundamental limitations when it comes to explaining the **internal operation of generative AI**—the “black box” between prompts and outputs. This gap instead calls for a cross-framework synthesis with other theoretical resources, such as **Kittler’s media ontology** or **media aesthetics**.
3. The absence of bodily sensation between prompts and outputs poses a **transitional challenge** to theories of perception. This challenge requires us either to redefine the concept of perception itself, theorize the possibility of disembodied perception, or classify AI outputs under another concept—such as “statistical representation” or “symbolic simulation”—rather than perception.

Perhaps the most productive response to this challenge would be a **synthesis of Merleau-Ponty and Kittler**. If Merleau-Ponty provides a phenomenology of the body, Kittler analyzes the material conditions of media—systems of inscription, storage, and transmission. Generative AI is situated at the intersection of these two axes: **mediated embodiment** and **embodied mediality**. Theorizing this intersection is the central task currently facing theories of perception.
