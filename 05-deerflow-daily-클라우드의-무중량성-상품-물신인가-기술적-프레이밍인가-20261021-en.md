# The Weightlessness of the Cloud: Commodity Fetish or Technological Enframing?

## Introduction: One Phenomenon, Two Readings

The philosophical problem posed by the weightless experience of the cloud is not a simple choice. Faced with the same phenomenon—the experience of digital services as if floating without place, and the invisibilization of the e-waste, energy, and labor behind it—Marx's theory of commodity fetishism and Heidegger's thesis of Enframing (Gestell) each offer powerful yet distinct explanations. Below, after examining each reading, I attempt a layered synthesis that transcends their intersection and limits. The local Khoj references (MACHINOCENE Ecology 8-element system, generative aura discussion, 4 stages of AI-human contact) provide the specific coordinates for this analysis.

## 1. Marxist Reading: Is the Cloud a Commodity Fetish?

### 1.1 Structural Analogy

The core structure Marx analyzes in Chapter 1 of *Capital*, 'The Fetishism of Commodities,' is as follows. A commodity possesses the duality of use-value and exchange-value, and fetishism occurs when exchange-value appears as if it were a natural property of the thing itself. In this sensuous-supersensuous (sinnlich-übersinnlich) structure, the social character of production relations and labor disappears behind the fetishistic appearance.

The cloud reproduces this structure almost perfectly in the digital realm. 'Storage,' 'computing power,' and 'services' as experienced by the user are presented as if they were immaterial, natural properties. In the statement "my files are in the cloud," the cloud functions as a placeless, transparent medium. However, behind this virtual appearance lies the following:

- Data center power consumption (a single data center consumes as much electricity as a small city)
- Massive water usage for server cooling
- Labor in the global supply chain, from rare earth mining to semiconductor manufacturing and waste processing
- **E-waste dismantling labor in Agbogbloshie**—exposed to lead and cadmium

This concealment is a digital version of what Marx called the 'reification of value' (Versachlichung). Digital services, by thoroughly erasing traces of materiality from their user interface, appear as if they exist without labor or resources.

### 1.2 Power of this Reading

The most powerful analytical tool the Marxist reading provides is the **directionality of critique**. This reading reveals that the weightlessness of the cloud is by no means neutral and functions to conceal specific production relations. As the 8-element system of *MACHINOCENE ECOLOGY* (Reference 3) shows, 'base materials' (servers, semiconductors, minerals, e-waste) and 'base energy' (AI/data center power consumption) are the conditions of existence for the artificial nature, yet they are systematically removed from view. Whose interests does this concealment serve? Cloud service providers (Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure) evade responsibility for their material footprint by maintaining the weightless experience, while users remain unaware of the cost of their "clean" digital experience.

As Tung-Hui Hu deconstructs in *The Prehistory of the Cloud*, the weightless, placeless narrative of the cloud is a product of specific cultural representations originating in Cold War military networks. This can be read as an extension of Marxist fetish critique—how cultural representations conceal material conditions.

### 1.3 Limits of this Reading

However, there is a phenomenon that the Marxist reading alone cannot explain: the **way concealment persists**. The existence of Agbogbloshie has been widely known since the late 2000s through journalism, documentaries, and academic research. Yet the weightless experience of the cloud remains unshaken. If the problem were simply 'ignorance' or 'false consciousness,' the spread of information and critical awareness should be able to change this structure. But change is minimal.

This suggests a depth of concealment that Marxist analysis cannot fully account for—the possibility that concealment is not merely a repressive apparatus but is connected to the very way the world reveals itself.

## 2. Heideggerian Reading: Is the Cloud a Manifestation of Gestell?

### 2.1 Structure of Gestell

The Enframing (Gestell) thesis that Heidegger develops in *The Question Concerning Technology* (1954) operates on a fundamentally different level from Marxist fetish analysis. Gestell is the essence of technology, a mode of revealing (Entbergung) that reduces the world to 'standing-reserve' (Bestand). The decisive characteristic of this revealing is **self-concealment (Selbstverbergung)**. That is, Gestell reveals the world as Bestand while simultaneously concealing that this revealing itself is just one specific mode of revealing. This is why the technological worldview is experienced as the only natural worldview.

### 2.2 Cloud and Gestell

Reading the cloud from the perspective of Gestell reveals the following structure:

1. **World as Bestand**: The cloud repositions everything as 'standing-reserve.' Data is a resource to be stored, processed, and streamed. The user is a resource as user data, behavior, and interests. Nature (electricity, water, rare earths) is a resource to be optimized. Even waste remains within the logic of Bestand under the name of "resource recovery."

2. **Self-concealment**: The weightlessness of the cloud is not simply falsehood, but part of the way technological revealing conceals itself. When a user says their files are "in the cloud," what they experience is a specific ontic mode of manifestation. This experience conceals the ontological level—the structure of revealing that makes this experience possible.

3. **Danger (Gefahr) and Saving Power (Kehre)**: When Gestell is radicalized, everything appears only as Bestand, creating 'danger.' The cloud's failure to make e-waste visible is not a simple mistake or repression, but an expression of this danger itself. However, as Heidegger says, where the danger is greatest, the saving power also grows. Specifically, when the existence of e-waste begins to be consciously recognized, the self-concealment of Gestell begins to crack.

Jussi Parikka's *A Geology of Media* extends the Heideggerian reading into media theory at this point. Media must be understood in their material and mineralogical depth (rare earths, e-waste, the geography of server farms), and this depth is ontologically connected to the surface user experience. Extending Friedrich Kittler's thesis that 'media determine our situation' to discarded hardware, even the dismantled servers of Agbogbloshie still 'determine the situation'—the way they are dismantled conditions the global techno-ecosystem.

### 2.3 Power of this Reading

What the Heideggerian reading explains is the **persistence** and **systematicity** of concealment. The reason the cloud experience does not change even as knowledge of Agbogbloshie spreads is that this concealment is not merely an absence of information but is embedded in the very way the world reveals itself. If the Marxist reading asks 'what is hidden,' the Heideggerian reading asks 'how does concealment itself belong to the essence of technology.'

### 2.4 Limits of this Reading

However, the Heideggerian reading runs a risk:

- **Risk of technological determinism**: When Gestell is read as an ontological destiny (Seinsgeschick), the possibility of changing the material and political conditions of the cloud is overlooked.
- **Invisibilization of concrete exploitative relations**: The specific suffering of Agbogbloshie workers, environmental racism, and inequality between the Global South and North risk being abstracted within an ontological analysis.
- **Loss of critical directionality**: As the ecological inequality analysis of *MACHINOCENE ECOLOGY* (Reference 4) makes clear, "the problem of pollution and resource distribution" is "expanded into the problem of data, algorithms, and network access." This expansion requires a framework of Marxist distributive justice, which the Heideggerian reading alone cannot adequately guarantee.

## 3. Comparison and Discrimination: Which Reading is More Suitable?

When the two readings are set in competition over the same phenomenon, the conclusion leans toward **'not one instead of the other, but both, depending on the layer.'**

| Criterion | Marxist Reading | Heideggerian Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Object of Concealment | Concrete labor, production conditions, exploitation | The mode of revealing of Being itself |
| Nature of Concealment | Ideological, political (changeable) | Ontological, structural (necessary) |
| Path to Liberation | Critical awareness → transformation of production relations | Turn (Kehre) in thinking → different relationship with Being |
| Scope of Explanation | Economic, social inequality | Mode of being of the entire technological world |
| Limitation | Weak in explaining the persistence of concealment | Risk of abstracting concrete exploitation |

**Specific Case: Agbogbloshie**

The Agbogbloshie e-waste processing site is a complex phenomenon that the two readings explain on different layers:

- **Marxist layer**: In the division of labor of the global electronics industry, the Global South functions as the processing site for e-waste, a 'hidden abode' of capital accumulation. The weightlessness of the cloud is precisely the ideological effect that conceals this exploitative relationship.

- **Heideggerian layer**: However, even the servers dismantled in Agbogbloshie remain within the logic of Bestand—as "recyclable resources." True escape might be possible at the point where waste is no longer subsumed as a resource, i.e., outside the technological enframing. At this point, Donna Haraway's concept of 'response-ability' intervenes: when waste and dead media appear no longer as Bestand but as beings that demand a response.

## 4. Alternative Readings and Layered Synthesis

Beyond the dichotomy of Marx and Heidegger, several complementary frameworks are needed.

### 4.1 Bernard Stiegler's Pharmakon

Stiegler's pharmacological perspective captures the duality of the cloud: it simultaneously offers the liberation of the senses (data access anytime, anywhere) and material dependence (e-waste, energy consumption). It is both remedy (pharmakon) and poison. The weightless experience of the cloud is an expression of this pharmacological duality, which should be understood not as simple concealment or ontological hiding, but as an inseparable combination of remedy and poison. This perspective provides a pathway for synthesizing Marxist critique (the poison aspect) and Heideggerian thought (the ontological condition of the remedy).

### 4.2 Combining Ontological and Epistemological Layers

My proposal is as follows: The weightlessness of the cloud operates through a **dual structure of invisibilization**.

- **Primary Invisibilization (Marxist layer)**: Concrete labor, production conditions, e-waste, and energy consumption are systematically concealed from the experience of cloud services. This is ideological, can be made visible through critical analysis, and this visibilization itself is a political act. At this layer, the 8-element system of *MACHINOCENE ECOLOGY* (Reference 3) serves as an effective analytical tool for deconstructing the concealment of base materials, data, energy, and networks.

- **Secondary Invisibilization (Heideggerian layer)**: However, even after this critical visibilization is achieved, the problem is not fully resolved. The ontological structure in which the cloud continues to reposition the world as Bestand persists even after critique. Even after Agbogbloshie is made visible, the workers and waste there remain in the paradox of "visible invisibility."

### 4.3 Connection to the 4-Stage Topology of AI-Human Contact

The topological structure presented in local Reference 1 (4 stages of AI-human contact)—generative wonder → uncanny unfamiliarity → critical reflection → relational resonance—suggests an intellectual path through this dual structure of invisibilization:

1. **Generative wonder**: Initial wonder at the weightless experience of the cloud (at this stage, Agbogbloshie is invisible)
2. **Uncanny unfamiliarity**: Discovery of the materiality of e-waste and data centers (deconstruction of primary invisibilization)
3. **Critical reflection**: Systematic critique through Marxist analysis (political and ethical awakening)
4. **Relational resonance**: Ontological reconfiguration through Heideggerian and Stieglerian depth—reconstructing the relationship with the cloud not as Bestand but as resonance

Of these 4 stages, the current experience of the cloud usually stops at stage 1, or in some critical discourse reaches stage 3 but fails to transition to stage 4. True transformation requires stage 4—a way of experiencing the cloud no longer as a weightless service or ideological illusion, but as a relational resonance with its material, energetic, and waste conditions.

## 5. Conclusion: Which One Is It?

The weightless experience of the cloud is **both at the same time**—but on different layers.

The Agbogbloshie e-waste processing site is not the outside of the digital, but the concealed inside that constitutes the digital. This concealment operates through the structure of commodity fetishism analyzed by Marx: the value of digital services (convenience as use-value) appears as if it were a natural property existing without material conditions, while the global division of labor and ecological costs behind it disappear from view. On this layer, the cloud is an **ideological illusion**.

However, why this concealment is so persistent and systematic is explained by Heidegger's Gestell. The weightlessness of the cloud is not simply false consciousness, but part of the ontological structure in which the technological world reveals the world as Bestand while concealing this very revealing. On this layer, the cloud is the **necessary self-concealment of technological enframing**.

Therefore, the final judgment is as follows:

> **The weightlessness of the cloud is, primarily, an ideological concealment operating through the structure of commodity fetishism (Marx), but the ontological condition for the persistence of this concealment is Gestell (Heidegger). The former provides the language of critique to reveal 'what is hidden,' while the latter provides the language of ontology to think 'how concealment itself belongs to the mode of revealing of the world.' These two are not competitors but complementary layers that demand each other.**

Stiegler's pharmakon and Haraway's response-ability provide a pathway beyond this dual reading, allowing us to think about the duality of remedy and poison in the cloud, and our responsibility to respond to waste. And the 8-element system of *MACHINOCENE ECOLOGY* provides practical coordinates connecting this theoretical analysis to concrete ecological inequality analysis.

Ultimately, Agbogbloshie must no longer be concealed. However, its visibilization is not simply a matter of 'providing information,' but must be the beginning of an ontological practice that reconstructs the relationship with the technological world itself—not as Bestand, but as resonance.
