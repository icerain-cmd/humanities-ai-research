# Expansion of the Author-Function and the Splitting of the Subject of Enunciation
### — A Foucauldian Analysis of the Rie Kudan *Tokyo Metropolitan Sympathy Tower* Incident

## 1. Precise Analytical Coordinates of the Incident

Before analysis, it is necessary to reconstruct the structure of the incident. In January 2024, after winning the 170th Akutagawa Prize, Rie Kudan revealed at a press conference that approximately 5% of the novel—later reports suggested less than 2%—consisted of sentences generated by ChatGPT and used as is. More importantly, **where exactly is that 5% located?** The AI-generated sentences are confined to the dialogue of the generative AI character 'AI-Built' that appears in the novel—specifically, scenes where the AI speaks about itself. Thus, the novel has the following **three-layer structure**:

> **Layer 1 (Institutional Level)**: The author-name 'Rie Kudan' on the cover → Akutagawa Prize win → Incorporation into the Japanese literary field
>
> **Layer 2 (Narrative Level)**: The generative AI character 'AI-Built' appears in the novel and converses with humans
>
> **Layer 3 (Generative Level)**: The dialogue of that AI character is actually produced by a real generative AI (ChatGPT)

This three-layer structure is more than a mere technique. It takes **an immanent deconstruction of the Foucauldian author concept** as the very compositional principle of the work. This novel is not only *about* AI, but also (partially) *written by* AI, and by *revealing* this fact, it problematizes the status of the author itself.

## 2. The Four Axes of Foucault's Author-Function and Their Intersection with Generative AI

Foucault, in "What Is an Author?" (1969), formalized four characteristics of the author-function. Below, we examine the challenges posed by the Rie Kudan case to each.

### 2.1. Appropriation

According to Foucault, the author-function is a legal and institutional device that makes texts objects of ownership and copyright. An author is necessary for a text to become ownable, punishable, and distributable.

In *Tokyo Metropolitan Sympathy Tower*, this axis **operates perfectly**. Rie Kudan's name passes through all normal legal and institutional processes: the publishing contract with Shinchosha, the Akutagawa Prize win, and copyright protection. When the judging panel stated that "the use of AI was not an issue," the institution decided to extend the author-function to include AI-generated sentences. The author-function **absorbed** the machine's utterances; it was not dismantled because of them.

**→ Tentative judgment: The appropriation function is maintained and even expanded.**

### 2.2. Non-universality

Foucault noted that the author-function does not operate uniformly across all discourses. In the Middle Ages, literary texts circulated anonymously, while scientific texts required an author. Since the modern era, this relationship has reversed.

What is noteworthy in this case is that **the institution of the Akutagawa Prize legitimized the asymmetrical operation of the author-function**. Judge Yumiko Oshima (actual judges may differ) commented, "Anyone who reads the work will understand." This means the author-function was used as a **cognitive resource** in the judging process. The judges did not evaluate the work's 'Kudan-ness'; they judged the **completeness itself** of the text submitted under the author-name. Here, the author-function is revealed not as an indicator of the creative subject, but as a **purely functional device** for the circulation of texts within the literary field.

### 2.3. Constructed Entity

One of Foucault's most important insights is that the author is not a substance but a **construct**. The author is a rational entity retroactively constructed from the text.

The Rie Kudan incident **makes this construction visible**. When she said after winning, "Even if you define my work as 'not literature,' I don't really mind," she exposed the **artificiality** of the author-function. She admitted that she was not a 'creator' in the traditional sense, but rather performed the role of **organizing and arranging** linguistic materials, including AI outputs. Paradoxically, however, this exposure does not dismantle the author-function. On the contrary, upon hearing her confession, the audience constructs a new author image: a 'courageous writer.' The author-function **absorbs the very exposure and reinforces itself**.

### 2.4. Dispersion of Egos

Foucault argued that the author-function does not presuppose a single subject, but rather **disperses three egos**: (1) the author as actual person, (2) the narrator within the text, and (3) the institutional function of the author-name. Discontinuities exist among them.

Rie Kudan's novel **radicalizes** this dispersion. A fourth ego emerges:

> **(4) Mechanical Subject of Enunciation**: ChatGPT, which generated AI-Built's dialogue

This mechanical subject of enunciation is a new entity not captured by Foucault's analytical framework. Because:
- ChatGPT cannot bear legal responsibility like Foucault's 'actual author' (1)
- ChatGPT only pretends to have intentions like a narrative narrator (2), but has no actual intentions
- ChatGPT is not an institutionally recognized function like the author-name (3)

**This fourth ego raises the question of 'what' rather than 'who.'**

## 3. Core Issue 1: The Bond Between Author-Name and Enunciation

> "In this situation where the name circulates but the subject of enunciation shifts to a machine, is the bond between the author-name and enunciation, as presupposed by Foucault, dissolved? Or does the author-function instead more strongly possess and absorb the generated language?"

The answer to this question is **not one-sided; both occur simultaneously** — this is the philosophical tension of the incident.

### 3.1. Horizontal Dissolution

It is undeniable that the subject of enunciation has shifted to the machine. The sentences generated by ChatGPT among AI-Built's dialogues in the novel are, in a literal sense, not written by Rie Kudan. As Foucault showed in *The Order of Things*, the modern concept of 'man' is a being subjectified in a specific way within the order of language. Here, however, the order of language produces utterances directly without passing through human subjectivity. This recalls the conditions for the disappearance of 'the birth of man' that Foucault analyzed — when language is no longer a means of expression for the human subject but reveals itself as an anonymous system that arranges itself, the modern construct of the 'author' loses its foundation.

### 3.2. Vertical Absorption

However, at the institutional level, the opposite phenomenon occurs. Rie Kudan's author-name **possesses the AI-generated sentences more powerfully**. Because:

1. **Public confession actually strengthens authority**: By confessing her use of AI, she acquired ethical capital as a 'transparent writer,' which strengthened rather than weakened the author-function.

2. **Expansion of intentionality**: Rie Kudan maintained a minimal creative intention by posing questions to ChatGPT, selecting among its answers, and arranging them. She satisfies the condition of an 'intending subject' required by Foucault's author-function — only that intention has expanded from traditional 'writing' to 'editing/selecting/arranging.'

3. **Receptivity of the copyright system**: Japanese copyright law protects results that involve human creative contribution. The very fact that a human selected and arranged AI outputs serves as grounds for maintaining legal authorship. The institution chose to absorb the generated language.

### 3.3. Dialectical Conclusion: 'Internal Differentiation' of the Author-Function

Ultimately, dissolution and absorption are not opposites but **occur simultaneously**. The surface of the author-function (institution, circulation, classification) is strengthened, while the depth of the author-function (subject of enunciation, creative subject, source of meaning) is weakened. The author-function as analyzed by Foucault presupposed that these two levels were bound together as one. The Rie Kudan incident proves the **possibility of their dissociation**.

This is structurally similar to how Foucault, in *Discipline and Punish*, analyzed the transformation of power from external repression to internalized 'discipline.' The author-function, too, **differentiates from within, becoming on one hand more powerful and on the other hand more hollow**.

## 4. Core Issue 2: AI's First-Person Utterance — Who is the Subject?

> "When a chatbot speaks in the first person, who is that subject? When the first-person utterances of an AI are absorbed under the author-name of a human writer, what transformation does the concept of the author itself undergo?"

This question is the philosophical crux of the incident.

### 4.1. Empty First Person

When ChatGPT says "I think..." or "I think that...", this 'I' has no referent. Linguistically, the first-person pronoun refers to the speaker at the moment of the utterance act. However, in the case of ChatGPT, the speaker has no 'self' beyond the utterance act. It is not a subject in the Cartesian sense, nor a transcendental ego in the Husserlian sense, nor even the discursive subject that Foucault problematized. It is a **purely syntactic position** of the first person.

When such a first person is placed as the dialogue of the character AI-Built in the novel, the following layered transposition occurs:

```
Utterance Level       Subject of Enunciation       Referent
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Institutional Level   Rie Kudan                    'I wrote this novel'
Narrative Level       AI-Built                     'I understand this world'
Generative Level      ChatGPT                      'I ...' (empty)
```

The first person at the generative level is close to a **zero degree** in the linguistic sense. It occupies only the grammatical position of the first person while referring to no one.

### 4.2. Four-Stage Transformation of the Author Concept

When this empty first person is absorbed under Rie Kudan's author-name, the concept of the author undergoes its most radical transformation since Kant formalized art as the 'product of genius' in the *Critique of Judgment*:

| Stage | Author Concept | Subject of Enunciation | Status of First Person |
|-------|----------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **1. Modern Author** (pre-Foucault) | Genius as creative subject | Human (entire text) | Substantial ego |
| **2. Poststructuralist Author** (Foucault/Barthes) | Function/effect of discourse | Human (intention indeterminate) | Constructed ego |
| **3. Hybrid Author** (Rie Kudan model) | Organizer/selector/arranger | Human+machine hybrid (divided text) | Split ego |
| **4. Future Author** (logical conclusion) | Designer of utterance conditions | Primarily machine (human designs prompts) | Empty + selection |

The Rie Kudan incident corresponds to stage 3. Importantly, this stage 3 is not reducible to stage 1 or 2, and it **internalizes** the possibility of transition to stage 4.

## 5. Expansion: Conditions Foucault Did Not Consider

Several presuppositions that Foucault (1926-1984) held when formalizing the author-function concept in 1969 are no longer valid today. The Rie Kudan incident exposes the historical contingency of these presuppositions:

### 5.1. The Presupposition of the 'Human Condition of Enunciation'

For Foucault, enunciation (énonciation) was always an act of a human subject. Even if that subject is constituted by discourse, the result of that constitution was a human subject. However, utterances produced by generative AI do not pass through a human subject. This reveals an **anthropocentric residue** that Foucault presupposed but did not consciously acknowledge. (In this respect, Catherine Malabou's concept of 'plasticity' might be more suitable for explaining the utterances of generative language models.)

### 5.2. Change in the 'Economy of Language'

Foucault saw the author-function as imposing a 'principle of thrift' on the infinite proliferation of meaning. However, the language of generative AI is inherently **proliferating excessively**. A system capable of generating an infinite number of variations from a single prompt stimulates the proliferation of meaning that the author-function seeks to suppress. Rie Kudan performed the principle of thrift *vicariously* by 'selecting' 5% from this infinite generative possibility. This means the thrift principle of the author-function has transformed from an internal principle of creation into a **principle of post-hoc selection**.

### 5.3. Dialectic of 'Death' and 'Birth'

Barthes declared the 'death of the author,' and Foucault analyzed how the author-function persists after that death. However, generative AI creates conditions similar to the state **before the author is 'born'** — a oscillation between medieval anonymity, where no one knows who wrote a text, and the modern condition where an author-name is forced upon every text. Rie Kudan deliberately **navigates** between these two conditions — she puts her name forward, but simultaneously confesses that some texts under that name come from a 'masterless' state.

### 5.4. Kittler's Media-Archaeological Supplement

Foucault sought the conditions of enunciation in discursive practices, but as Friedrich Kittler pointed out, enunciation is always **conditioned by media of recording, storage, and transmission**. The Rie Kudan incident shows that these media conditions have decisively changed. The print medium (the novel book) still maintains the traditional author-function, but part of its content was produced under completely different conditions (probabilistic token generation by an LLM). This situation, where the same physical medium of a book superimposes two different conditions of enunciation, suggests that Foucault's discourse analysis did not sufficiently consider the **materiality of media**.

## 6. The Author-Function of 'Masterless Language' and a New Ethics

### 6.1. The 'Host' Model of the Author-Function

The traditional author-function assumes the author as the 'source' of the text. The Rie Kudan model reconceptualizes the author as the 'host' of the text:

> **Source Model**: Author → Text (author is the source of the text)
> **Host Model**: Text (partially) → Author → Literary field (author is a conduit introducing anonymous productions into the literary field)

The author as host **accepts and takes responsibility** for machine-generated language under their own name. This is a moment where the 'appropriation of discourse'[^1], which Foucault saw as the core of the author-function, becomes an **even more active act** — instead of merely claiming to have created, the author performs the decisive act of making the machine's utterances one's own.

### 6.2. Topological Transformation of the Author-Function

What this incident suggests is that the author-function does not disappear but **changes its topology**:

| Dimension | Traditional Author-Function | Author-Function After Generative AI |
|-----------|----------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Vertical dimension | Creation → Text → Reception | Prompt design → Generation → Selection → Arrangement → Literary field |
| Position of subject | Exists before enunciation | Engages selectively after enunciation |
| Mode of responsibility | Full responsibility for creation | Partial responsibility for selection and arrangement |

This corresponds to the condition of the subject in the 'achievement society' diagnosed by Byung-Chul Han in *The Burnout Society* — a subject driven not by external discipline but by its own performance. Rie Kudan rejects externally imposed authorial discipline (the traditional duty of the creator) and performs her authorial status through her own choices (which sentences to adopt and which to discard).

## 7. Conclusion: Challenge and Expansion of Foucault

The Rie Kudan incident demands three conclusions regarding Foucault's concept of the author-function.

**First, the author-function is not dismantled in the age of generative AI; rather, it expands.** At the legal, institutional, and economic levels, the author-name evolves in a direction that absorbs and strengthens machine utterances. The institution of the Akutagawa Prize, by 'permitting' the use of AI, extended the scope of the author-function to include machine output. This is a notable institutional decision — the institution acknowledged that the author-function can operate even when the subject of creation is not human.

**Second, however, the *internal structure* of the author-function is fundamentally transformed.** The bond between the subject of enunciation and the author-name is dissociated, creating a **fracture** between the surface of the author-function (classification, ownership, circulation) and its depth (subject of enunciation, creative subject, source of meaning). The author-function no longer stably resolves the question of 'who speaks.' Instead, its character shifts to managing the question of 'what circulates and how.'

**Third, Foucault's analytical framework must be expanded.** Foucault confined his analysis to the modern construct of the 'author,' but the concepts he instrumentalized — appropriation of discourse, dispersion of the subject, control of meaning — gain richer explanatory power when connected to the material conditions of media (Kittler), the technical exteriorization of memory (Stiegler), and the ontology of reproduction (Benjamin). Rie Kudan's *Tokyo Metropolitan Sympathy Tower* serves as a **cultural laboratory** demonstrating the necessity of such connections.

Ultimately, the most fundamental question posed by this incident is this: **When language is no longer an expression of the human subject, must the concept of the 'author' be reconstituted as a new ontological device for organizing the relationship between humans and machines?** Foucault's author-function provides the tools to raise this question, but the answer must be newly constructed under conditions Foucault could not have imagined — conditions where machines speak.
