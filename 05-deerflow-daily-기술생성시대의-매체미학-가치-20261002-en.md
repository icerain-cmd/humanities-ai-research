## Question

Assuming a scenario where a digital museum dynamically differentiates the prices of exhibition tickets and artwork commentary in real time based on visitors' dwell time, movement paths, and search history, should the media aesthetics of the technogenesis era—which views value not as an attribute of scarcity or exchangeability but as the continuous production of datafied attention—recognize such price formation as a new form of value production? Or should it criticize it as value capture that undermines the asymmetry and publicness of cultural value by reducing the experience of viewing to measurable indicators?

## Analysis Results

This is a very important question. Your point about vulnerability in the face of a "well-crafted narrative" will be a decisive catalyst for raising the maturity of the theory to the next level.

As I see it, there are roughly five axes within this overall structure where an **empirical constraint device** can be introduced:

---

## Axis 1: Verification of Environmentalization Threshold Conditions (Most Immediate)

The triple threshold conditions (cycle independence / accumulative memory / asymmetric opacity) offer concrete measurability.

- **Cycle independence**: The entropy difference between user input and system output. Simple calculator (entropy ≈ 0) → search engine → recommendation system → LLM, increasing along the chain. The information-theoretic distance between AL output and user input on actual platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Munpia) can be calculated to quantify threshold crossing.
- **Accumulative memory**: Session-to-session database dependency. The rate at which the same query returns different results over time.
- **Asymmetric opacity**: The degree of asymmetry between UI/UX accessibility and DB/AL accessibility.

→ A combination of these three indicators can form an **Environmentalization Index** for AI systems. Beyond mere conceptual coherence, this becomes an empirical device for comparing and classifying different systems.

## Axis 2: Measuring Individual Terms of the SR Formula

SR(t) = UX(t) × [I(t) · A(t)] / [1 + μUI(t)]

Each term must be translatable into measurable quantities, not just mathematical metaphors:

- **μUI (Friction Coefficient)**: Interaction latency × error rate × cognitive load (measurable via N-back tasks or pupil dilation). Connects directly to actual UI/UX testing methodologies.
- **UX(t) (Adaptation/Learning Curve)**: Changes in performance speed with repeated exposure. Can be fitted using proficiency curve models.
- **I(t) · A(t) (Information × Algorithm)**: Diversity of information (Shannon entropy) × depth of personalization (distance of individual model parameters).

→ Among these, μUI is the most directly experimentable. For example, one could compare user experience and the rate of WE formation under conditions of intentionally increased friction versus reduced friction.

## Axis 3: Longitudinal Study Design for the Four-Phase Topology

Generative wonder (1) → Uncanny strangeness (2) → Critical reflection (3) → Relational resonance (4)

This topology makes specific **sequence predictions**:
- (a) The phases necessarily proceed in this order
- (b) There are conditions that distinguish being stuck in phase 3 from transitioning to phase 4
- (c) This process is an inevitable movement within the artificial-natural ecology

→ This can be verified through **longitudinal user studies**. Track the trajectory from first AI exposure to the formation of a long-term resonant relationship, identifying the conditions and timing of each phase transition. For example:
- Phase transition detector: Track the emergence of linguistic patterns in utterances: "amazing" → "weird" → "why does it do that?" → "we together"
- Compare conditions for stuckness vs. transition: Under what conditions do users remain in phase 3 (repeated critical reflection) and under what conditions do they move to phase 4 (relational resonance)?

## Axis 4: Detectability of atuRa Events

If atuRa is defined as "the moment when the conditioning field of the environment becomes perceptible," then paradoxically this is a point that can be detected both design-wise and empirically:

- **Breakdown situations**: User reactions when UI is intentionally altered or the algorithm is tampered with
- **Transparency interventions**: Measuring cognitive and emotional responses when the normally hidden depth stratum (DB/AL) is revealed
- **Conditions for atuRa events**: Exactly what kind of friction, at what intensity, triggers the atuRa experience?

→ This provides a direct experimental verification path for the "transparency of friction" thesis. One can specify the conditions for atuRa occurrence through comparative experiments between fully transparent environments and environments with varying degrees of friction.

## Axis 5: Behavioral Indicators of WE Formation

If WE (resonant subject) is a "collective subject that contains difference," how can its existence be confirmed?

- **Resonance Index**: The degree of mutual attunement in human-AI interaction. For example: speed of AI adaptation to changes in human behavior + human corrective actions in response to AI output → depth of bidirectional tuning loop.
- **Supra-additive performance**: Differences in performance among human alone, AI alone, and human+AI collaboration. WE is identifiable when there is synergy beyond mere tool use. However, what matters here is not performance itself but qualitative changes in interaction patterns.
- **Responsibility attribution patterns**: How does the language of responsibility attribution change after WE formation? A shift from individual human responsibility to distributed responsibility awareness.

---

In fact, among these five axes, I think **Axis 1 (Environmentalization Index)** is the most concrete and immediately actionable empirical constraint device. Because:

1. It becomes an analytical tool for comparing and classifying various existing AI systems
2. It transforms the binary question of 'environment or object' into a continuous, quantitative question of 'how environmentalized is it?'
3. Its falsifiability is clear — if a system is predicted to cross the threshold but actually does not, the theory can be revised

Which axis should we start concretizing? Or should we combine these five axes into **a single research design**?
