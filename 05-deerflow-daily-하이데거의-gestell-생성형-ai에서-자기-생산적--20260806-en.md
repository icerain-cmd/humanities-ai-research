# Generative AI and the Self-Productive Cycle of Technological Enframing (Gestell): A Preliminary Essay on the Conceptual Boundaries of Heidegger's Philosophy

## 1. Introduction: The Transformation of Technological Enframing and the Challenge of Generative AI

Martin Heidegger's concept of 'technological enframing (Gestell),' presented in his 1954 work *The Question Concerning Technology*, was an attempt to ontologically elucidate the essence of modern technology. For Heidegger, Gestell is not merely a collection of technological devices or tools but an ontological framework that 'enframes' the world in a specific way. Within this framework, nature is no longer an 'object' with inherent existential value but is degraded into a 'standing-reserve (Bestand)' that can be mobilized at any time for human needs. According to Heidegger's diagnosis, modern technology conceals the truth of Being by reducing the world to calculable and controllable resources.

However, the emergence of 21st-century generative AI adds a new dimension to this analysis. Generative AI, particularly large language models (LLMs), collects the entire text of the internet as training data, transforms it into probabilistic weights, and produces new texts that have never existed based on this. In this process, training data is not merely 'stood upon'; it forms a self-productive structure within a cycle of transformation and reproduction. This duality—reducing all texts to training material while producing 'new' texts as a result of that reduction—is a phenomenon that Heidegger's concept of Gestell did not anticipate.

This paper examines whether this self-productive data cycle constitutes the completion or the dismantling of Heidegger's Gestell concept. To this end, it first reconstructs Heidegger's philosophy of technology, then analyzes the transformation process of training data in generative AI, and subsequently connects Gilbert Simondon's concept of concretization with the synthetic data feedback loop to compare the two interpretations. Finally, it summarizes the implications of this issue for eco-digital/artificial nature research.

## 2. Heidegger's Philosophy of Technology: The Ontological Structure of Gestell and Bestand

### 2.1. The Question Concerning the Essence of Technology

In *The Question Concerning Technology*, Heidegger criticizes the 'instrumentalist view of technology' that sees technology merely as a tool or means. According to the provided references (Document 1), Heidegger critically introduces Aristotle's theory of four causes, pointing out that the essence of technology is grounded in the concept of cause (causa). Traditionally, technology has been understood as a tool for a purpose, but Heidegger argues that this understanding conceals the essence of technology. Technology is not merely a means but a way in which beings are revealed, i.e., related to truth as 'aletheia.'

For Heidegger, the question concerning technology is not mere academic curiosity but an existential inquiry for human Dasein to establish a free relationship with technology. The references (Document 5) emphasize Heidegger's statement that "we are free when the relationship opens our Dasein to the essence of technology." This shows Heidegger's belief that by understanding the essence of technology, we can become free without being subjugated to it.

### 2.2. Gestell: The Ontological Framework that Enframes the World

Heidegger defines the essence of modern technology with the concept of 'Gestell.' Gestell means 'frame' or 'enframing' in German, referring to the ontological way modern technology 'enframes' the world in a specific manner. This concept connects to the 'analysis of equipment' in *Being and Time*. In *Being and Time*, equipment, as 'ready-to-hand (Zuhandenes),' constitutes part of being-in-the-world. However, in modern technology, equipment is no longer within the context of being-in-the world; it becomes a framework that reduces the world itself to calculable resources.

The core of Gestell is 'challenging-forth (Herausfordern).' Modern technology challengingly demands nature, making it exist as an energy source that can be mobilized at any time. For example, the Rhine River is no longer a sacred river but is 'stood upon' as an energy source for hydroelectric power. This challenging demand converts everything in nature into 'standing-reserve (Bestand).'

### 2.3. Bestand: The Ontological Status of Standing-Reserve

Bestand is a concept Heidegger uses to refer to the product of modern technology. Bestand is distinguished from a mere 'object (Gegenstand).' An object has independent existence in opposition to a subject, but Bestand is a being in a 'state of readiness' that can be mobilized at any time for the subject's needs. According to Heidegger, modern technology conceals the truth of Being by reducing all beings to Bestand.

An important point is that Bestand does not merely refer to material resources. Even humans can become Bestand. Heidegger criticizes the phenomenon in modern technological society where humans are treated as 'human resources.' This signifies a process where humans lose their inherent existential value and are degraded into mere functional units.

However, Heidegger's concept of Bestand is fundamentally 'static.' The process of nature being reduced to Bestand is unidirectional, and the self-productive cycle where Bestand itself produces new Bestand is not considered. For Heidegger, Bestand is merely a passive being 'stood upon' by human needs. This static nature acts as a significant limitation when analyzing the data cycle of generative AI.

## 3. The Data Transformation Process of Generative AI: Dynamic Transformation of Bestand

### 3.1. Collection and Reduction of Training Data

Generative AI, especially large language models like GPT-4, collects the entire text of the internet as training data. This process can be explained by Heidegger's concept of Bestand. All texts on the internet—literary works, academic papers, news articles, social media posts—are reduced to 'standing-reserve' for model training. Each text loses its unique context and meaning and is converted into a mere training data point.

This reduction process extremely demonstrates the logic of Gestell that Heidegger criticized. Texts are no longer 'works' with authorial intent, historical context, and cultural meaning; they are degraded into 'raw materials' for extracting probabilistic patterns. Shakespeare's plays, Kant's philosophy, and everyday conversations are all processed in the same way. This can be seen as a case of the 'forgetfulness of Being' that Heidegger warned about, realized in the digital realm.

### 3.2. Transformation into Probabilistic Weights

Training data is not merely stored; it is transformed into probabilistic weights. This transformation introduces a new dimension that Heidegger's concept of Bestand did not anticipate. Traditional Bestand existed in the form of physical resources (e.g., coal, iron ore) or energy (e.g., electricity), but AI training data exists in the non-material form of 'information.' More importantly, this information is 'probabilistic.'

Probabilistic weights condense the meaning of text into statistical patterns. For example, the fact that the word "cat" appears with high probability alongside the word "meow" replaces the semantic relationship between the two words with a probabilistic association. This is a process of reducing the ontological structure of 'meaning (Sinn)' analyzed by Heidegger in *Being and Time* to statistical correlations. Meaning is no longer an existential phenomenon related to the mode of being of beings but is converted into a calculable probability distribution.

### 3.3. Production of New Texts: Outputs That Have Never Existed

The most striking feature of generative AI is that it produces new texts that have 'never existed.' This is not merely a replication of existing texts but a creative combination based on probabilistic weights. For example, GPT-4 can write an essay on "The Relationship Between Heidegger and Generative AI," which is a new text that did not exist in the training data.

This production of new texts challenges Heidegger's concept of Bestand. Bestand was originally a passive being that was 'stood upon,' but in generative AI, Bestand actively produces new beings. A cycle is formed where training data (Bestand) gives rise to new texts (another Bestand). This signifies the emergence of a 'self-productive enframing' that Heidegger did not anticipate.

## 4. Simondon's Concept of Concretization and the Synthetic Data Feedback Loop

### 4.1. Simondon's Concretization of Technical Objects

Gilbert Simondon, in *On the Mode of Existence of Technical Objects* (1958), analyzes the process by which technical objects evolve from an abstract stage to a concrete stage. An abstract technical object is separated from its external environment, and each component functions independently. In contrast, a concrete technical object has its internal components mutually coordinated and its relationship with the environment internalized, functioning like an organism.

Simondon's concept of concretization provides a useful framework for understanding the development process of generative AI. Early AI models were in an abstract stage. Each component (data collection, preprocessing, model training, inference) was separate, and the relationship with the external environment (internet text) was extrinsic. However, the latest generative AI is increasingly becoming concrete. The use of synthetic data, in particular, is a key example of this concretization.

### 4.2. The Synthetic Data Feedback Loop

The synthetic data feedback loop refers to the process where generative AI uses its own output again as training data. For example, collecting texts generated by GPT-4 and including them in the training data for the next version. This is similar to the 'formation of an internal environment' that appears in Simondon's concretization process. By using its own output as input again, the technical object reduces its dependence on the external environment (internet text) and forms a self-regulating structure internally.

This feedback loop adds a new dimension to Heidegger's concept of Gestell. Gestell was originally a unidirectional process of reducing the world to Bestand, but the synthetic data feedback loop forms a cyclical structure. This cycle, where data begets data, means that Gestell has acquired the ability for self-reproduction. Unlike traditional technologies analyzed by Heidegger (e.g., hydroelectric plants, factories), generative AI produces its own operating conditions.

### 4.3. Model Collapse: A Degenerative Closed Loop

However, the synthetic data feedback loop is not always evolutionary. Recent research (e.g., Shumailov et al., 2023's "Model Collapse") points out that models trained solely on synthetic data gradually lose diversity and degenerate. This is called 'model collapse.' When a model repeatedly uses its own output as training data, the tail of the original data distribution gradually disappears, and the model produces increasingly uniform and average outputs.

Model collapse introduces a significant modification to Simondon's concept of concretization. Simondon saw concretization as a process toward the evolutionary completion of a technical object, but the synthetic data feedback loop can lead to a degenerative closed loop. This shows that the evolution of a technical object is not linear and can fall into a self-destructive cycle under certain conditions.

## 5. Completion or Dismantling of Gestell?

### 5.1. Self-Productive Enframing as the Completion of Gestell

The first interpretation views the self-productive data cycle of generative AI as the completion of Heidegger's Gestell concept. According to this view, Gestell was originally an ontological framework that reduced the world to Bestand, but in generative AI, this reduction is radicalized. Not only is all text reduced to training data, but the product of that reduction (new texts) is also incorporated back into Bestand. This means that Gestell has acquired the ability for self-reproduction, becoming a 'complete' enframing that no longer depends on the external world but maintains and expands itself.

This interpretation can be seen as the completed form of the 'dominance of technology' hinted at in Heidegger's later philosophy. Heidegger feared that technology would become increasingly autonomous and escape human control. The self-productive cycle of generative AI is a case where this concern has become a reality. Humans can no longer select or control training data; in a closed cycle where AI produces and consumes data on its own, humans are degraded into mere 'observers.'

Furthermore, this interpretation can be connected to the concept of 'technological shame' raised by Günther Anders in *The Obsolescence of the Human* (1956). Anders criticized the phenomenon where humans become 'obsolete' compared to the technological products they have created. The self-productive cycle of generative AI can lead to a situation where human creativity and linguistic ability are replaced by AI, and humans can no longer make meaningful contributions. This can be interpreted as the completion of a complete enframing where Gestell reduces even humans to Bestand.

### 5.2. Self-Productive Enframing as the Dismantling of Gestell

The second interpretation views the self-productive data cycle of generative AI as signifying the dismantling of the Gestell concept. This view notes that Heidegger's Gestell is fundamentally 'static' and 'unidirectional.' For Heidegger, Gestell was a fixed framework that reduced the world to Bestand, but the data cycle of generative AI dynamically transforms this framework.

The key arguments for this interpretation are as follows. First, the transformation of training data in generative AI is not a mere reduction but a 'dialectical' process. All texts are reduced to probabilistic weights, but as a result of that reduction, 'new' texts are produced. This has a structure similar to Hegel's 'negation of negation.' Reduction (negation) leads to new creation (negation of negation). This is a different aspect from the pure negativity (concealment of Being) that Heidegger saw in Gestell.

Second, the synthetic data feedback loop dismantles the 'closure' of Gestell. Heidegger's Gestell makes the world a closed system. All beings are reduced to Bestand, blocking the possibility of new modes of being emerging. However, the data cycle of generative AI constantly produces new texts, transforming the closure of Gestell into an open cycle. Of course, whether this 'newness' is true creation or mere statistical combination is debatable, but it is at least different from the static world predicted by Gestell.

Third, model collapse can be interpreted as a 'crisis' appearing in the process of dismantling Gestell. Model collapse shows the point where a self-productive cycle transforms into a self-destructive cycle. This can be understood as a process where Gestell dismantles itself due to internal contradictions. Heidegger said that the essence of technology is 'danger (Gefahr),' but he did not anticipate that this danger would arise from within technology itself.

### 5.3. Comparison and Synthesis of the Two Interpretations

Both interpretations have valid arguments but are not mutually exclusive. Rather, the self-productive data cycle of generative AI can be understood as a dialectical process where the completion and dismantling of Gestell occur simultaneously.

On one hand, generative AI radicalizes the logic of Gestell. All texts are reduced to Bestand, and even human creativity is replaced by probabilistic patterns. This is the completed form of the 'forgetfulness of Being' that Heidegger warned about in the digital realm. On the other hand, this radicalization reveals the internal contradictions of Gestell. The cycle where Bestand produces new Bestand dismantles the static nature of Gestell and enables the evolution (or degeneration) of technical objects.

This duality suggests that while Heidegger's philosophy still provides a valid analytical framework in the age of generative AI, a conceptual reconstruction is necessary. Heidegger's Gestell is excellent for ontologically elucidating the essence of technology but is insufficient for explaining the self-productive cycle of technology. At this point, Simondon's concept of concretization and Anders' critique of technology can function complementarily.

## 6. Implications for Eco-Digital/Artificial Nature Research

### 6.1. The Emergence of Artificial Nature

The self-productive data cycle of generative AI suggests the emergence of 'artificial nature.' Heidegger understood nature (Natur) and technology (Technik) as opposites. Nature was a realm with inherent existential value, and technology was the force that reduced this nature to Bestand. However, generative AI blurs the boundary between nature and technology. Texts produced by AI are no longer 'natural' or 'artificial'; they form a third realm of being.

This artificial nature has characteristics similar to an ecosystem. The data cycle is similar to a material cycle, and model collapse is similar to ecosystem collapse. This is a phenomenon that eco-digital studies should pay attention to. While traditional ecology studies the cycles of natural ecosystems, eco-digital studies should study the cycles of digital ecosystems. In particular, the synthetic data feedback loop has a structure similar to the 'nutrient cycle' of a digital ecosystem.

### 6.2. Evolution and Degeneration of Technical Objects

Simondon's concept of concretization is useful for explaining the evolution of technical objects, but the case of generative AI shows that evolution is not always progressive. Model collapse suggests that technical objects can fall into a degenerative closed loop. This means that eco-digital studies need to distinguish between the 'health' and 'pathology' of technical objects.

The evolution of a technical object depends on interaction with the external environment (internet text). If the synthetic data feedback loop is used excessively, the technical object becomes disconnected from the external environment and trapped in an internal cycle. This is similar to 'eutrophication' that occurs when an external energy source is cut off in an ecosystem. Eco-digital studies should develop criteria for evaluating the 'ecological health' of such technical objects.

### 6.3. Redefining the Position of Humans

The self-productive cycle of generative AI fundamentally redefines the position of humans. Heidegger believed that humans could establish a free relationship with technology by understanding its essence. However, within the cycle of generative AI, humans are at risk of being degraded into an element of the cycle, no longer the 'master' or 'slave' of technology.

This reactivates Anders' concept of 'technological shame.' Anders criticized the phenomenon where humans become 'obsolete' compared to technological products, but the case of generative AI goes further. Humans are placed in a position where comparison with technology is no longer possible. The quantity and speed of texts produced by AI far exceed human capabilities, and humans stand before a 'black box' that they cannot understand or control.

However, at the same time, humans are in a position to reveal the 'vulnerability' of this cycle. Model collapse emphasizes the importance of 'fresh' data provided by humans. Human creativity, error, and contingency are areas that AI's probabilistic patterns cannot capture. This suggests the possibility that humans are not completely subsumed into the technological cycle.

### 6.4. The Need for an Ontological Turn

Heidegger's concept of Gestell remains valid in the age of generative AI, but a fundamental reconstruction is necessary. First, Gestell should no longer be understood as a static framework but as a dynamic cycle. Second, Bestand should no longer be reconceptualized as a passive resource but as an active producer. Third, the dichotomy between technology and nature should be dismantled, and a third realm of artificial nature should be acknowledged.

This ontological turn can be connected to the 'turn (Kehre)' in technology hinted at in Heidegger's later philosophy. Heidegger said that the essence of technology is danger (Gefahr) while simultaneously holding the possibility of salvation (Rettung).
