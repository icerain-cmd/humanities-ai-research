### 1. The Affect-Reactivity Condition of Platform Capitalism: The Infrastructure of 'Right Now'

Platform capitalism is not simply a system that collects big data, but a machine that commodifies temporality (time-ness) itself. TikTok's infinite scroll, Instagram's stories, YouTube's Shorts — all operate within a violent temporal compression of '15-60 seconds of playback time.' This is not merely a matter of content format, but an Atura-like pre-formative arrangement that preconfigures the temporal conditions of affect.

The key point is that this operates not at the level of 'conscious choice,' but at the level of the sensory-motor schema. The act of pressing the 'like' button is not the result of careful judgment, but an almost reflexive response to stimuli. Here, affect arrives before cognition, reaction arrives before deliberation.

### 2. The Sensory Ethics of 'Slow Information': Reconfiguring Temporality

Slow Information responds to this condition by reconfiguring the temporality of the senses. Slow Information is not simply 'reading slowly,' but a multilayered intervention:

**2.1 Rhythm Shift of Information**
It breaks the rapid cycle of affect-reaction and inserts 'time to dwell' in information. This is an intervention that changes not the content of information, but the way information reaches the senses (the temporal conditions of the medium). Examples: reading newsletters only once a day, blocking all notifications, accessing social media only at set times.

**2.2 Restoring Sensory Asymmetry**
Platforms enforce sensory symmetry — the system is designed so that when a stimulus arrives, an immediate response is triggered. Slow Information breaks this symmetry, creating a 'gap' between stimulus and response. In this gap, critical reflection becomes possible. This is the core work of the techno-editing era — editing and recontextualizing the conditions of technology.

**2.3 Sensory Ethics as Aesthetic Attunement**
Sensory ethics is not an individual moral resolution, but an aesthetic response to medial conditions. It is a practice of refusing the speed imposed by platforms and attuning to a different tempo, a different rhythm, a different sensory grain. This differs from simple 'digital detox.' Detox is avoidance; the sensory ethics of Slow Information is re-articulation — not leaving technological conditions entirely, but creating the possibility of different sensations upon those conditions.

### 3. Transition to Collective Intervention: The Political Economy of the Senses

Here we return to the core question: Does Slow Information remain at the level of individual sensory management, or can it lead to collective intervention that changes the operating conditions of the platform itself?

**3.1 The Limits of Individual Practice: The Paradox of 'Commodified Resistance'**
If Slow Information remains an individual practice, platform capitalism can absorb it as just another consumption style. 'Digital minimalism' and 'ascetic tech use' have already been commodified as trendy lifestyles. Turning off notifications does not prevent the platform from collecting data, and reading slowly does not change how recommendation algorithms operate.

**3.2 Conditions for Collective Intervention: Building 'Slow Infrastructure'**
For Slow Information to become a genuine collective intervention, the following conditions are necessary:

1. Construction of alternative technical infrastructure: Going beyond individual 'slow consumption' to create alternative technical conditions that enable slow information. Examples: decentralized information sharing without central servers, platforms without notification structures (Are.na, Pinboard, etc.), time-limited messaging systems (Slow Letter).

2. Formation of a collective Sensory Commons: A group that shares the practice of Slow Information transforms from a mere consumer group into a community that collectively reconfigures sensory conditions. This is the constitution of a new WE — a moment of relational resonance where humans and technological conditions are reconstituted together.

3. Counter-politics against the affect economy: Collective affect experiments that resist the way platforms collect and valorize affect. Examples: a slow information group collectively 'falling silent' during specific time periods, proposing depth of information rather than speed as a new value metric.

**3.3 Mechanism of Condition Change: From Micro to Macro**
The mechanism that leads individual sensory management to collective condition change is not 'scale-up' but 'knotting':
- Network effects where individual sensory practices connect to each other
- These connections generate non-visible data for the platform, disrupting the learning of recommendation algorithms
- Collective practice injects 'noise' into the platform's user behavior model
- When this noise accumulates beyond a certain threshold, the platform is forced to recalibrate its operating conditions

This is not a revolutionary overthrow of the platform from outside, but a strategy of transforming conditions from within, while operating within the platform's own conditions.

### 4. Slow Information as Re-editing of Atura Conditions

The Atura concept from Document 3 can be applied here. The practice of Slow Information is:
- An attempt to change not the content of experience, but the conditions of experience
- An attempt to intervene not in the surface of events, but in the arrangement that makes events possible
- That is, a meta-intervention that makes Atura's pre-formative formatting operate in a different direction

This is why Slow Information becomes a media-aesthetic intervention rather than a mere lifestyle improvement.

### Conclusion: Possible — But Conditional

Can the sensory ethics of Slow Information move beyond individual sensory management to become a collective intervention that changes the platform's operating conditions? — Yes, but only when the following conditions are met:

1. Slow Information must be theorized as an aesthetic practice, understood as a reconfiguration of the senses rather than moral asceticism
2. Individual practice must be connected to the construction of alternative infrastructure
3. Practitioners of Slow Information must organize not as mere consumers but as a sensory community
4. This community must develop a collective strategy of injecting noise and disruption into the platform's affect economy

When these conditions are met, Slow Information can become a counter-micro-physics against the micro-physics of platform capitalism. It is not a frontal confrontation with a massive system, but a collective practice of reconfiguring conditions from the most minute point of affect — that brief pause of not reacting 'right now' — onward.

This is precisely why Eco-Digital's sensory ethics is located not as a mere individual ethics, but at the juncture of technology, sensation, and politics.
