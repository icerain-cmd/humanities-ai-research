## Question

Applying Walter Benjamin's "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction" thesis to technological production in the generative AI era, how does the aura transform from a matter of 'reproduction' to one of 'generativity itself'?

## Analysis Results

# The Transition of the Aura — The Collapse of the 'Reproduction' Frame and the Eventhood of Generativity

## 1. Problem Setting: Why Benjamin's Thesis Cannot Be Applied 'As Is'

Benjamin's aura is essentially a **concept of status within the regime of reproduction**. As photography and film enabled the copying of originals, the "here and now" (*hier und jetzt*)—the unique existence of the original—became the point of distinction from copies, and the aura referred to what sustained that distinction: the unique presence in time and space, the testimonial quality (authenticity) embedded in tradition and ritual. The aura debate has always taken the form of **"the copy damages the aura of the original."**

However, with generative AI, this very frame collapses. The generated output is not a copy of any original. Just as a photograph is not an index of a pre-existing object in the way a negative is developed, the output is a sampling from a probability distribution, not a rubbing, copy, or reproduction. In Baudrillard's terms, it approaches the status of a "copy without an original" ([citation:Algorithmic Aura and Spiritual Alienation](http://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/uploads/vol10-iss6-pg6876-6885-202606_pdf.pdf)), and thus the very distinction between "original vs. copy" loses its ground. As Presti points out, **since the outputs of generative systems are by definition unique each time**, the problem of reproduction that the reproduction regime assumed is no longer a threat to the aura ([citation:The Aura in the Machine](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17940v1)).

The question then becomes precise: the aura has not disappeared, but **"the aura of what" has changed**. This opens up the core of the user's question: if the aura was a matter of reproduction (originality), it now transforms into a matter of **generativity itself**. This transformation can be formulated along three axes.

## 2. The Three Axes of Transformation

**(1) Uniqueness of the object → Uniqueness of the generative event.** Presti argues that the Benjaminian aura does not disappear in the generative age but "condenses onto the productive system." That is, the coordinate of uniqueness shifts from the individual work (the output) to **the system and process itself**. To this end, he proposes a third ontological status that transcends the original/copy dichotomy: **"manifestation."** The outputs of a generative system are "not originals because infinitely many equivalent instances exist, and not copies because they do not reproduce a pre-existing object"—they are manifestations of a process ([citation:The Aura in the Machine](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17940v1)). As precedents in generative art (Ballestrini's *Tristano*, Eno's *Music for Airports*, Tinguely's Metamatics) already show, the artistic substance here is not the output but the **space of possibilities that made generation possible**.

**(2) Authenticity (originality) → Verifiability of generative conditions.** In the reproduction regime, authenticity meant "was this object actually made at that productive moment?" In the generative regime, the corresponding question is **"under what generative conditions was this produced?"**—the model, prompt, temperature, seed, fine-tuning data, and curation. As Bar-Gil notes, generative AI works lack the traditional markers of the aura as defined by Benjamin (material historical immanence, spatiotemporal uniqueness, individual creation), yet occupy an ambiguous position where "the distinction between copy and original becomes blurred" ([citation:The transformation of artistic creation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-025-02432-5)). What is traceable beyond this ambiguity is not the physical history of the object but the **configuration of the generative pipeline**. The locus of testimony shifts from "is this real?" to "from what moment in what possibility-space did this output emerge?"

**(3) Ritual value → Value of encounter with possibility.** In Benjamin, the aura is rooted in ritual, and reproduction destroys it by dragging it into politics. In the generative regime, the point of aura emergence is no longer ritual but **the generative encounter as event**—the very moment when latent space meets a specific context, prompt, and judgment. Interestingly, this intersects precisely with the local literature's "four stages of AI-human contact" argument: **"generative wonder" is described as an affect prior to aesthetic judgment, an immediate resonance** (Document 1, score 0.1008). This affective openness operating before aesthetic judgment is precisely the "ritual moment" of the new age. When Benjamin defined the aura as "a unique phenomenon of distance, however close it may be," that distance is now not the distance of physical matter but **the distance before the abyss of possibility**.

## 3. Core Proposition: Aura = The Eventhood of Generativity

Converging the three axes above, we can formulate:

> **The aura in the generative age is no longer 'the marker distinguishing the original from the copy,' but 'the non-repeatable eventhood of the encounter with possibility.'**

The grounds are as follows:

- **Why event?** Benjamin's aura was ultimately a perceptual phenomenon ("appearance," *Erscheinung*). To shift the bearer of the aura from object to process, what must be perceptible is not the process itself but **the event of encounter**—the emergent moment when the space of possibilities meets a situated interpreter. Since fixing the prompt, seed, and temperature can reproduce identical outputs, statistical uniqueness at the output level alone cannot ground the aura. **This very vulnerability logically forces the transition from "object to relation/event."** What is unreproducible is not the output but the encounter itself—the meeting of a specific latent space with a specific situation, attention, and judgment. This is precisely what the user's question's "generativity itself" points to.

- **Why possibility?** Borrowing Presti's ontological distinction, generative systems are, in Goodman's terms, **allographic**—the code is executable notation and the output is its execution. But unlike correct performances of a symphony being notationally equivalent, executions of a generative system produce different objects each time. That is, the aura attaches to **what is not identical to the notation—processuality itself**. Here, the claim that "manifestation" as a third status transcends original/copy means that **the coordinate of the aura has shifted from the status of the output to the nature of generativity** ([citation:The Aura in the Machine](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17940v1)).

## 4. A New Form of Decline: 'Algorithmic Repetition' = The New Aura Loss

With the core proposition established, the Benjaminian critical function is also reconstructed at new coordinates. **Not all generation is auratic.** Presti defines the phenomenon of alignment/RLHF over-stabilized models amplifying only the center of the distribution as **'Algorithmic Repetition'**: such models, "without making copies, become copies of cultural processes at a higher level"—copies of repetition itself. Auratic generation occurs rather through **noise, failure, and deviation** (the Baron couple's circuit torture, DeepDream, older models with instability as aesthetic freedom). This is explained by Boden's distinction: while AI excels at combinational creativity, transformational creativity—breaking the rules themselves—is currently structurally impossible—Model Collapse being the evidence—"one cannot add dimensions that do not exist." Thus, the condition for preserving the aura lies **where generation exceeds statistical expectation, where the space of possibilities actually expands**; generation that fails to do so is a new aura loss in the name of repetition ([citation:The Aura in the Machine](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17940v1)). Just as Benjamin diagnosed the destruction of the aura against aestheticized politics, the target of critique now becomes **the point where generative apparatuses amplifying the center of the distribution become apparatuses of power**.

## 6. Distinguishing Limitations

**Limitations of search and evidence.** (a) The Khoj RAG similarity scores for local documents are low (0.10–0.13), and the documents are lecture notes and educational cases, not academic texts directly discussing Benjamin. Therefore, the theoretical construction in Sections 1, 2, and 5 is my interpretive connection to these documents, not conclusions based on the documents themselves. (b) The key reference, Presti (2026), is an arXiv preprint, not a peer-reviewed journal publication; Park Sun-ah (2024), Bar-Gil (2025), and Fernández-Castrillo (2023) are peer-reviewed but I have only verified abstracts and excerpts, not full texts ([citation:AI & SOCIETY | Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-025-02432-5)). (c) Existing literature generally covers up to "the aura condenses onto the system" (Presti and Ambrosini's 'algorithmic aura'), and **the formulation "aura = generativity itself" is my theoretical attempt that goes one step beyond the current literature**. This remains both a strength of the answer and a falsifiable claim.

**Theoretical issues inherent in the question itself.** (a) *The possibility of a processual aura*: If Benjamin defined the aura as a perceptual phenomenon, the process cannot be perceived as such. The answer relies on an event ontology—"what is perceived is not the process but the event of encounter"—which is an open issue requiring more refined conceptual work. (b) *Aura inflation*: If all generation is auratic, the aura becomes meaningless. The safeguard against this is the diagnosis of 'algorithmic repetition' in Section 4—preserving the critical function of the aura is the responsibility of this formulation. (c) *Seed reproducibility*: The fact that identical outputs can be reproduced with fixed seeds and low-temperature sampling weakens the premise that "every generated object is unique," and thus forces the aura to be located in relations and events rather than objects. This is not so much a limitation as **the very reason the question was necessary**.

## 7. Conclusion

> In the reproduction regime, the aura was a matter of **"whether the here-and-now of the original is testified in the copy."** In the generative regime, it transforms into a matter of **"whether the encounter with the space of possibilities is experienced as eventhood irreducible to repetition."** If reproduction destroyed the aura, what now destroys it is no longer reproduction but **repetition**—generation that amplifies the center of the distribution, adds no dimensions, and erases unfamiliarity and deviation. Conversely, what preserves the aura is **the generative encounter that actually expands the space of possibilities**—the event in which "generativity itself" manifests non-repetitively at every moment. Thus, the problem of the aura is reborn from a question of aesthetic status into an ethical-political question of production: **whether the generative regime expands or contracts the space of possibilities.**
