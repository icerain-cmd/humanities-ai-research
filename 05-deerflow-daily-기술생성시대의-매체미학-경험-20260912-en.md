## Question

When location information, vibration, and group synchronization on a smartphone screen constitute players' on-site experience in Pokémon GO's augmented reality raids, can Walter Benjamin's concept of 'aura' explain the restoration of the place's uniqueness and co-presence, or does it reveal the paradox that the platform's designed repeatable participation routes erase the uniqueness of experience?

## Analysis Results

## Pokémon GO Augmented Reality Raids and Benjamin's Aura — Structural Analysis of the Paradox

---

### 1. Precise Positioning of the Question

First, we must clarify the premise of the question. The question you posed is **not a binary choice**. That is, it is not a matter of choosing between 'aura is restored' and 'aura is erased,' but rather a problem revealing the structure of a paradox in which the platform accelerates the erasure of aura precisely by replicating it. My judgment: the latter is a deeper analytical path. However, it is not a simple 'erasure,' but a more complex dialectical situation: the **simulacral reconstruction of aura**.

---

### 2. Core Dimensions of Benjamin's Concept of Aura — Particular Points to Note When Applying to AR Experiences

Benjamin's aura does not converge into superficial definitions such as mere 'uniqueness' or 'here and now.' Three layers operate in an overlapping manner:

**(a) Phenomenology of Distance:** "Aura is the unique appearance of a distance, however near it may be." This connects to the core of cult value as **unapproachability**. The moment aura operates is when the object is not fully captured but maintains a certain distance. This gives rise to the first paradox.

**(b) Embedding in Tradition:** The aura of an original work arises from the fact that it is embedded in the context of tradition. That is, a work acquires aura not merely as a physical object, but when it stands upon a stratum of ritualistic and historical continuity.

**(c) Unique Appearance (das einmalige Erscheinen):** Aura is a singular, non-repeatable event. When reproductive technology breaks this singularity, aura 'withers' (verkümmert).

---

### 3. Experience Structure of Pokémon GO Raids — Analysis of Technical Components

The three elements pointed out in the question — **location information (GPS), tactile feedback (vibration), and group synchronization** — intersect with the aura issue in different ways.

**(1) GPS-based location information:** Raids are fixed to specific Gym coordinates. Players must physically arrive at that location to participate. This clearly satisfies the condition of 'here and now.' But simultaneously, **that location is merely a 'Point of Interest' (PoI) that countless other players repeatedly visit under identical conditions**. This place, nothing more than a coordinate registered in Niantic's database, lacks the context of tradition that Benjamin spoke of.

**(2) Tactile feedback (vibration):** The countdown of the raid start timer, vibrations responding to the raid boss's attack patterns — these directly integrate the player's body into the experience. According to Liberati's research (2017/2018), in AR games, digital objects form a new 'hybrid body' between the player's actual physical body and the world. Vibration is the most primitive layer of this bodily sensation. However, the problem is that this vibration is a **repeatable feedback loop determined by algorithms**.

**(3) Group synchronization:** The experience of 20 players simultaneously attacking the same raid boss generates a powerful sense of 'co-presence.' Research by Bhattacharya et al. (2019) reports that Pokémon GO raids fluctuate between ad-hoc groups and planned groups, creating improvised social bonds. This can be read as a **momentary reconstruction of ritual community**.

---

### 4. The Argument for the Restoration of Aura — But Its Limitations

**The restoration argument:** Pokémon GO raids summon players to a specific time and place. This condition of 'here and now' restores the uniqueness of place beyond the placelessness of digital reproduction. The simultaneous experience created by group synchronization reproduces the aura of an original ritual community. Liberati argued that AR brings digital objects back into the everyday world, reviving the on-site nature of experience that had been exiled beyond the screen.

**Fatal flaw of this argument:**

- The place of a Pokémon GO raid is **merely a coordinate record in a database**. The next day, a different raid boss appears at the same coordinates. The singularity of 'here and now' is replaced by a **pre-planned periodicity** determined by the raid boss rotation cycle. It is not the fateful, unpredictable appearance that aura presupposes, but the repetition of a schedule set by the game master (GM).

- 'Co-presence' is a synchronized interface event, not a genuine communal ritual. As Adorno pointed out (see Reference 1), the 'fun' of the culture industry is not a pure state of tension relief and surrender to meaninglessness, but already takes the **form of a commodity subsumed under exchange value**. The group experience of a raid is merely simultaneity mediated by algorithms, not standing upon the continuity of tradition that Benjamin spoke of.

---

### 5. Core Structure of the Paradox — Simulacral Reconstruction of Aura

Here lies the precise structure of the paradox:

> **Pokémon GO simulates the conditions of aura (fixity of place, singularity of time, collective appearance), but these conditions are designed from the outset by the platform's repeatable participation routes. Therefore, this experience shows the 'phenomenon' of aura but lacks the 'ontological conditions' of aura.**

This is precisely the **simulacral reconstruction of aura**. This is not to say it is 'fake' in the sense of not being genuine aura. Rather, it is more complex: this experience produces the effect of aura, but the foundation of that effect lies in the platform's database and algorithms.

The concept of 'reproductive aura' from Reference 4 provides important insight here. In the context of design, 'reproductive aura' is not about the complete destruction of the original's aura through reproduction, but rather about **how reproductive technology activates the subject's unconscious memory (forgotten human elements)** . What operates in Pokémon GO raids is not aura itself, but the **reproductive-technological satisfaction of an unconscious longing for aura**.

That is:

1. Players believe they experience the **uniqueness of place** in a raid, but that uniqueness is an effect produced by the precision of GPS coordinates.
2. They feel they experience **co-presence**, but that simultaneity is the result of server-client synchronization algorithms.
3. They experience **singularity**, but that singularity is only allowed within the periodic frame guaranteed by the boss appearance timer.

### 7. Conclusion: Dialectics Between Momentary Restoration and Ontological Erasure of Aura

Returning to the question, the answer is:

**(1) The concept of aura can explain certain aspects of the raid experience.** In particular, the fact that players are 'summoned' to a specific time and place and appear together with others forms an analogy with the dimension of aura's 'unique appearance.' The formation of the 'hybrid body' pointed out by Liberati bodily restores the sense of here-and-now.

**(2) However, this explanation is immediately constrained by the platform's designed repeatability.** The ontological conditions of aura (embedding in tradition, genuine singularity, distance as unapproachability) are absent from the raid experience. The aura of distance that is "however near it may be, yet far" is difficult to operate within Pokémon GO's spatial structure, where every coordinate can be fully approached via GPS.

**(3) Final judgment: A 'momentary restoration' of aura does occur, but the core paradox is that this restoration itself is structured by the platform's repeatability.** That is, Pokémon GO raids thematize the disappearance of aura by replicating it, and commodify nostalgia for that disappearance. This is a digital variation of the path Benjamin foresaw in *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction*: a self-referential structure in which reproductive technology not only destroys aura but also reproduces and circulates the longing for the destroyed aura.

---

This analysis affirms the 'incompatible tension' already presupposed by the question, while suggesting that this tension is not a simple opposition but possesses a **dialectical cycle (restoration of aura conceals erasure, and erasure reproduces longing for restoration)** . This structure extends beyond the individual case of Pokémon GO to general implications for how 'authenticity of experience' is technically produced and consumed across location-based media.
