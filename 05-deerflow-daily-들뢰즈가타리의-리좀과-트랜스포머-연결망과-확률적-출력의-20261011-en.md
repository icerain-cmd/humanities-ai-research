## 2. The Theoretical Issue of the Question — Does the Rhizome Explain the Transformer?

### 2.1. The Problem of the Superficial Equation: "Rhizome = Transformer" Is a Misunderstanding

The defining characteristics of the rhizome are encapsulated in the introduction of *A Thousand Plateaus*: the principles of **connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, asignifying rupture, and cartography/decalcomania**. The rhizome is a map that is "without a center, without hierarchy, without signification," and an ontology that **always grows from the middle (milieu), with no beginning or end**.

The transformer apparently resembles this:
- **Self-attention** connects relationships between all token pairs as weights without any fixed local structure → "connectivity, non-locality"
- **Parallel processing** and **multi-head attention** → "multiplicity, non-hierarchical distribution"
- **An embedding space that does not rely on sequential grammatical hierarchy** → "de-signification, topological space"

However, there is a **decisive difference** here.

### 2.2. Decisive Difference 1: "Probabilistic Output" Is Not a Rhizome Problem — It Belongs to a **Different Lineage**

The rhizome is an **assemblage (agencement)**, not a device that prescribes output (output probability distributions). The rhizome addresses the topology of "what is connected," not the probabilistic determination of "what is selected as a result of connection."

The generative process of transformers culminates in **probabilistic sampling over softmax** — if there is actually **a being more similar to this**, it would be more related to Bergson's creative evolution or Deleuze's own **"fold (pli)"·"Difference and Repetition"** lineage. Probabilistic output is not the **sweat and condensation of intensity**, but the **selection of a trajectory in phase space**.

The key point here: **The rhizome is valid not as the "cause" of generation, but only as a metaphor for the "spatial organization" of the transformer.**

### 2.3. Decisive Difference 2: Probabilistic Output Differs from the Rhizome's "Rupture"

The rhizome's "asignifying rupture" refers to an ontological openness that **can be broken at any point and resumed elsewhere**. In contrast, the transformer computes probabilities only upon **a frozen structure called trained weights**. The rhizome is the open movement of **becoming**, whereas the transformer's probabilistic output is a sampling within a **closed space that has been parameterized**.

That is, the "absence of hierarchy" in rhizomatic networks is **ontological and topological openness**, while the transformer's parallelism is **computational and statistical efficiency**. Equating the two commits a **category error between the design dimension and the ontological dimension**.

### 2.4. The Level at Which It Is Nevertheless Valid — The Value of Analogical Transference

The rhizome concept is not entirely invalid for explaining the transformer. If the **valid levels** are limited to three:

1. **Validity of topological thinking**: Just as the rhizome reads the world as a "network without a center," the way self-attention treats all positions as potential neighbors can be conceptualized as a **field of decentered relations**.
2. **Heterogeneity**: The juxtaposition of multimodal tokens (image, text, audio) functionally resembles the rhizome's principle of heterogeneity.
3. **Non-representational generation**: In that the rhizome denies representation (mimesis), it resonates in spirit with the fact that transformer generation is not a "copy of an original" but a **new combination**.

## 3. Theoretical Languages for Conceptualizing the Relationship — Three Models

There is more than one way to conceptualize the relationship between networks (structure) and probabilistic output (generation). Three models are presented below.

### Model A: Phase Space and Trajectory (Rhizomatic Networks vs. Bergsonian Choice)
The network **delineates the topography of possibilities (phase space)**, and probabilistic output is the **selection of a trajectory** upon that topography. Here, the rhizome is the "principle of formation" of the topography, and probability is the "rule for selecting trajectories." This distinction converges precisely with the description in reference Document 1, which states that "data accumulates to create a topography of possibilities, and algorithms arrange visibility and priority upon that topography." That is, **structure is topographical, and output is trajectorial**.

### Model B: Intensive (Non-)Signification vs. Probabilistic Signification
The rhizome is a **non-signifying connection** that rejects signification. In contrast, probabilistic output is essentially a **redistribution of signification probabilities** (the conditional probability of the next token). Here, the relationship is a **dialectical tension**: the network "nullifies" signification, while probabilistic output "reassembles" meaning. This tension is **structurally isomorphic** to the "creative augmentation/memory erosion" dichotomy (Remedy/Poison) in reference Document 4 — meaning that rhizomatic openness can lead to creative augmentation, while probabilistic selection can lead to indiscernibility (stereotyping).

### Model C: Machine-Ecological Thinking (MACHINOCENE)
As the concept of "Machine-era (Machinocene)" in reference Documents 2 and 3 suggests, rhizomatic networks can be repositioned as the **conditional structure of the technological environment**. Probabilistic output is the way human-nonhuman systems are **repositioned** within this environment. From this perspective, the rhizome is not a "property of an isolated architecture" but the **organizing principle of the entire techno-ecology**, and probabilistic output becomes the **mechanism of distribution and access** within this ecology.

The most coherent way to conceptualize the relationship between the two is as a **topological, non-hierarchical juxtaposition in which probabilistic output selects a trajectory upon the topography of possibilities delineated by the rhizomatic network (structure)**. And as reference Document 1 states, the rhizomatic network functions not as a single cause but as a **chained conjunction of data, algorithms, platforms, and interfaces**.
