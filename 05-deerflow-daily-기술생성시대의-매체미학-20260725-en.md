# Media Aesthetics in the Age of Technogenesis: Kittler's Hardware Determinism and the Software Conditions of Generative AI

## Introduction: The Dialectical Turn of Media Determinism

Friedrich Kittler's media theory formed a core paradigm of media archaeology in the late 20th century. His famous thesis, "the medium determines the message," radicalized Marshall McLuhan's declaration, taking the position that the material and hardware conditions of a medium prescribe the very possibility of cultural perception and knowledge. For Kittler, media were not merely tools of transmission but ontological devices that a priori condition human perception and thought. However, the current media environment dominated by generative AI poses a fundamental challenge to this hardware determinism. In an environment where software conditions such as model weights, data distribution, and prompt embeddings determine output, how must Kittler's determinism be reconstituted?

To answer this question, this paper mobilizes three theoretical resources. First, it analyzes the evolution of technical objects through Gilbert Simondon's concept of concretization. Second, it interprets the synthetic data feedback loop and model collapse phenomenon of generative AI from a media-aesthetic perspective. Third, based on this analysis, it compares two opposing interpretations—the evolution of technical objects and a regressive closed loop—and derives implications for eco-digital/artificial nature research.

## 1. Kittler's Hardware Determinism: The Primacy of Materiality

### 1.1. The Material Apriority of Media

Kittler's media theory is a unique theoretical synthesis combining Foucault's discourse analysis, Lacan's psychoanalysis, and information theory. In his work *Discourse Networks 1800/1900* (Aufschreibesysteme 1800·1900), Kittler argues that media technologies are not neutral tools that merely transmit cultural content but themselves constitute conditions of knowledge and power. In particular, he analyzes how the emergence of analog media at the end of the 19th century—the phonograph, film, and typewriter—fundamentally restructured human perception and memory.

What is decisive for Kittler is the hardware materiality of media. The phonograph stores acoustic events, film stores visual continuity, and the typewriter stores written records, each in its own material way. These media select, amplify, and exclude data according to their respective physical operating principles, and in this process, human senses and cognition become subordinated to the material conditions of the medium. Kittler's famous declaration, "the medium determines the message," implies precisely this material determinism.

### 1.2. The Hierarchy of Hardware and Software

In Kittler's theory, software is merely a derivative layer subordinate to hardware. Tracing the development of computers, he argues that while the von Neumann architecture enabled the separation of hardware and software, ultimately all software operations can be reduced to physical state changes in hardware. From this perspective, the model weights and data distributions of generative AI are nothing but secondary phenomena operating on top of the material conditions of specific hardware (e.g., GPU, TPU).

However, this reductionist position reveals its limitations when explaining how generative AI operates. What determines the output of generative AI is not simply the physical characteristics of hardware, but software conditions: model weights composed of billions of parameters, the statistical distribution of vast training data, and the way user prompts are positioned in the embedding space. These conditions run on hardware, but their determinative power has a unique logic irreducible to the materiality of hardware.

## 2. Simondon's Concept of Concretization: The Evolution of Technical Objects

### 2.1. From Abstract Technical Object to Concrete Technical Object

Gilbert Simondon's philosophy of technology provides a rich theoretical resource in contrast to Kittler's hardware determinism. In *On the Mode of Existence of Technical Objects* (Du mode d'existence des objets techniques), Simondon argues that technical objects evolve from an abstract stage to a concrete stage. Abstract technical objects consist of components with separate functions and are vulnerable in their relationship to the external environment. In contrast, concrete technical objects have functions that are coupled and integrated into a single organic system, achieving greater stability and efficiency in relation to the environment.

Simondon's concept of concretization explains a process in which technical objects are not simply designed by human intention but evolve by responding to internal demands and environmental conditions. In this process, technical objects gradually acquire their own ontological status, developing from mere tools into "technical individuals."

### 2.2. The Concretization Process of Generative AI

Analyzing generative AI through Simondon's concept of concretization yields interesting insights. Early generative AI models (e.g., early GPT models) had relatively simple structures, with each component (attention mechanism, feedforward network, embedding layer, etc.) operating relatively separately. However, as models developed, these components became increasingly tightly integrated, and the model as a whole began to function as a single organic system.

What is particularly notable is that the concretization of generative AI occurs through the interaction between the material conditions of hardware and the abstract conditions of software. On one hand, hardware constraints such as GPU parallel processing capability, memory bandwidth, and power consumption condition the design of model architectures. On the other hand, the distribution of model weights, statistical patterns of data, and the geometric structure of embedding spaces have their own determinative power that goes beyond the materiality of hardware.

From this perspective, Kittler's hardware determinism requires modification. In the media environment of the generative AI era, what is decisive is not the materiality of hardware itself but the mutual concretization process of hardware and software. Model weights run on hardware, but their meaning and effect have statistical and geometric structures irreducible to the materiality of hardware. Data distributions are internalized into the model during training, but these distributions reflect the social and cultural conditions of data rather than the physical characteristics of hardware. Prompt embeddings transform user input into mathematical space, but the result of this transformation is determined by the topological structure of the embedding space rather than the materiality of hardware.

## 3. Synthetic Data Feedback Loop and Model Collapse

### 3.1. The Cyclical Structure of Synthetic Data

One of the most important characteristics of generative AI is its ability to use its own generated output as training data. The so-called "synthetic data feedback loop" refers to the process by which generative AI recursively consumes its own output. In this process, the model gradually becomes trapped in the statistical patterns of the data it has generated, risking the loss of the diversity and creativity of the original training data.

This phenomenon has significant media-aesthetic implications. From Kittler's perspective, media were devices that recorded and stored the external world. The phonograph stored sound, film stored images, and the typewriter stored writing, each collected from the external world. However, generative AI not only collects data from the external world but also has a cyclical structure in which it internalizes the data it generates. This cyclical structure fundamentally reconfigures the relationship between media and the world.

### 3.2. Model Collapse: A Regressive Closed Loop

Model collapse is the extreme result of the synthetic data feedback loop. When a model is repeatedly trained on its own generated data, the tails of the original data distribution gradually disappear, and the model's output converges toward increasingly average and repetitive patterns. This process is similar to the degradation of image quality when repeatedly copying a photocopy.

Model collapse provides an important counterexample to Kittler's determinism. It shows that even if the material conditions of hardware remain the same, software conditions—especially the composition of training data and the cyclical way it is used—can fundamentally change the model's output. This demonstrates the autonomy of the software layer that Kittler's hardware determinism overlooked.

At the same time, however, model collapse challenges Simondon's concept of concretization. For Simondon, the evolution of technical objects was a progressive process toward greater concreteness and adaptability. But model collapse shows that technical objects can fall into a regressive closed loop. This suggests that the evolution of technical objects does not automatically proceed toward concretization but can, under certain conditions, regress.

## 4. Evolution of Technical Objects and Regressive Closed Loop: A Comparison of Two Interpretations

### 4.1. Evolutionary Interpretation: Generative AI as Concretization

The interpretation that views the development of generative AI through Simondon's concept of concretization follows this logic: First, generative AI develops increasingly complex internal structures, and the functional integration of its components deepens. Second, generative AI increases its adaptability to the environment (user input, data distribution) and becomes stable across diverse contexts. Third, generative AI gradually acquires its own ontological status, advancing from a mere tool to a technical individual.

According to this interpretation, the development of generative AI creates a new media environment that goes beyond Kittler's hardware determinism. Hardware remains an important condition but is no longer the sole determining factor. Software conditions (model weights, data distribution, prompt embeddings) interact with hardware materiality and reconfigure the determinative power of media.

### 4.2. Regressive Interpretation: Generative AI as a Closed Loop

On the other hand, the interpretation that views the development of generative AI as a regressive closed loop follows this logic: First, the synthetic data feedback loop gradually traps the model in its own output, causing it to lose contact with the external world. Second, model collapse shows that the evolution of technical objects does not automatically proceed toward concretization but can instead regress. Third, the development of generative AI risks converging into a closed system that reproduces its own statistical patterns, rather than internalizing the diversity and creativity of the external world.

According to this interpretation, generative AI partially confirms Kittler's hardware determinism. The material conditions of hardware operate indirectly through software conditions, and the determinative power of media still has a material basis. However, at the same time, this interpretation extends Kittler's determinism by acknowledging that software conditions can have determinative power independent of hardware materiality.

### 4.3. Synthesis of the Two Interpretations: A Dialectical Reconstitution

The two interpretations are complementary rather than mutually exclusive. The development of generative AI simultaneously contains possibilities for evolution and regression. On one hand, generative AI can evolve into a more complex and adaptive technical object. On the other hand, the synthetic data feedback loop can lead the model into a regressive closed loop.

In this dialectical situation, Kittler's hardware determinism must be reconstituted as follows. First, the materiality of hardware remains an important condition but is no longer the sole determining factor. Software conditions interact with hardware materiality and reconfigure the determinative power of media. Second, the determinative power of media is not a linear causal relationship but has a multi-layered and cyclical structure. Hardware conditions software, but software can transform and reconfigure the conditions of hardware. Third, the determinative power of media is not fixed but changes over the course of the technical object's evolution. The development of generative AI continuously reconfigures the determinative power of media, and in this process, new determining factors emerge while existing ones are transformed.

## 5. Implications for Eco-Digital/Artificial Nature Research

### 5.1. The Concept of Artificial Nature

Eco-digital or artificial nature research is an interdisciplinary field that analyzes the blurring boundary between technology and nature. This research conceptualizes the new media environment created by generative AI as "artificial nature" and analyzes its impact on human perception and behavior.

Artificial nature has two meanings. First, the output generated by generative AI imitates natural processes but is itself an artificial product that is not natural. Second, the way generative AI operates models natural processes (e.g., evolution, learning, adaptation), but this process is prescribed by artificial conditions (e.g., algorithms, data, hardware).

### 5.2. Media-Aesthetic Implications

The reconstitution of Kittler's hardware determinism in eco-digital/artificial nature research has the following implications.

First, the media aesthetics of artificial nature requires a new analytical framework that goes beyond the dichotomy of hardware and software. What determines the output of generative AI is neither the materiality of hardware nor the abstraction of software alone, but the mutual concretization process of the two. Therefore, the media aesthetics of artificial nature must analyze the dialectical relationship between materiality and abstraction.

Second, the synthetic data feedback loop and model collapse reveal the vulnerability of artificial nature. Artificial nature risks losing contact with the external world and becoming trapped in its own statistical patterns. This suggests that artificial nature does not simply imitate nature but has its own unique logic that differs from nature.

Third, the dialectic of the evolution of technical objects and the regressive closed loop raises important questions about the future of artificial nature. Will artificial nature evolve into a more complex and adaptive system, or will it fall into a regressive closed loop? The answer to this question will be determined not only by technical conditions but also by social and cultural conditions.

### 5.3. Directions for Research

Eco-digital/artificial nature research needs to develop in the following directions.

First, systematic analysis of the software conditions of generative AI is required. Theoretical and empirical research is needed on how model weights, data distribution, and prompt embeddings reconfigure the determinative power of media.

Second, in-depth analysis of the media-aesthetic implications of the synthetic data feedback loop and model collapse is required. Research is needed on what these phenomena suggest about the evolution and regression of technical objects, and how they reconfigure the concept of artificial nature.

Third, the development of a new media theory that integrates Kittler's hardware determinism and Simondon's concept of concretization is required. This theory must be able to explain the dialectical relationship between hardware and software, the possibility of evolution and regression of technical objects, and the media-aesthetic characteristics of artificial nature.

## Conclusion: The Reconstitution of Media Determinism

This paper analyzed whether Kittler's hardware determinism remains valid in the media environment of the generative AI era and how it should be reconstituted. The analysis shows that Kittler's hardware determinism has limitations in explaining how generative AI operates and must be reconstituted as follows.

First, the materiality of hardware remains an important condition but is no longer the sole determining factor. Software conditions such as model weights, data distribution, and prompt embeddings have determinative power independent of the materiality of hardware.

Second, the determinative power of media is not a linear causal relationship but has a multi-layered and cyclical structure. Hardware and software mutually condition each other, and in this process, the determinative power of media is continuously reconstituted.

Third, the evolution of technical objects is not only a progressive process toward concretization but also contains the possibility of a regressive closed loop. The synthetic data feedback loop and model collapse show the risk of technical objects becoming trapped in their own statistical patterns.

Fourth, eco-digital/artificial nature research requires the development of a new media aesthetics in this dialectical situation. This media aesthetics must be capable of analyzing the dialectical relationship between hardware and software, the possibility of evolution and regression of technical objects, and the unique logic of artificial nature.

In conclusion, Kittler's hardware determinism should not be completely discarded in the era of generative AI but must be dialectically reconstituted. The materiality of hardware remains an important condition, but its meaning and effect change through interaction with software conditions. This reconstitution will provide the theoretical framework needed for media aesthetics to understand the dialectic of evolution and regression of technical objects.
