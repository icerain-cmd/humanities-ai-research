# Media Aesthetics in the Age of Technogenesis: Kittler's Hardware Determinism and the Software Conditions of Generative AI

## Introduction: The Dialectical Turn of Media Determinism

Friedrich Kittler's media theory is well known for its strong deterministic stance that "the medium determines the message." His hardware determinism centers on the claim that the material basis of media—the physical structure and operation of hardware—defines the very possibilities of information processing, storage, and transmission. However, in the era of generative AI, especially in a media environment dominated by large language models (LLMs) and image generation models, this determinism demands a fundamental reexamination. The output of generative AI is determined by software conditions such as model weights, data distributions, and prompt embeddings, which constitute a new media condition beyond the scope of traditional hardware determinism.

This paper aims to answer the question of whether Kittler's hardware determinism remains valid in the age of generative AI by employing Gilbert Simondon's concept of concretisation as a theoretical tool. Simondon's genesis of technical objects offers theoretical resources to reconfigure Kittler's deterministic view of media into a more fluid, process-oriented perspective. In particular, the synthetic data feedback loop and the phenomenon of model collapse in generative AI simultaneously allow for two opposing interpretations: the evolution of technical objects and a degenerative closed loop. Through this analysis, I will ultimately clarify the implications of this issue for eco-digital or artificial nature research.

## 1. Kittler's Hardware Determinism: The Primacy of the Material Basis

### 1.1. Materiality of Media and Deterministic Logic

Kittler's media theory can be understood as a radicalization of Marshall McLuhan's thesis that "the medium is the message." While McLuhan focused on the sensory and cognitive effects of media, Kittler concentrates more on the material and technical conditions of media. In his works *Aufschreibesysteme 1800·1900* (Discourse Networks 1800/1900) and *Grammophon, Film, Typewriter*, Kittler analyzes how the hardware conditions of media fundamentally structure human perception and culture.

For Kittler, media are not merely tools of transmission but technical systems that define the material conditions of information storage, processing, and transmission. For example, the alphabet enables the linear recording of speech, while the phonograph can store continuous waveforms of sound. Each medium, according to its material structure, can only process specific types of information, thereby determining which messages are possible and which are not. Kittler's famous declaration "the medium determines the message" emphasizes the primacy of these material conditions.

### 1.2. Limitations of Hardware Determinism

However, Kittler's determinism has several theoretical limitations. First, by overemphasizing the material basis of media, his theory overlooks the autonomy and emergence of the software layer. Second, it lacks an explanation of how the relationship between hardware and software has changed over the historical development of media. Third, it inadequately analyzes the interactive aspect between users and media—that is, how media are reconfigured through human practice.

These limitations become even more pronounced in the age of generative AI. What plays a decisive role in the operation of generative AI is not so much the hardware (GPUs, TPUs, etc.) but rather software conditions: model architecture, learning algorithms, data distributions, and prompt embeddings. Of course, the importance of hardware cannot be denied, as hardware advances have enabled the training of large-scale models. However, the fact that entirely different outputs can be generated on the same hardware limits the explanatory power of hardware determinism.

## 2. Simondon's Concept of Concretisation: The Genesis of Technical Objects

### 2.1. The Process of Concretisation and the Evolution of Technical Objects

Gilbert Simondon's genesis of technical objects provides theoretical resources to complement Kittler's determinism. In *Du mode d'existence des objets techniques* (On the Mode of Existence of Technical Objects), Simondon understands technical objects not as mere tools or machines but as beings possessing internal coherence and a logic of evolution. His core concept of concretisation refers to the process by which a technical object gradually integrates and acquires internal coherence from an abstract, dispersed state.

According to Simondon, an early technical object exists in an abstract state where multiple functions are separated and depend on external coordination. Through the process of evolution, these functions gradually become integrated, and the technical object develops into a concrete state capable of self-regulation. For example, early internal combustion engines had separate components, but over time the interdependence among parts increased, and the entire system began to operate like an organism.

### 2.2. The Concretisation Process of Generative AI

The development of generative AI can be illuminated through Simondon's concept of concretisation. Early neural network models were simple perceptrons or shallow networks, with each layer performing a separate function. However, the advancement of deep learning, especially the emergence of the Transformer architecture, has dramatically increased the internal integration of models. The self-attention mechanism of the Transformer considers relationships among all positions in the input sequence simultaneously, integrating tasks that previous models handled with separate modules.

Furthermore, the training process of generative AI can be understood as the "individuation" of a technical object in Simondon's terms. As the model learns from large-scale data, it gradually adjusts its internal structure and acquires the ability to generate appropriate outputs for new inputs. In this process, model weights are not merely a set of parameters but evolve into a form of concretised knowledge that internalizes the structural features of the data distribution.

## 3. Software Conditions of Generative AI: Model Weights, Data Distributions, and Prompt Embeddings

### 3.1. Model Weights and the Reconfiguration of Determinism

The most critical factor determining output in generative AI is model weights. Model weights store patterns extracted from data during training in numerical form, corresponding to what Simondon would call the "internal structure of the technical object." Model weights are stored on hardware, but their meaning and function are purely software. On the same hardware, models with different weights produce entirely different outputs.

In this situation, Kittler's determinism must be reconfigured. The claim that "the medium determines the message" can be transformed into the form "model weights determine the output." However, an important difference is that model weights are not fixed material conditions like hardware but dynamic structures that continuously change through learning. This approach allows for maintaining determinism while extending the materiality of media to the software layer.

### 3.2. Data Distributions and Statistical Determinism

The second condition determining the output of generative AI is data distribution. The model internalizes the statistical patterns of the training data, which fundamentally constrains the model's output space. For example, a model trained only on English text cannot generate Korean text, and a model trained only on images of a specific genre has difficulty generating images outside that genre.

The deterministic effect of data distribution forms an interesting interface with Kittler's media theory. If Kittler argued that the material conditions of media define the possibilities of information, in generative AI, data distribution defines the possibilities of output. This can be understood as a form of determinism: "data determines the message." However, data distribution differs from Kittler's determinism in that it is not a fixed material condition but a dynamic structure formed and changed through social, cultural, and historical processes.

### 3.3. Prompt Embeddings and User-Media Interaction

Prompt embedding is the third condition determining the output of generative AI, referring to the process by which user input is transformed into the model's internal representation space. Prompt embeddings mediate between the user's intention and the model's internal structure, highlighting the user-media interaction aspect that Kittler's theory overlooks.

The deterministic effect of prompt embedding appears in that user input activates specific regions in the model's internal representation space, thereby inducing output. However, the same prompt can produce different outputs depending on the model's state or embedding method, which acts as a mitigating factor for determinism. This indeterminacy suggests that the media condition of generative AI takes the form not of simple determinism but of probabilistic or statistical determinism.

## 4. Synthetic Data Feedback Loops and Model Collapse

### 4.1. Generation and Circulation of Synthetic Data

One unique characteristic of generative AI is that it can use its own generated output as training data again. This forms a synthetic data feedback loop, which allows for two opposing interpretations: the evolution of technical objects and a degenerative closed loop.

The synthetic data feedback loop can be linked to Simondon's concept of concretisation. By using its own output as training data, the model can adjust and develop its internal structure autonomously. This can be understood as a process in which a technical object acquires the autonomy to evolve by itself without interaction with the external environment. From this perspective, the synthetic data feedback loop can be seen as a mechanism that accelerates the concretisation of a technical object.

### 4.2. Model Collapse and Degenerative Closed Loops

However, the synthetic data feedback loop entails the risk of model collapse. Model collapse refers to the phenomenon where a model, when repeatedly trained on its own generated synthetic data, gradually loses diversity and becomes fixated on specific patterns. This corresponds to the case of forming a degenerative closed loop rather than the evolution of a technical object.

Model collapse can be interpreted through Simondon's concepts. Simondon notes that the evolution of technical objects does not always proceed in the direction of concretisation. Technical objects can degenerate when interaction with the external environment is blocked or when internal contradictions are not resolved. In the synthetic data feedback loop, model collapse can be understood as a form of such degeneration. When the model uses only its own output as training data, it loses contact with the diversity of the external world and becomes trapped within its own internal structure.

### 4.3. Comparison of the Two Interpretations: Dialectics of Evolution and Degeneration

The two interpretations of the synthetic data feedback loop show that the media condition of generative AI is not simple determinism but a complex dynamic that simultaneously contains possibilities of evolution and degeneration. The evolution interpretation emphasizes that the synthetic data feedback loop can act as a positive mechanism enhancing the model's autonomy and adaptability. In contrast, the degenerative closed loop interpretation points out the risk that this mechanism may hinder the model's diversity and creativity.

These two interpretations are not mutually exclusive; in fact, they coexist in the development process of generative AI. If the model learns by appropriately mixing external data and synthetic data, it can follow an evolutionary path, but if it relies excessively on synthetic data, it may fall into a degenerative path. This suggests that the media condition of generative AI is not simple determinism but a probabilistic structure that opens up a space of diverse possibilities.

## 5. Implications for Eco-Digital / Artificial Nature Research

### 5.1. Emergence of Artificial Nature and the Reconfiguration of Media Aesthetics

The development of generative AI opens up a new conceptual horizon of eco-digital or artificial nature. Artificial nature refers to systems that humans have artificially created but that possess autonomy and emergence similar to natural processes. Generative AI is a representative example of such artificial nature, functioning as an autonomous system that internalizes statistical patterns of training data and generates new outputs.

The emergence of artificial nature demands a fundamental reconfiguration of media aesthetics. Traditional media aesthetics regarded media as tools for human intentions and purposes, but artificial nature shows that media can operate autonomously beyond human control. This requires a new view of media beyond Kittler's determinism. Media should no longer be understood as simple deterministic conditions but as complex ecosystems that evolve in interaction with humans.

### 5.2. Tasks of Eco-Digital Research

Eco-digital research is an academic field that analyzes the new ecosystems formed by digital technologies, including generative AI. This research faces the following tasks.

First, it must analyze the interaction between the material and software conditions of digital technology. In the operation of generative AI, hardware and software form an integrated system that cannot be separated, requiring a theoretical framework that synthesizes Kittler's hardware determinism and Simondon's concept of concretisation.

Second, it must understand the dynamics of synthetic data feedback loops and model collapse. This is a task of identifying the conditions for evolution and degeneration of technical objects, requiring a theoretical extension of Simondon's genesis theory to digital technology.

Third, it must redefine the relationship between humans and artificial nature. Generative AI functions as an entity that replaces or complements human creativity and autonomy, requiring new ethical and aesthetic reflections on the human-technology relationship.

### 5.3. Aesthetic Implications of Artificial Nature

The emergence of artificial nature also carries important implications at the aesthetic level. The texts, images, and music generated by generative AI provide new aesthetic experiences beyond the categories of traditional art. These experiences form a new aesthetic based on statistical patterns and probabilistic processes, different from traditional art grounded in human intention and creativity.

The aesthetics of artificial nature can develop in a direction that expands Kittler's media aesthetics. If Kittler argued that the material conditions of media determine aesthetic experience, the aesthetics of artificial nature must analyze how software conditions, especially data distributions and model architectures, structure aesthetic experience. This could lead to a new form of aesthetic determinism: "data determines aesthetics." However, it must also consider the indeterminacy and emergence created by the interaction between human prompt input and the model's probabilistic output.
