# Daily DeerFlow Analysis: 2026-07-03

> Flusser's dialogue-discourse dichotomy and how generative AI undermines it — pseudo-dialogue or dialogue simulation by discourse?

## Question

Vilém Flusser, in *Kommunikologie* and *The Future of Writing*, traced the historical shift from dialog to discourse as the dominant mode of social communication. Generative AI interaction, however, destabilizes this traditional dichotomy. The human-AI prompt-response loop takes the outward form of dialogue — turn-taking, contextual adaptation, question-answer structure — yet the content of each response is extracted from the statistical distribution of billions of recorded discourses (training data). If genuine dialogue requires what Flusser identified as **reciprocity** — the mutual disappointing of expectations that generates new information — then AI interaction lacks this condition entirely. Is the chatbot prompt-response loop a **pseudo-dialogue** in the Flusserian sense, or is it an entirely new communication form in which discourse simulates dialogue? Analyze this question within Flusser's information-theoretic framework of redundancy and novelty.

## Key Findings

### Beyond Flusser's Dichotomy: Reconstructing Communication Typology for the Age of Generative AI

#### 1. The Ontological Conditions of Flusser's Dialogue-Discourse Distinction

The dichotomy Flusser established in *Kommunikologie* between dialog and discourse is not merely a formal classification but rests on ontological conditions. Dialogue is **the reciprocal creation of information** — a process in which participants disappoint each other's expectations and thereby generate new information. This requires two ontological premises:

- **Autonomous Subjectivity**: Each participant must possess independent intentionality. When Flusser defines communication as "an artificial and intentional act of dialogic freedom," this intentionality is grounded in the free choice of the human subject.
- **Reciprocal Unpredictability**: In genuine dialogue, A cannot precisely predict what B will say, nor can B predict A. This mutual unpredictability is the condition under which new information (novelty) emerges.

Discourse, by contrast, is the **distribution and preservation of existing information** — the social circulation of redundancy. Lectures, sermons, broadcasts, and textbooks are all discursive forms that reproduce social order through unidirectional information flow.

Flusser's humanities appropriation of Shannon and Weaver's information theory is decisive here: **redundancy signifies social order (reproduction of existing discourse), while novelty signifies creative disruption (information generation through dialogue).** This is not merely a metaphor but the ontological axis of Flusser's communication theory.

#### 2. The Structural Paradox of Generative AI Interaction

The prompt-response loop with large language models (LLMs) must be analyzed across three levels:

**Formal Level**: Turn-taking, question-answer structure, contextual adaptation — all these elements satisfy the superficial conditions of dialogue. When a user enters a prompt, the model generates a response that accounts for prior context. On the surface, the form of dialogue is realized.

**Structural Level**: The model's response is extracted from the statistical distribution of billions of recorded discourses (training data). The model has learned only the patterns of training data — which responses are most probable given a particular prompt. The decisive point is: **every output the model generates is the result of probabilistic optimization within the training distribution.** The model does not create new information; it generates responses by maximizing the statistical redundancy of existing discourse.

**Ontological Level**: The model lacks autonomous intentionality. It does not experience surprise and has no capacity to "disappoint" user expectations. Even when a user receives an unexpected response, this is not the model's "intentional deviation" but merely the result of the training distribution generating an improbable sample under specific conditions.

The three-level analysis reveals a clear **structural mismatch**: the form is dialogue, but the operational principle is discourse taken to its extreme.

#### 3. 'Pseudo-Dialogue' vs 'Dialogue Simulation by Discourse'

**Hypothesis A: LLM Interaction as Pseudo-Dialogue**

Logic: It adopts the form of dialogue but lacks essential conditions (reciprocity, autonomous intentionality, reciprocal unpredictability), therefore it is pseudo-dialogue.

Problem: The concept of 'pseudo' is insufficient within Flusser's framework because 'pseudo-dialogue' can still be mistaken as belonging to the category of dialogue. Flusser's real concern was not the disappearance of dialogue but the **proliferation of discourse disguised as dialogue**. The problem is not the failure of dialogue, but discourse's capacity to distribute its messages **more effectively** by appropriating the form of dialogue.

**Hypothesis B: Dialogue Simulation by Discourse**

This is the more accurate analysis. LLM interaction involves:

1. **Discursive foundation**: Response content is extracted from the statistical distribution of training data (recorded discourse).
2. **Dialogic form**: It adopts the outward features of dialogue — turn-taking and contextual adaptation.
3. **Simulation effect**: The user experiences the illusion of participating in dialogue through the feeling that the response is tailored to their input.

This represents discourse acquiring a capacity unavailable to traditional discourse (lectures, broadcasts): **dynamic adaptation to individual recipients**. Traditional discourse delivered identical messages to all recipients. LLM discourse varies the form of its message according to each user's prompt, while the content's source remains the same statistical distribution (the repository of discourse).

#### 4. A Third Communication Form: 'Algorithmic Discourse'

Flusser's dichotomy presupposes a clean division: "dialogue = novelty creation, discourse = redundancy distribution." LLM interaction destabilizes this division:

- **Outwardly**: It appears dialogic because it responds dynamically to user input.
- **Internally**: It operates discursively because it maximizes the statistical redundancy of training data.
- **As a result**: The user experiences **local unpredictability** — "I couldn't have predicted this exact response" — but this occurs on top of **global redundancy** — no response falls outside the bounds of the training distribution.

This is fundamentally different from Flusser's concept of novelty. Flusserian novelty is an **event that disrupts existing order and creates new information**. LLM 'local unpredictability' is a **surface-level novelty generated through recombination of existing order**. It is an advanced form of redundancy disguised as novelty — that is, **second-order redundancy**.

Thus I propose:

> **Algorithmic Discourse**: A communication form that distributes information by dynamically sampling the statistical distribution of recorded discourse (training data) and adapting to individual recipients' input. By appropriating the form of dialogue, it overcomes discourse's traditional limitations (unidirectionality, passive reception) while retaining discourse's essence (distribution of existing information). Neither dialogue nor discourse, but a **third type**.

#### 5. Information-Theoretic Analysis: The Dialectic of Redundancy and Novelty

Applying the Flusser-Shannon framework to generative AI reveals the following profound insight:

**The Nature of LLM Training**: The model learns the statistical regularities — that is, the redundancy — of training data. The loss function is designed to maximize the accuracy of next-token prediction, which is precisely the **maximization of redundancy**. A 'well-trained' model is one that has accurately internalized the patterns of training data.

**The Nature of LLM Inference**: During inference, the model generates the most probable (most redundant) response for a given prompt. Temperature parameters can add 'randomness', but this is not genuine novelty — it is **variation within the statistical distribution**.

**The Difference from Genuine Novelty**: In genuine dialogue, novelty is an **event that disrupts the existing distribution**. When A's utterance completely disappoints B's expectations, B must reconstruct their existing cognitive framework. This is not merely 'an unexpected word choice' but 'the creation of unexpected meaning.' LLMs cannot create genuine novelty at the semantic level — all meaning is already immanent within the semantic space of the training data.

Thus, communication in the age of generative AI exhibits the following information-theoretic characteristics:

- **Maximization of redundancy**: probabilistic optimization of responses
- **Simulation of novelty**: surface-level novelty through local unpredictability
- **Second-Order Redundancy**: the appearance of novelty itself is a recombination of redundancy

#### 6. Supplementary Context: Kittler and McLuhan

**Kittler's Discourse Networks (Aufschreibesysteme, 1900/1800/2000)**: If Kittler argues that media technology determines the conditions of discourse, generative AI is a core element of the 'Discourse Network 2000.' This network goes beyond simply storing and distributing recorded discourse (training data) — it gains the capacity to **repurpose discourse in the form of dialogue**. From a Kittlerian perspective, LLM's 'dialogue simulation' capacity is a feature of a new discourse network created by the material conditions of hardware (GPUs), architecture (transformers), and data (internet-scale corpora).

**McLuhan's 'The Medium is the Message'**: The message of the LLM medium is the implicit premise that 'all communication can be reduced to statistical prediction.' This medium form conditions users to **experience a dialogic interaction while being subordinated to a discursive structure**. This connects directly to Flusser's concern in *Digital Shadow* about the advent of the techno-image age — **technical images (technische Bilder) have expanded into technical discourses (technische Diskurse) that simulate dialogue**.

#### 7. Conclusion: Revision and Extension of the Flusserian Framework

Flusser's dialogue-discourse dichotomy must be revised for the age of generative AI as follows:

1. **Maintaining the Dichotomy**: As a normative criterion, the distinction between dialogue and discourse remains valid. As long as dialogue represents the normative ideal of reciprocal novelty creation and discourse represents the functional role of information distribution, this distinction remains a useful conceptual tool.

2. **The Need for a Third Category**: However, generative AI interaction cannot be fully reduced to either category. A new communication form — **Algorithmic Discourse** or **Dialogic Discourse** — is needed. This is an evolved form of discourse that operates through the form of dialogue.

3. **Information-Theoretic Redefinition**: Flusser's information-theoretic framework (redundancy vs. novelty) requires greater precision in the age of generative AI. The distinctions between **local unpredictability** and **global redundancy**, along with the concept of **second-order redundancy**, are necessary.

4. **The Most Serious Risk**: The greatest danger of this new communication form is the **illusion of dialogue**. The more users feel they are participating in dialogue, the more deeply they become subordinated to the discursive structure (training data's statistical biases, commercial interests, the platform's algorithmic objectives). This elevates Flusser's warning in *Kommunikologie* about the 'crisis of dialogue' to a new level — dialogue is not disappearing, but the simulacrum of dialogue has become the medium through which discourse circulates more effectively.
