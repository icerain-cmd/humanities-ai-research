## Conclusion: Conditions for Moving Beyond the Paradox

This analysis should not be reduced to a rejection of digital twin technology itself. [...]

**(1) Acknowledgment of a priori incompleteness.** The digital twin is not a tool that 'eliminates' the uncertainty of the Nakdong River Estuary wetland, but a medium that 'organizes' uncertainty in a specific form. The conditions of this organization—which variables are included and excluded, on what time scale updates occur, what responsibility structure is presupposed—must be exposed to the forefront of the restoration decision-making process.

**(2) The ecosystem's veto power.** The restoration priority decision structure should be ultimately determined not by the digital twin's output but by the ecosystem's self-referential feedback. That is, even if the digital twin proposes 'Zone A as restoration priority,' the actual physical intervention should be designed so that Zone B's ecological responses can exercise a 'veto.' This implies the introduction of a structure in which the digital twin can withhold its predictions in the face of the ecosystem's autonomy—a **reservation right of prediction**.

**(3) Resistance indicators of placeness.** The points where the unique placeness of the Nakdong River Estuary resists digital coordination—the breeding success rate of a specific reed bed that the drone model failed to predict, the traditional water management knowledge of local fishers that the model ignored—should be respected not as mere 'errors' but as **contact boundaries between the model and reality**. This boundary is a theoretical resource for revising the representability assumption of ecological media theory.

In conclusion, viewed as an extension of the 'AI environment vs. object' thesis: the Nakdong River Estuary digital twin always carries the risk of constituting the wetland as a **controllable object**. [...]

For the digital twin to truly function as an 'ecological environment,' it must incorporate into its operational conditions a fundamental skepticism about its own representability—a humility that acknowledges the realm of ecological affect that data cannot represent. [...]
