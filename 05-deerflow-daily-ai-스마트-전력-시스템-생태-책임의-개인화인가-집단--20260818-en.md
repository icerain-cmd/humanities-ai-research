### 2. Direct Analysis: A Shift in Ecological Responsibility or the Learning of a Collective Sensibility?

#### 2.1. From the Perspective of Foucault's Governmentality: A Shift to Individualized Self-Surveillance

The AI smart power system is a sophisticated example of **environmental governmentality**. In Foucault's later work, governmentality operates not through legal prohibition or ideological interpellation, but by **computationally adjusting the behavioral environment to induce the subject's self-management**. This system does three things:

- **Asymmetric Arrangement of Visibility**: The electricity usage of each household is made visible in real-time to a central server, but residents can only see their own data. Comparative data with neighbors or the overall energy flow of the complex is presented only as an abstracted 'average' or 'target'. This digitizes the **asymmetry of panoptic visibility**.
- **Normative Framing of Behavior**: 'Carbon reduction action recommendations' are not just information provision; they set a **standard of normal/abnormal**. Households that follow the recommendations are potentially framed as 'good consumers', while those that do not are framed as 'environmentally irresponsible'.
- **Individualization of Responsibility**: The central heating system of an apartment complex was originally a matter of collective infrastructure (management fee allocation, boiler efficiency, insulation performance). By shifting to **household-specific usage prediction and individual adjustment**, the AI system reduces structural problems (poor insulation, old pipes, energy prices) to individual 'consumption choices'.

At this point, Foucault's concept of **'technologies of the self'** operates decisively. Through the data dashboard presented by the system, residents are asked to constitute themselves as 'energy consumers' and become subjects who optimize their own consumption patterns. This directly reproduces the core dynamic of neoliberal governmentality—**the conversion of structural conditions into the responsibility of individual self-management**.

#### 2.2. From the Perspective of Stiegler's Pharmacology: The Potential for Learning a Collective Energy Sensibility

However, stopping here would miss the second term of your question—**the possibility of Stiegler's pharmacological technology**. For Stiegler, technology (techne) is always a **pharmakon**: both poison and cure. The externalization of memory (writing, printing, digital) weakens individual memory but simultaneously creates **the conditions for new collective memory and sensibility**.

The **pharmacological potential** that the AI smart power system can open up includes three aspects:

- **Sensibilization of Energy**: Electricity is inherently 'the insensible'. Consumers could only feel their electricity usage through monthly bills. By providing real-time data visualization, predictive models, and behavioral feedback, the AI system newly constitutes an **'energy sensibility' that did not exist before**. This is an example of what Stiegler calls 'tertiary retention'—the process where memory and sensibility are externalized into technological media, forming new conditions for perception.
- **Infrastructure for Collective Learning**: If data from individual households is anonymized, aggregated, and the energy consumption patterns of the entire complex are learned, this could become the basis for a **collective energy intelligence beyond the individual**. For example, if actions to distribute peak load at specific times lead to voluntary adjustments across the entire complex beyond individual recommendations, this constitutes the **formation of a new collective sensibility**.
- **Persistence of Pharmacological Tension**: However, Stiegler emphasizes that the pharmacological nature of technology is **not automatically liberating**. Whether technology opens up a collective sensibility or closes it off into individual consumerism depends on **the design and governance of the technology**. That is, how the system configures data ownership, algorithm transparency, and the direction of feedback is decisive.

**Interim Conclusion**: The two terms of your question are **not mutually exclusive**. The AI smart power system is simultaneously a **device of individualized responsibility** and a **device of potential collective sensibility**. The problem is not which direction it will operate in, but **how to analyze the field of tension in which these two directions operate simultaneously**.

### 3. Haraway and Foucault: Analyzing What the System Does Not Make Visible

#### 3.1. Haraway's Situated Knowledges

What Haraway calls the "God-trick" is the illusion of an omniscient viewpoint that can see everything. The dashboard of the AI smart power system performs precisely this **God-trick**. The system presents the electricity usage of all households as 'objective data', but this data is **only visible from a specific position**.

- **Positionality of Data**: What the system measures is a single indicator: electricity usage (kWh). However, the data of 'electricity usage' completely abstracts away qualitative factors such as **the time of care labor, the health status of household members, and sensitivity to indoor temperature**. For example, a household with an elderly person suffering from respiratory disease maintaining a higher heating temperature is recorded only as 'inefficient consumption'; the **need for care** is not included in the data.
- **Exclusion of Partial Perspectives**: For Haraway, 'objectivity' is not an omniscient viewpoint but **responsible knowledge through the connection of partial perspectives**. The single optimization criterion (carbon reduction) adopted by the AI system pushes other values (care, health, comfort) **outside the realm of visibility**.

#### 3.2. The Analytical Exclusion of Energy Poverty

**Energy poverty** is something this system structurally cannot make visible. The reasons are threefold:

- **Illusion of Voluntary Choice**: The system is designed on the premise of a 'rational consumer'—the assumption that a rational subject provided with information will make the optimal choice. However, energy-poor households have no room for 'choice'. Reducing heating and cooling is not 'saving' but a **risk to health and survival**. The system's recommendations demand an 'impossible choice' from them.
- **Inequality in Data**: The electricity usage patterns of energy-poor households are likely to be recorded as 'outliers'. However, the fact that these outliers are connected to **income inequality, housing dilapidation, and the vulnerability of household composition** is not included in the system's data model. Instead, the system presupposes a fictional equality where 'all households have access to the same information under the same conditions'.
- **Political Economy of Management Fees**: Energy management in Korean apartments cannot be separated from the **management fee** system. The management fee is a complex cost structure that includes not only heating and cooling costs but also common area electricity, cleaning, security, and elevator maintenance. When the AI system targets only the electricity usage of individual households for optimization, the **political-economic structure of the management fee—for example, an equal distribution method that places a greater burden on low-income households**—completely falls out of the analysis.

#### 3.3. The Structural Invisibility of Care Labor

**Care labor** is the area most systematically excluded by the AI system. This is revealed in the following analytical layers:

- **Gendering of Time**: The 'peak time' of electricity usage patterns often overlaps with the time when housework and care labor are concentrated. However, the system records consumption during this time as 'inefficiency' without analyzing **whose labor and what kind of labor** this consumption results from. Care labor is primarily unpaid labor assigned to women, and it is considered 'valueless' in data economics.
- **Differences in Sensitivity to Temperature**: Infants, the elderly, and the chronically ill are more sensitive to temperature changes. Households caring for them use energy based on the **physical needs of the care recipient** rather than a 'rational' temperature setting. The AI system's optimization **standardizes this physical difference**. It assumes all household members are 'standard humans' with the same temperature sensibility.
- **Foucault's Governmentality and the Exclusion of Care**: Foucault's analysis of governmentality reveals how the subject is constituted as a 'free subject who manages themselves'. However, this 'free subject' is a **subject free from care**, who does not bear the responsibility of care. The 'rational consumer' presupposed by the AI system is an individual optimizing their own energy consumption; the labor of caring for others—that is, the labor of deciding and taking responsibility for the energy needs of others—exists outside the system's frame.
