## II. The Three Axes of the Apparatus: The Combination of Data, Rubrics, and Dashboards and the A Priori Production of the 'Evaluable Student'

For Foucault, the apparatus (dispositif) is a network of heterogeneous elements—discourses, institutions, architectural forms, regulatory decisions, laws, administrative measures, scientific statements, philosophical propositions, moral teachings—which possesses an ontological efficacy beyond that of a mere tool. The Panopticon in *Discipline and Punish* is a paradigm of such an apparatus. The Panopticon is not merely an architectural structure for surveillance; through an 'economy of visibility,' it constitutes the inmate as a **'being who is always seen,'** thereby simultaneously realizing the automation of power and the self-disciplining of the individual.

When analyzing a generative AI-based university writing evaluation system through this concept of the apparatus, the combination of the following three axes must be elucidated.

### 1. The Data Axis: Decomposition of Writing and Transformation into Reconstructable Elements

The first condition for an AI evaluation system to operate is the decomposition of the student's writing into **discrete, quantifiable units**. Sentence length, vocabulary diversity, grammatical accuracy, logical connectivity, structural consistency, etc., are extracted as individual parameters. This process is not a mere technical measurement but a digital reproduction of **'partitioning'** and **'classificatory examination'** which Foucault analyzed as core procedures of disciplinary power in *Discipline and Punish*.

What is important here is not the 'meaning' or 'context' of the text written by the student, but that the **'measurable attributes' of the text are databased and become circulatable information**. Just as Foucault, in *The Birth of the Clinic*, described how the medical gaze constitutes disease as a 'visible' and 'utterable' object within the individual patient's body, the data extraction of the AI evaluation system makes certain aspects of writing visible while simultaneously rendering other aspects (e.g., creative ambiguity, intentional rule-breaking, genre hybridity) invisible.

### 2. The Rubric Axis: The Normative Grid of Normality

The rubric constitutes the **normative dimension** of the apparatus. For Foucault, disciplinary power operates more efficiently through **normalization** than through punishment. Normalization makes individuals comparable to a standard, measures deviations from this standard, and constitutes them as correctable.

The rubric of an AI evaluation system has the following characteristics:

- **Implicit Standardization**: It presupposes a specific discursive norm of what constitutes 'good writing' (e.g., clear argument structure, logical consistency, use of academic vocabulary) as a universal standard. Although this norm originates from a specific cultural and academic tradition (primarily the Anglo-American analytic philosophical writing tradition), it assumes the appearance of technical neutrality.
- **Reductive Commensurability**: By placing writing from different genres (critical essays, reports, reflective journals) on the same evaluation axis, it reduces essentially incomparable writing forms into a single grading system.
- **Predictive Evaluation Logic**: The way a generative AI model predicts 'which text will receive a high score' based on the regularities of the large corpus it was trained on makes the rubric function not merely as a post-hoc evaluation tool but as an **a priori constitutive principle**.

### 3. The Dashboard Axis: The Politics of Visibility

The dashboard is a digital implementation of what Foucault terms the **surveillance hierarchy**. Among the three tools of disciplinary power in *Discipline and Punish*—hierarchical surveillance, normalizing judgment, and the examination—the examination performs a dual function: it constitutes the individual as a 'describable object' and simultaneously places them within a 'comparable system.'

The dashboard extends the function of the examination as follows:

- **Comparative Visibility**: By displaying an individual's score within the distribution of the group, the student is constantly made aware of their position. Deviations from the rubric criteria are visualized, identifying 'points for improvement.'
- **Temporal Visibility**: By displaying the trend of an individual's score changes in a graph, the student's writing 'growth' or 'stagnation' is presented as an objective fact. This is structurally similar to the way the 'table of progress' (tableau des progrès) in military training schools, as analyzed by Foucault in *Discipline and Punish*, projects individual development onto a standardized temporal axis.
- **Internalization of Self-Surveillance**: By making the dashboard accessible to the student themselves, the student internalizes the gaze of the external supervisor and continuously monitors themselves as an 'object of evaluation.'

### The A Priori Production of the 'Evaluable Student'

The crucial point when these three axes combine is that the subject of the 'evaluable student' is produced **a priori**. This occurs through the following cyclical process:

1.  The rubric establishes a specific writing norm as the standard of normality.
2.  The AI model trained according to this standard evaluates the student's text within a binary framework of normal/abnormal, excellent/insufficient.
3.  The dashboard presents this evaluation result to the student visually, comparatively, and temporally.
4.  Based on this information, the student adjusts their writing behavior to achieve a 'better score.'
5.  This adjusted writing behavior is, in turn, included as data in the AI model's training data.

This cycle constitutes a mechanism of **productive power that does not merely evaluate post-hoc but makes the student 'naturally' write in a specific way**. The moment a student chooses a particular sentence, that choice is already made under the shadow of the rubric. This precisely aligns with Foucault's point in *Discipline and Punish* that the effect of the Panopticon is "to automate and deindividualize the operation of power before power is actually exercised."

## III. The Ambivalent Subjectivation of Objections and Self-Narratives: The Dialectic of Resistance and Self-Management

The core issue of this question is whether the student's self-narratives and objections constitute resistance to the apparatus or an intensification of the self-management demanded by the apparatus. Viewed across Foucault's entire body of work, this is a more complex field of subjectivation that cannot be captured by a simple dichotomy.

### 1. The Possibility of Resistance: How Objections Reveal the Boundaries of the Apparatus

The conditions under which objections can have the character of resistance are as follows:

- **Challenge to the Implicit Premises of the Rubric**: When a student questions the validity of the evaluation criteria themselves by stating, "This rubric only favors a specific way of writing," or "These criteria fail to measure the creativity of this genre," this becomes a moment of resistance that makes visible the **power effect** of the normative grid that is the rubric.
- **Resistance to Data Reduction**: The question, "Why was this part of my text not reflected in the evaluation?" reveals that the AI evaluation system is intentionally excluding certain aspects. This is an act of exposing the political nature of datafication.
- **Challenge to the Visualization Frame of the Dashboard**: The assertion, "A low rate of change does not mean I haven't grown," problematizes the fact that the dashboard is based on a specific temporality and model of development.

As Foucault stated in *The Will to Knowledge*, "Where there is power, there is resistance." This signifies the co-presence of power and resistance, not that resistance is an external force coming from outside of power. Resistance arises from within power relations and prevents those relations from becoming fixed.

### 2. The Intensification of Self-Management: How Objections Strengthen the Apparatus

However, at the same time, there are conditions under which the act of objection itself can be reabsorbed as an intensification of the self-management demanded by the apparatus:

- **Conversion into Corrective Feedback**: When an objection is transformed into the question, "How can I get a higher score?", it becomes a strategy of self-optimization to internalize the normative criteria of the apparatus and better adapt to them. In Foucault's concept of 'technologies of the self,' this is a process of constituting oneself in a specific way through particular **self-practices**.
- **Intensification of Datafication**: The objection procedure itself produces new data (frequency, type, timing of objections), which is then used to 'advance' the apparatus. In other words, by functioning as a feedback loop that corrects the system's errors, the objection contributes to increasing the precision of the apparatus.
- **Narrative Subjectivation**: The 'self-narrative' in which a student explains their writing process is an attempt to bridge the gap between the data-based identity provided by the dashboard and their own experiential identity. However, when this narrative explains oneself in light of the evaluation criteria, e.g., "I actually tried hard to write well like this," it becomes a mode of subjectivation that constitutes oneself as a **perpetually evaluable being**.

### 3. Ambivalent Subjectivation: The Simultaneity of Resistance and Self-Discipline

Foucault's later work, particularly the concept of subjectivation in Volumes 2 and 3 of *The History of Sexuality*, offers insights that transcend this dichotomy. For Foucault, subjectivation is not a simple imposition of external power onto the subject, but a process of **active practice through which the subject constitutes themselves within power relations**.

The student's objection in an AI writing evaluation system exhibits the following ambivalence:

- **Moment of Resistance**: The objection is a moment when the student recognizes the asymmetry of power relations and can secure a critical distance to question the premises of the evaluation system. This connects to Foucault's definition of critique in *What is Critique?* as "the art of not being governed quite so much."
- **Moment of Self-Constitution**: Simultaneously, the objection is a field of subjectivation where the student constructs a specific narrative about their own writing ability. This narrative may oppose the evaluation system, but it can also reinforce the very premise that "I am a being who can be evaluated."
- **Paradoxical Coexistence**: The most interesting case is when a student criticizes the unfairness of the evaluation system while simultaneously demanding a more sophisticated evaluation system or modifying their own writing style to get a better score. This shows that resistance and discipline are not simply in opposition, but are **ambivalent processes that can occur simultaneously within a single subject**.

## IV. Conclusion: Conditions for Moving Beyond the Effects of the Apparatus

What is revealed when analyzing a generative AI-based writing evaluation system through Foucault's concept of the apparatus is that this system is not merely a tool for evaluating student writing more efficiently, but an arrangement of productive power that simultaneously produces the **'evaluable subject'** and the **'normative model of writing.'**

To prevent this analysis from falling into simple technological determinism or conspiratorial critique of power, the following conditions must be recognized:

1.  **Constitutive Incompleteness of the Apparatus**: No apparatus is a completely closed system. Every apparatus has cracks, contradictions, and exceptions, which are points of possibility where the apparatus can be transformed through unexpected student uses, instructor interventions, or policy changes.

2.  **Education in Critical Literacy**: It is necessary to include in education not just technical training on how to use AI evaluation tools, but a critical understanding of the composition of the evaluation system, the politics of datafication, and the normative premises of the rubric. This is a way of implementing Foucault's critical spirit in educational practice.

3.  **Acknowledging the Indistinguishability of Resistance and Self-Management**: Rather than univocally defining a student's objection as either simple 'resistance' or 'intensification of self-management,' acknowledging its ambivalence increases the accuracy of the analysis. What is important is not the content of the objection, but distinguishing whether the objection **problematizes the power relation itself, or is a strategy for securing a better position within that power relation**.

4.  **Making the Apparatus Visible**: By transparently disclosing the operating principles of the AI evaluation system, the process of rubric construction, and the criteria for data processing to students and instructors, the apparatus can be made an object of critical reflection rather than an 'invisible power' operating in the shadows. This act itself can be a strategy for weakening the effects of the apparatus.

In conclusion, the student's objections and self-narratives within an AI writing evaluation system constitute a **field of 'paradoxical subjectivation' that simultaneously encompasses the ambivalence of resistance and self-management**. Moving beyond a simple dichotomy, making this ambivalent subjectivation process itself the object of analysis would be the true utility of a Foucauldian analysis.
