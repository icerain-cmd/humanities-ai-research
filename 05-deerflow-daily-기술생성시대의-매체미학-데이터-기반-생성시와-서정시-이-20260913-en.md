# Theoretical Analysis of the Han River Real-Time Sensor-Generated Poetry Project: Crisis or Expansion of Lyric Poetry?

## 1. Introduction: The Media Specificity of the Case

The case set by this question—a generative poetry project that receives real-time water level and flow velocity sensor data from the Han River and updates its sentences every hour—fundamentally differs from existing examples of digital or generative poetry. While Samantha F. Jones's `05BH004 (1915–2019)` retrospectively acquires 104 years of historical flood data and converts it into a single static poetic constraint (character count per line)[citation:Turning Data into Poetry](https://www.heliotropejournal.net/helio/turning-data-into-poetry), the data flow in this case never terminates, nor does the model's output conclude. Sasha Stiles's `A LIVING POEM` (MoMA, 2025–26) claims to regenerate itself every hour, but in practice it is closer to a loop that subtly rearranges fragments of a static "core text"[citation:A LIVING POEM - Brooklyn Rail](https://brooklynrail.org/2026/02/artseen/sasha-stiles-a-living-poem). In contrast, the generation process in this case is dependent on the real-time fluctuation of **environmental variables**, which, combined with the LLM's probabilistic sampling, produces a **non-finite, non-predictable** textual flow.

This case directly challenges the premises of existing lyric poetry theory along three axes:

1. **The axis of the subject**: The source of poetic utterance is not the human interior (lyrical I) but a non-human actor network composed of sensors, data, and models.
2. **The axis of form**: Form is not meter, stanza, or conclusive structure, but a dynamic constraint system.
3. **The axis of temporality**: Time is not the representation of a completed past, but an ongoing generative process open to the future.

Below, I will examine the explanatory power of traditional lyric poetry theory along these three axes and, if explanation proves impossible, propose alternative definitions. ## 2. The Three Pillars of Lyric Poetry Theory and Their Collapse

### 2.1. Poetry as Expression of Human Interiority — The Crisis of the Subject

The canonical theory of lyric poetry originates in Hegel's *Lectures on Aesthetics*, where he defines lyric poetry as "the form in which the individual subject's interiority (Innerlichkeit) represents its own feelings and views." Korean lyric poetry theory also stands on this tradition, linking the poetic subject to "the authenticity of the self" and "the psychological truth of the interior."

In this case, the question "Who speaks?" is unanswerable. The source of utterance lies in the combination of the physical flow data of the Han River—a non-human, more precisely pre-human environmental process—and a GPT-class model that extracts probabilistic patterns from a large text corpus. There is no "interior," no "authenticity," and no "self" here.

However, it is worth noting that **the concept of the lyric subject itself has already been deconstructed through various paths since the late 20th century**. Rimbaud's "Je est un autre," Mallarmé's dissolution of the subject in "pure poetry," Barthes's "death of the author," Foucault's "author function"—this lineage shows that the poetic subject is not a self-evident psychological entity but a discursive and institutional construct. More directly, N. Katherine Hayles, a pioneer of digital poetics, defined "text in digital poetry as an event, not an object," proposing an epistemological shift that does not limit the source of poetic utterance to a human author[citation:N. Katherine Hayles - INTERFACECRITIQUE](https://interfacecritique.net/journal/hayl).

**Interim Judgment**: The absence of a human subject is not a fatal crisis for lyric poetry theory. Rather, post-structuralist critiques of the subject already provide theoretical vocabulary to accommodate such non-human utterance. The problem lies at a more structural level: **the level of temporality and form**.

### 2.2. The Completeness of Linguistic Form — The Problem of Temporality and Closure

The formal completeness of lyric poetry operates on two dimensions: **(a) Synchronic completeness** — A poem is a bounded unit with a beginning and an end, possessing an internally homogeneous formal order (meter, rhyme, line breaks, etc.). **(b) Diachronic completeness** — Once creation is complete, the poem exists as a stable object that no longer changes.

This case violates both dimensions:

- **Synchronic dimension**: The text updated every hour has no moment of "closure." The output at 13:00 is replaced by the new output at 14:00, and this process continues indefinitely as long as the Han River flows, the sensors operate, and the model generates responses.
- **Diachronic dimension**: The concept of a 'final text' does not hold. Every output is in principle variable; even for the same water level data, different sentences can be generated depending on the model's probabilistic sampling (temperature parameter, top-k sampling, etc.). This deconstructs the unique identity of the text.

Here, **Kittler's concept of time axis manipulation** becomes crucial. Kittler defines the essence of technical media as "data processing operating below the temporal threshold of human perception" and "the reversible manipulation of the time axis"[citation:Kittler - Real Time Analysis, Time Axis Manipulation](https://monoskop.org/images/e/e0/Kittler_Friedrich_1990_2017_Real_Time_Analysis_Time_Axis_Manipulation.pdf). In this case:
- The sensor converts the physical time of the Han River (continuous flow) into discrete data points.
- This data is injected into the model's prompt template and converted into text.
- This text is then presented as a "poetic moment" through the foregrounding process of the display.

This sequence, from Kittler's perspective, is a **chain of media operations that bypass human perception and cognitive abilities**. The poet is no longer the subject or creator of the poem, but functions as the **designer/architect** or **setter of computational conditions** for this chain.

## 3. Paths of Revision: Reconceptualizing Poetic Form and Temporality

At this point, I take the position that **'lyric poetry theory can explain this case within an expanded conceptual framework'** rather than the radical conclusion that 'it cannot explain it.' However, this requires the following revisions.

### 3.1. Redefining Form: Form as a Constraint System

Traditional poetic form meant **immanent order**—regularity inscribed within the text, such as meter, rhyme, line length, and stanza structure. In this case, form must be redefined as an **external, dynamic constraint system**.

Form is no longer a property of the text's surface but becomes an **architecture of generative conditions** composed of three elements:

1. **Data pipeline**: Sensor sampling frequency, data normalization method, threshold settings.
2. **Prompt engineering**: Template structure that converts data into linguistic commands.
3. **Generation parameters**: Hyperparameters controlling the model's probabilistic behavior, such as temperature, top-k, and top-p.

This concept of form has a deep analogy with **Simondon's process of concretisation of technical objects**. According to Simondon, a technical object continuously concretises itself through interaction with its **associated milieu (milieu associé)**, and this process is inherently incomplete[citation:Simondon individuation and AI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-024-02152-2). Similarly, the poetic form of this project is not a fixed structure but is re-concretised every hour in the co-evolution of the Han River (environment) and the LLM (technology).

To name this concept in poetic terms, it could be called **'generative constraint poetry'** or **'environment-mediated generative poetry.'** This distinguishes it from established Oulipian constraints (lipogram, N+7, etc.) that operate within language, as it uses external, dynamic variables—environmental data—as constraints.

### 3.2. Redefining Temporality: Event-Time and the Environmental Present

The temporality of lyric poetry is generally **retrospective**. The poetic subject represents past experiences (love, loss, natural landscapes) in present language, and this act of representation fixes the past as 'completed meaning.' Hölderlin's 'Andenken' (remembrance) and Wordsworth's 'spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings' both stand on this retrospective temporal structure.

The revision of temporality required by this case occurs on three levels:

**1) The Environmental Present**
Each output reflects the 'here and now' state of the Han River. This is not a representation of the past but a non-human, non-psychological 'attestation' of an ongoing environmental process. Poetic time is determined not by psychological memory but by the sampling frequency of environmental sensors.

**2) Event-Time**
Each hourly output is not a completed 'work' but an 'event' that has passed through the generative process. Borrowing Hayles's expression, "digital poetry is an event, not an object," and it has a "sensitive dependence on temporal and spatial context"[citation:Hayles - The Time of Digital Poetry: from Object to Event](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/bitstreams/7472ecc6-a1c5-4091-a2d0-734f8a53ee7e/download). This case radicalizes the concept of 'poetry as event': one 'poem-event' exists for an hour and is then replaced by the next event.

**3) Circular, Non-Teleological Time**
The time of the poem is not linear completion (beginning → middle → end) but a complex temporality where the natural cycle of the Han River's water level fluctuations (rise → fall, flood → drought, seasonal variation) intersects with the technical cycle of LLM generation (hourly prompt → generation → display → disappearance). This can be called **ecological time**. 'Closure' is no longer the conclusion of a work but merely the moment when one generative cycle ends and the next begins.

## 4. Alternative Definitions: Comparing Two Paths

If the above revisions are not accepted, the project's output must be defined as a media configuration other than 'poetry.' This path offers two main alternatives.

### 4.1. Path A: Recognize as Poetry but Expand the Concept (Recommended)

As proposed in Section 3, this case can be recognized as poetry under the following conditions:

| Traditional Criterion | Expanded Criterion | Fulfillment in This Case |
|:---|:---|:---:|
| Expression of human subject's interiority | Utterance of non-human actor network | ⚠️ Fulfilled (assuming redefinition) |
| Completeness of linguistic form | Instance of dynamic constraint system | ✅ Fulfilled |
| Representation of psychological time | Event-like occurrence of environmental present | ✅ Fulfilled |
| Transmission of intended meaning | Design intention of generative condition architecture | ✅ Fulfilled (poet as designer) |

This is a kind of **category extension**. Just as Dada in the early 20th century asked "Is this poetry?" and expanded the concept of poetry, this case also demands that **the boundaries of poetry be extended into the domain of data environments and generative models**.

### 4.2. Path B: Define as a 'Media Configuration' Rather Than Poetry

If even the expanded concept is not accepted, the following alternative definitions are possible:

**1) Linguistic Extension of Real-Time Data Visualization**
The output of this project is closer to a linguistic version of **data-constrained visualization** like Joshua's '05BH004' than to poetry. That is, poetry is merely an alternative form to visual graphs or charts; its essence belongs to the same category as aesthetic transformations of data (e.g., sonification, data sculpture).

**2) Cybernetic Environmental Text**
Applying Kittler's media theory more strictly, this output should be defined as "a self-descriptive computational result of technical media" rather than "meaning for humans." That is, it is not poetry but merely a **linguistic indication of media state**. Following this position, the output is closer to a linguistic residue of **aesthetic measurement** (Birkhoff/Galanter type) than to poetry.

**3) Generative Environmental Chronicle**
This perspective focuses on the temporal continuity of the output. Each hourly output is more appropriately read as an 'environmental log' or 'non-human chronicle' than as poetry. While it creates an effect as if nature itself is verbalizing its processes, strictly speaking, it is merely an accumulation of the process by which media translate natural data into human language.

## 5. Comprehensive Judgment and Theoretical Conclusion

**Final Answer: It can be explained. However, this presupposes a fundamental revision of lyric poetry theory.**

My judgment leans toward Path A (recognize as poetry but expand). The reasons are as follows.

**First, the history of the category 'poetry' is a history of continuous expansion and redefinition.** From classical lyric poetry to Romantic poetry, Symbolist poetry, Modernist poetry, concrete poetry, Language poetry, Fluxus event poetry, and digital poetry, the definition of poetry has responded to changes in media and epistemology. This case lies on the extension of this lineage, particularly as a developmental culmination of algorithmic poetry since the 1960s, networked poetry since the 1990s, and generative AI poetry in the 2020s.

**Second, while explanation is impossible without revision, there are theoretical advantages that become possible through revision.** The concepts introduced to explain this case—**'form as a dynamic constraint system,' 'temporality as environmental present,' 'poetry as event'**—are theoretical assets that can be extended to explain various experiments in contemporary poetry, including climate poetry, ecopoetry, and data poetry, beyond just this case.

**Third, defining it as 'not poetry' offers theoretical convenience but incurs explanatory loss.** The alternative definitions in Path B (data visualization, cybernetic indication, environmental log) explain the mediality of the text but fail to account for the **receptive attitude** that a reader can adopt toward this text—the sense of rhythm, the imagistic transformation, the perception of linguistic coherence—that is, 'the way of reading poetically.' If this project's text actually produces a 'poetic effect' in readers, this effect itself provides a reason for inclusion within the category of poetry.

## 6. Remaining Issues: Three Tasks for an Expanded Poetics

The above answer is closer to a 'conceptual blueprint' than a complete theoretical system. To construct a practically operable poetics, the following subsequent theoretical work is necessary.

**1) The Problem of Redistributing Agency**
Who is the 'poetic creator' of this project? — The human designer, the sensor hardware, the physical process of the Han River, the LLM, the data pipeline software, the viewer. On what basis should their agencies be hierarchized or flattened? Hayles's concept of 'cognitive assemblage' and Latour's Actor-Network Theory (ANT) provide theoretical resources for this problem[citation:Hayles - Cognitive Assemblages](https://scholars.duke.edu/person/katherine.hayles/scholarly-works). The 'poetic subject' of this case should be redefined not as a single entity but as a **distributed agency network** of technology, environment, and humans.

**2) The Problem of Criteria for Aesthetic Judgment**
If all outputs are non-finite and variable, how is it possible to distinguish between 'good poetry' and 'bad poetry'? Are all outputs from a single constraint system equally valid, or can certain outputs be poetically 'more successful' than others? This question connects to the tendency of Kittlerian media theory to separate technical analysis from aesthetic judgment. My proposal: The architectural design of generative conditions itself should become the primary object of aesthetic judgment, and the qualitative differences between individual outputs can be evaluated based on the degree of 'expressive fidelity' of the constraint system and the 'emergence of unexpected meaning.'

**3) The Problem of Institutional and Receptive Context**
How is such a work received in an exhibition space? Stiles's A LIVING POEM installed at MoMA is received as 'poetry' due to its location within the white cube. The same generative system, placed on a web dashboard, is likely to be read as 'data visualization,' and installed in a gallery as 'interactive installation.' Further research is needed on the influence of receptive attitudes and institutional frames on genre definition.

## 7. Conclusion

The project combining real-time sensor data from the Han River with a generative language model directly violates all three axes of existing lyric poetry theory—the human subject, formal completeness, and retrospective temporality. However, this violation does not signify the abandonment of theory. Rather, by mobilizing the conceptual resources that 20th-century poetry theory has already prepared—non-human utterance, form as constraint, poetic experience as event—this case opens a new genre horizon: **generative environmental poetry as a radical reflection on the environmental and technical conditions of poetry.**

The theoretical revisions required for this can be summarized in three points: **① reconceptualizing form from immanent structure to external constraint system, ② reconceptualizing temporality from retrospective psychological time to environmental present and event-like occurrence, and ③ reconceptualizing the subject from a single human author to a distributed agency network.** These revisions are not limited to this case alone; they will present a valid theoretical paradigm for contemporary poetics as a whole, which explores the new relationships among technology, nature, and humans in the era of climate crisis.
