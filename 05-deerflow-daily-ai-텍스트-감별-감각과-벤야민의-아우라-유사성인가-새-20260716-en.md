## Question

The 'AI Text Discernment Sense' Observed on the Internet in 2025-2026 — A unique sensibility is forming on platforms like LinkedIn and Medium that distinguishes human-written text from AI-generated text. This sense operates not by catching grammatical or factual errors, but by detecting a vague discomfort—a feeling that 'something is too smooth and inoffensive, it feels strange'—the absence of the text's 'anthropic texture'. Is this structurally similar to Walter Benjamin's discussion of the decay of the 'aura' in *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction* (1936)—the loss of the original's uniqueness and its *hic et nunc* through mechanical reproduction? Just as Benjamin argued that the reproduction of photography and film replaces the 'cult value' of the artwork with 'exhibition value', does the proliferation of AI-generated text extinguish the 'aura of the author' and reduce text to a pure function of information transmission? Or, conversely, is a new phenomenon occurring that Benjamin did not predict—the emergence of a new readerly sensibility, the 'AI text discernment sense', in the very place where the 'aura of the author' has been extinguished? If the latter, is this new sense an extension of Benjamin's concept of 'aura', or the emergence of an entirely different sensory system?

# Structural Comparison of the AI Text Discernment Sense and Benjamin's Aura — An In-Depth Analysis

---

## 1. Introduction: Distinguishing the Layers of the Problem

This analysis is not a simple task of 'finding similarities', but rather a task of testing the **explanatory boundaries** of the concept. In other words, it does not ask 'Is the AI text discernment sense similar to Benjamin's decay of the aura?', but examines **to what extent Benjamin's concept can explain this new phenomenon, where it succeeds, and where it begins to fail**.

---

## 2. Examining Isomorphism: Similarities

### 2.1 Structural Parallel — A Three-Stage Model

A **formal similarity** exists between Benjamin's argument and the AI text phenomenon:

| Dimension | Benjamin (1936) | AI Text Phenomenon (2025-26) |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| **Technological Condition** | Mechanical reproduction technology of photography and film | Probabilistic text generation technology of LLMs |
| **Object of Extinction** | The 'here and now' (*hic et nunc*) of the artwork | The 'anthropic texture' of the text |
| **Substitute** | Exhibition value | Information value |
| **Perceptual Change** | Contemplative absorption → Distracted reception | Author-tracking reading → Discriminative reading |

This structural parallel suggests a **new instance of Benjamin's historico-philosophical thesis—that technological development transforms perceptual modes**. The proliferation of AI text shifts the reader's perceptual mode from 'a reader interpreting the author's intention' to 'a reader discerning AI from human'.

### 2.2 Parallel of 'Cult Value → Exhibition Value': 'Author's Aura → Information Value'

Just as Benjamin saw the cult value (ritual function) of the artwork replaced by exhibition value, the proliferation of AI text brings about the replacement of the **'author-origin value' of text** (who wrote this text, does it reflect their experience and intention?) with **'information-functional value'** (is the information this text conveys accurate and useful?).

This connects to Foucault's concept of the 'author-function'. For Foucault, the author-function was a principle of classification and a mark of ownership for texts. AI text **radically neutralizes** this author-function. The value of a text is no longer certified by 'who wrote it', but evaluated by 'how useful the information it contains is'. This is also a symptom of the **transparent society of information** (Byung-Chul Han).

---

## 3. Conceptual Rupture: Where the Isomorphism Breaks Down

### 3.1 The Difference between 'Reproduction' and 'Generation' — The Most Decisive Rupture

What Benjamin discussed was the **reproduction of an original**. Photography reproduces the image of the original work, film reproduces the actor's performance. An 'original' is presupposed here. However, AI text generation is **generation without an original**. An LLM does not 'reproduce' a specific human's text, but generates **text as a statistical average** based on a probability distribution extracted from hundreds of millions of texts.

This difference is philosophically decisive:

> **Benjamin's problem**: The uniqueness of the original is extinguished by reproduction.
> **The problem of AI text**: The very concept of the 'original' is nullified by probabilistic generation.

In other words, Benjamin's analysis is a **narrative of extinction premised on the existence of an original**, whereas the AI text phenomenon concerns a **sensory system newly emerging under the condition of generation without an original**. These two are not on the same trajectory.

This point connects directly to the conceptual distinction mentioned in the user's Khoj references:

> "From Technical Reproduction to Technical Generation — A Media-Historical Turn in Knowledge Structuring" (Document 4)

This distinction implies a **media-historical rupture**. The era of 'technical reproduction' can be explained by Benjamin's aura discussion, but the era of 'technical generation' demands a different conceptual apparatus.

### 3.2 Extinction of the Aura vs. Emergence of the Discernment Sense — A Reversal of Direction

For Benjamin, the extinction of the aura was a **passive experience**. The masses, surrounded by reproduced images, become distracted receivers. In the place where the aura has vanished, a **sense of loss** remains.

However, the AI text discernment sense is an **active judgment ability**. The reader detects the 'smoothness' of the text, captures the absence of 'anthropic texture', and judges 'this was written by AI'. This is **the activity of judgment, not the passivity of extinction**.

This implies the following:

- **Benjamin's prediction (extinction → passivity)**: As AI text proliferates, the reader becomes more passive, and the sense of the text's 'human origin' will disappear.
- **Actual phenomenon (extinction → active discernment)**: As AI text proliferates, the reader is instead developing a new active judgment ability: 'AI discernment'.

This is a **paradox that Benjamin's framework did not predict**. Extinction does not give rise to passivity, but rather an **active sense as a reaction to extinction** is emerging.

### 3.3 Tension with the 'Death of the Author' (Barthes)

Roland Barthes' 'Death of the Author' (1967) argued that the meaning of a text arises not from the author's intention, but from the reader and the structure of language. This declaration was intended to **dismantle the authority of the author**.

Yet, the AI text discernment sense operates in the **exact opposite direction of Barthes' declaration**. The reader seeks to verify whether there is an 'author' (a human) behind the text. In other words, the 'author' that Barthes killed is being **summoned back by the AI text discernment sense**. A reader has emerged who asks not about the *meaning* of the text, but about the *origin* of the text.

This creates the following theoretical tension:

> Barthes: The author is dead → the text belongs to the reader
> AI Discernment: Is the author dead? → We must verify the origin of the text
> Paradox: In the age of the theory that killed the author, a reader has been born who most persistently seeks the author.

## 4. The Identity of the AI Text Discernment Sense — Three Hypotheses

### 4.1 Hypothesis 1: 'Aura Transfer' — From the Author's Aura to the Human Aura

According to this hypothesis, the AI text discernment sense is the **emergence of a new form of aura in the very place where the Benjaminian aura has been extinguished**. What has been extinguished is the 'aura of the individual author' (this text came from the unique experience of a specific human), and what has newly emerged is the 'aura of the 'human in general'' (this text was written by a human).

In other words, the **content of the aura has expanded from the individual (author) to the species (human)**. This can be seen as an extension of Benjamin's concept—an 'Anthropic Aura'.

**Evidence**: The 'strangeness' the reader detects in AI text is not the absence of a specific author's individuality, but the absence of the **unique 'texture of imperfection' inherent in human text**—errors, contradictions, hesitation, leaps, personal preferences. It is a sense that detects the extinction of the aura of 'humanness'.

**Limitation**: This hypothesis risks diluting the original meaning by extending the concept of 'aura' too far. Benjamin's aura is based on 'here and now', whereas the 'Anthropic aura' is based on the origin of the text (human vs. machine). The former is a matter of **spatial and temporal uniqueness**, the latter is a matter of the **ontological category of the generating subject**.

### 4.2 Hypothesis 2: 'The Politics of Smoothness' — Byung-Chul Han's Framework

This hypothesis explains the AI text discernment sense through **Byung-Chul Han's concept of 'smoothness' (*das Glatte*)**. In *Saving Beauty* (2015), Han states that 'the smooth is today's signature', reading Jeff Koons' sculptures, the iPhone, and Brazilian waxing as part of the same genealogy of smoothness.

In this framework, the 'excessive smoothness' of AI text represents:
- **Absence of negativity**: Human writing leaves traces of pain, conflict, hesitation, and failure. AI text is a text of 'pure positivity' that has removed all this negativity.
- **Extinction of the Other**: For Han, 'smoothness' is the expulsion of the Other (resistance, friction, discomfort). AI text signifies the extinction of the 'text as Other'—a text that discomforts the reader, makes them resist, and compels them to reread.
- **Violence of transparency**: In Han's *The Transparent Society*, transparency is a violence that makes everything visible and calculable. AI text is a 'perfectly transparent' text—a text where all meaning is revealed on the surface, leaving no room for interpretation.

**Strength**: This hypothesis explains the **specific content** of the 'vague strange feeling' detected by the AI text discernment sense. The 'strangeness' is precisely the **absence of negativity**—the feeling that the text is so smooth it offers no resistance.

**Weakness**: Han's analysis remains at the level of cultural critique and does not explain the **technical conditions** specific to AI text (probabilistic generation, token prediction).

### 4.3 Hypothesis 3: 'Failed Naturalization' — Culler's Framework

Jonathan Culler's concept of 'naturalization' explains the process by which a reader reduces an unfamiliar text to a familiar system of meaning. The AI text discernment sense can be explained as a **failure of naturalization**.

The reader attempts to naturalize the AI text as 'natural human writing', but some layer of the text resists this naturalization process. This resistance is the sense that 'something is strange'. This is a **negative sensation created by the failure of naturalization**—a surplus of the text that is not fully absorbed into the system of meaning.

**Strength**: This hypothesis explains why the 'discernment sense' is experienced not as conscious reasoning, but as a **vague discomfort**. Discernment is not an intellectual judgment, but a **sensory surplus** created by the failure of the naturalization process.

**Weakness**: This hypothesis is a general theory applicable to all experiences of 'unfamiliar texts', rather than an explanation specific to the AI text phenomenon.

## 5. Extension or Rupture? — A Comprehensive Judgment

### 5.1 Possibility of Extending Benjamin's Concept: Conditional Affirmation

The AI text discernment sense can be partially explained as an **extension of Benjamin's analytical framework**. In particular:

- The historico-philosophical thesis that **technology transforms perceptual modes** remains valid. AI text is shifting the reader's perceptual mode from 'interpretive reading' to 'discriminative reading'.
- The **replacement by 'exhibition value'** can be paralleled to the replacement by 'information value'.
- The **loss of ritual context** can be extended to the loss of the 'unique context' of human writing (personal experience, emotion, corporeality).

### 5.2 However: A Fundamental Rupture — The Emergence of a Completely Different Sensory System

Yet, the decisive difference lies in the **ontological difference between 'reproduction' and 'generation'**. This difference has the following implications:

1. **Benjamin's Tragic Nature vs. AI Text's Paradoxical Nature**: Benjamin's extinction of the aura is accompanied by a feeling of **tragic loss**. In contrast, the AI text discernment sense is a **paradoxical ability**—a new ability that emerges from the condition of extinction. In Benjamin's world, nothing remains when the aura disappears, but in the world of AI text, a **new sensory system, the discernment sense, emerges** in the very place where the aura has vanished.

2. **Direction of Perception**: Benjamin's perceptual change is a direction **from the work to the audience** (the work no longer emits an aura), whereas the AI text discernment sense is a direction **from the reader to the text** (the reader actively judges the origin of the text).

3. **Addition of a Meta-Cognitive Dimension**: The AI text discernment sense is not simple perception, but includes **meta-cognition that is aware of its own perceptual process**. The feeling that 'this text is strange' is converted into the judgment that 'this text seems to be generated by AI'. This is a dimension absent from Benjamin's framework.

### 5.3 Synthesis: The 'Extinction of the Aura' and the 'Emergence of the Discernment Sense' are Two Sides of the Same Technological Condition

In conclusion, the AI text discernment sense is **neither a simple extension of Benjamin's concept of aura, nor the emergence of a completely new sensory system**. Rather:

> **The AI text discernment sense is a new perceptual system that emerges as a meta-sense for detecting the extinction of the 'author's aura' under the condition of that very extinction.**

In other words, the extinction of the aura and the emergence of the discernment sense are **two effects of the same technological condition (generative AI)**. One is extinction (passive, negative), the other is emergence (active, positive). They are not in opposition, but are **two sides of the same technological event**.

The reason this is absent from Benjamin's framework is that Benjamin's technological condition (mechanical reproduction) presupposed an 'original'. Under the condition of 'generation without an original', extinction is not the only outcome; a new sense that detects this extinction emerges alongside it. This is the point where the media aesthetics of the 'age of technical generation' differs from that of the 'age of mechanical reproduction'.

- Anthropic aura: **Species-uniqueness** (the textual characteristics of the human species)
